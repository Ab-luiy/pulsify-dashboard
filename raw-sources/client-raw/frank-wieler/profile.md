# Creator profile: Frank Wieler ("Franz") — student interview transcript

Real, verbatim source material for Frank's outreach landing page and any follow-up
assets (VSL script, Typeform, email sequence). Frank is the mentor/interviewer in this
transcript, interviewing the youngest student in his mentorship. This is the first
confirmed evidence Frank runs an active paid mentorship with a real student result —
prior CRM data only showed his public YouTube/IG presence, not this.

## 1. Sub-niche

Subscription-based physical product dropshipping with paid Meta ads. Not generic
dropshipping — specifically converting a single winning product into recurring
subscription revenue (MRR) instead of chasing one-time-purchase ROAS.

## 2. Audience & stage

Frank runs a paying mentorship ("Today I'm interviewing the youngest student in my
mentorship that hit his first $1 million in revenue"). The student joined as a paying
student, not organically. No public community/Discord mentioned in this transcript.

## 3. Voice & language

**Signature phrases (verbatim):**
- "it's just all about the ads"
- "products don't die because the problem always stays there"
- "you just got lazy, and you didn't want to refresh their ad creative"
- "my break even is 1.21"
- "test as many products as possible and test them fast too"
- "this is not a supplement"
- "you should nobody should ever subscribe without knowing it's a subscription"
- "as a beginner, let's be real, your ads are not going to be great"

**Verbal tics:** "Right?" (seeking agreement), "You know" (filler), "So" (transition
opener), "Yeah" (confirming opener).

**Cadence:** conversational interview, short Q&A, opens into longer explanation when
justifying a business decision (e.g. ROAS math).

**Worldview (contrarian beliefs, quoted):**
1. Products don't die from saturation, only neglect: "For products to just die out, that
   means you just got lazy, and you didn't want to refresh their ad creative."
2. Break-even ROAS is fine with a subscription behind it: "If it's 1.25, or if it's .9, I
   really don't care. I just keep it running cuz I know next month... I'm going to make
   money off of it."
3. Subscriptions aren't just for supplements: "A lot of people out there think you can
   only do this with supplements, which is definitely not the case."
4. Test more products over more creatives per product: "I'd rather test 20 products with
   just one batch of creatives than five products with four batches."

## 4. Unique mechanism

Not branded with a name in this transcript — functionally MRR (monthly recurring
revenue) dropshipping.

**The promise:** "I made almost a million dollars and I'm 15 years old." / "Across the
two of them, it was at 100k in MRR."

**How it works (as stated):**
1. Find a product people need repeatedly, not a supplement.
2. Convert it into a subscription offer instead of a one-time purchase.
3. Accept a lower break-even ROAS because recurring revenue compounds.
4. Be transparent about the subscription (avoid bans/refunds/scammer perception).
5. Test new products fast rather than over-testing creatives on one product.
6. Refresh ad creative continuously to keep a product alive.

**Core contrarian belief:** "Products don't die because the problem always stays there,
right? For products to just die out, that means you just got lazy, and you didn't want
to refresh their ad creative."

**Why it works:** "Especially with the MRR, it's really never going to die out even if I
stop running the ads."

## 7. Content beats

**Origin story:** door-to-door hustle at age 12 in Texas ("I'll clean up your
tumbleweeds for 50 bucks") before dropshipping.

**The villain:** bad paid mentors ("hired one off of Fiverr... hurt my business more
than he helped me") and subscription scammers ("that is like what makes drop shipping
look so bad").

**The transformation:** a few thousand dollars in 8 months of organic dropshipping →
almost $1 million, $100K MRR.

**Pre-empted objections:** supplements-only myth ("this is not a supplement... a lot of
people think you can only do this with supplements, which is definitely not the case");
subscription ethics ("don't scam people... make sure you send out the product").

## 8. Recurring hook (for cold outreach)

1. Subscription products don't need supplements.
2. Products only die from neglect, not saturation.
3. Break-even ROAS is fine once MRR compounds.
