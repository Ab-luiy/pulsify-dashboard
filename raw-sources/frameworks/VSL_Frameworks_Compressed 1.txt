TRANSCRIPT 1: HOW TO CREATE A KILLER VIDEO SALES LETTER IN 2026 (VSL)

STRUCTURE: THE SUMMARY SECTION

In the first 60–90 seconds, treat it like a tweet version of the entire VSL — cover everything that's covered in the full video but condensed into that opening window. People optimize around energy conservation and efficiency; they want to determine if it's for them as fast as possible. They don't care about watching the whole thing. Most people open VSLs with hooks or dive straight in — instead, use this summary approach, then expand on each point for the rest of the video. Jeremy's Inner Circle VSL opens with the price immediately, then covers everything people want to know in the first 60–90 seconds.

VIDEO CHAPTERING

Add chapter timestamps to your VSL player. People will skip around — that's fine. Chaptering helps retention and consumption because it lets viewers go to what they care about. Most people won't watch cover-to-cover regardless.

WITHHOLDING THE BUTTON (Apply/CTA)

The debate: show the CTA button immediately vs. delay until a time threshold.

Case for withholding: People aren't watching the VSL before applying. A time delay forces consumption. Some test 1–2 minutes, others withhold until 20–30 minutes in.

Downsides of withholding:
- Dramatically reduces scheduling volume short-term
- Inflates cost per qualified call
- Limits conversion data sent back to the pixel — breaks pixel conditioning
- In 2026, exiting learning phase is critical. Fewer conversions = slower exit. Required data to exit learning phase is dynamic (sometimes 5 people, rarely hundreds).

Upside of withholding: When you can endure the short-term cost increase, you accumulate high-intent leads in the pixel who actually watched and applied — dramatically improves intent.

Decision depends on: (1) your bank account — can you afford the short-term CPL inflation? (2) Your sales team — if they need pre-framed leads, withhold. If they're skilled closers who can handle anyone, don't bother. (3) If your team just wants volume regardless of quality, skip withholding.

WITHHOLDING THE PLAY BAR

If you withhold the button, also withhold the play bar — no point in someone scrubbing to the end fast and finding no CTA. Withholding the play bar while showing the button = bad, because forcing people to watch something they want to skip attracts lower quality, lower intent people who get annoyed.

MAXIMIZING PLAY RATE

Play rate = % of page visitors who press play. Preferred platforms: Wistia and Vidalytics. Avoid YouTube and Vimeo for VSL hosting.

Video thumbnails (not static images) dramatically increase play rate. The thumbnail should show a specific moment or key point in the video that people actually care about — not a generic "press play" button image (those always perform badly). If using a static image for page speed reasons, same rule applies — show something that invites viewing.

Keep page minimal: headline + VSL + application/button only. More content on the page = people scroll, self-qualify without watching, and leave. The video being the only information source forces higher play rate.

ENGAGEMENT RATE

Engagement rate = average % of the VSL consumed. Example: 3 min watched on a 10-min VSL = 30% engagement.

Counterintuitive nuance: High engagement can be bad. It can mean people are confused and need to watch more to understand what you do — not that they're hooked. Ideal outcome is people get the point quickly and take action.

Improving engagement: Edited videos with captions, jump cuts, music, sound effects, and occasional memes hold attention better. B-roll and visual variety help. Tonality, messaging congruency from the ad that brought them there, and authority in communication all matter. The VSL messaging must feel congruent with whatever ad/content drove them to the page.

VSL LENGTH

- Rich/affluent: 2–5 minutes. Shorter = better. Summary tip is especially important here so they can decide at any point.
- General public: Can be as long as 60 minutes (functions like a webinar on the page). Beyond 60 minutes = diminishing retention.
- Current average across most accounts: 10–20 minutes.
- Three working styles: (1) talking head, (2) slide deck on left + talking head on right, (3) screen recording with voiceover. All three work equally well.

TRANSCRIPT 2: HOW TO CREATE A $1M/MONTH VIDEO SALES LETTER (VSL)

AFFLUENT DEMOGRAPHICS — VSL STYLE

Length: 2–15 minutes max. Extremely direct, no fluff, no added yapping. Only what they need to know.

Never use value stacks (e.g., "this is worth $10K, this is $3K, all for $1K now") — FTC non-compliant and nobody believes them. Affluent buyers especially tune out from this.

Style: Authentic confidence and authority. People can detect authentic vs. shaky certainty. Optimize around high trust. Presentation style should be simple — white background + black text or vice versa. No logos, minimal design. Overdone presentations come off like the flashy-person-trying-to-look-rich — which affluent demographics distrust. Simpler = more trustworthy. This has been tested repeatedly and simpler always outperforms.

~50% of clients hitting $1M/month use slide decks with screen recording + voiceover (no camera). The other ~50% use talking head. Both work. The key is not putting a bad talking head on camera — if they freeze up, slides are better.

Look the part: Match the character expectations of your target audience. Capital raise clients wear button-down suits, film in neutral offices — not because they have to, but because that's exactly what their buyers expect. A client selling a $40K alternative investment wore a Gucci jumpsuit → attracted unserious investors. Switched to a black tee + track pants → overnight, far more serious qualified people. Same ad, same messaging, same demo — only appearance changed.

GENERAL PUBLIC — VSL STYLE

Length: 5–60 minutes. More skepticism = longer needed. Test shorter first; lengthen if quality issues arise.

Must be highly designed presentations — contrast with affluent where minimal design works. General public treats minimal design as a scam signal. Over-design your slides for general public.

Trust assets are critical: Use highly believable testimonials from their original source (video, social screenshots, emails — not retyped quotes). Communicate empathetically, not too direct/authoritative (can create skepticism with general public).

Claims: For general public, you can and should make the biggest compliant claim possible. For affluent, be conservative and understate. Example: Saying "net a million/month" sounds overzealous to affluent but believable gross is fine. General public responds better to bigger claims.

Scarcity: General public needs real scarcity to move — they're savers, not earners. ~73% of the US working class lives paycheck to paycheck. Top 5% income threshold is ~$80K/year. When selling to general public, you're competing against their existing savings goals or forcing credit card purchases. Acknowledge this empathetically in your VSL and use it as a transition to objection handling.

Value stacks: Never do these for either demographic. Believable discounts (e.g., normally $5K, now $3.5K) are fine.

THE 6-STEP VSL FLOW (works for all VSLs)

Step 1 — HOOK (What you can do for them)
Open with a direct, concise statement of what you'll help them accomplish. Example: "I'm Jeremy Haynes, I'm going to help you master internet marketing." If there's a significant early drop-off, the hook is the problem. For affluent: short and direct. For general public: can be slightly longer and more empathetic.

Step 2 — WHY YOU (Credibility/background)
Immediately follow the hook with why you are the person/company who can deliver. If you skip this, viewers mentally check out — they had a question, you didn't answer it, they stopped hearing everything else. Don't use media mentions (Forbes, Fox News) — no one cares in a real conversation. Use real, meaningful credibility: years of experience, client results, refund rate, proven track record. Plant objection handlers here — e.g., "We've had 2,117 high-ticket clients over 10 years with a 0.57% dispute rate."

Step 3 — BUYING MOTIVES (Big picture / market drivers)
Cover all the reasons people actually buy from you, from majority hook (most common reason) down to minority hooks (specific use cases). Window tint example: majority motive = reduce heat/glare; minority = look cool; extreme minority = commit crimes (don't market that). Be elaborate here — this is typically the longest section. Cascade through each motive with explanation of what it is and why people who have that motive should buy.

Step 4 — THE OFFER
Present what you're selling. If you have multiple offers, present the main one and briefly acknowledge others exist. Always state the price — never hide it. Saying the price:
(a) qualifies buyers — those who can't afford it self-select out
(b) conditions the pixel to find more financially qualified people
(c) sets expectation so closers talk to informed buyers
Never withholding price = short-term call volume boost but long-term unqualified pipeline, higher CPL, and never exiting pixel learning mode.

Step 5 — OBJECTION HANDLING
After the offer, transition with something like: "And by the way, I think this is perfect — let me explain why." Then handle real objections pulled from actual sales calls — not made-up ones. Affluent: fewer, higher-quality objections that are deal-critical. General public: more objections, longer section. If you don't handle their key question in the VSL, they won't book or won't show up. This section can be a major bulk of the presentation.

Step 6 — QUALIFICATION + CTA
State clearly who this is for and who it's not for. The in-market person will further self-identify. The wrong person self-selects out. Finish with a clear, direct CTA: "Fill out the application below, schedule a call, looking forward to it." Optional: add testimonials here (especially for general public) before the final CTA. Ensure testimonials are believable.

ADDITIONAL PERFORMANCE INSIGHTS

- Steep drop-off early = fix the hook. Gradual decline = normal human behavior (people leave when they've decided it is or isn't for them). Don't obsess over 100% retention.
- Play rate of 9% can still drive million-dollar months if the qualifier headline does its job filtering to the right people.
- Good play rate is generally 30%+. Low play rate = disconnect between what got them to the page and what they found there, bad thumbnail, or too much info on the page.
- Blue collar niches (roofers, asphalt contractors, solar) consistently show single-digit play rates — for these demographics, consider replacing the VSL with text sections on the page.
- Preferred hosting platform: Wistia — best analytics, engagement visualization, drop-off graphs, video thumbnail capability, and customization. Vidalytics is second.
- Best engagement retention graph = steady gradual decline. Most VSLs have a significant early drop then flatten out. Both are acceptable. Steep early cliff = fix the opener.

TRANSCRIPT 3: THE BLUEPRINT TO SCALING A VSL CALL FUNNEL (TOP BEST PRACTICES)

CALL FUNNEL STRUCTURE

Standard setup (always start here before adding anything): Headline → VSL/Mini Webinar → Application (with scheduler embedded).

Use Typeform + Calendly integrated. The integrated combo eliminates the application-to-scheduler drop-off (typically ~50% of applicants don't separately schedule). Most people use HubSpot, GoHighLevel, or ScheduleOnce — these create a separate scheduling step, artificially doubling your cost per call. Typeform + Calendly = one seamless step = ~2x calls at the same cost.

Two outcomes after application:
- Qualified → Confirmation page (pixel fires here)
- Unqualified (DQ) → Drop-sell page (pixel does NOT fire here)

Never send unqualified people to the confirmation pixel. The pixel is a prediction machine — it finds more people like whoever converts. Send it turd data, it finds more turds. Only pixel qualified people. Unqualified people go to a drop-sell to liquidate ad spend. You can still have setters call the DQ'd people — many lie on qualification questions (majority of people in polls say they would lie about whether they have the money).

Pixel optimization: Set standard event to "Schedule" and/or "Complete Registration." Only fires on qualified confirmation page.

VSL length for call funnels: 2–15 minutes. More financially qualified the demographic = shorter/more direct.

ADVERTISING BEST PRACTICES FOR CALL FUNNELS

- Broad targeting works — but needs a seasoned pixel. Without data, you're relying entirely on messaging to do the filtering.
- Messaging controls who the algorithm targets — spoken words in ads, written copy on funnels, even VSL transcripts are all analyzed by the AI.
- Interest stack targeting: Layer in behavior and interest traits your demographic has. Combine with strong messaging.
- Environment and props matter (Tai Lopez lesson, 2017): The algorithm analyzes everything in the video — what you say, what's in the background, physical objects. When you film in environments your demographic has interests in, CPMs drop and reach improves. Play the character: if you're selling to people who expect success/wealth, film in an environment that reflects that. Agency owner pitching lawyers → wore a suit → response rate and sales volume spiked. Pitching doctors → bought a doctor's coat or rented a dental office for 15 min.
- The pixel also scans your funnel pages — it reads your VSL transcript to identify who's most likely to convert on your offer.

HAMMER THEM STRATEGY (overview)

Contextual content injected at key stages of the funnel process:
1. Pre-ad: Cost-effective content to build warm retargeting audience (fraction of a penny per view). CPMs drop when there's prior page interaction.
2. Between ad and funnel: Content for people who saw the ad but didn't go to the funnel yet — sell them harder on why they need to go.
3. Post-booking (between booking and call): The four content quadrants — common questions from sales calls, common objections, expectations for how to succeed with your offer, and follow-up questions. This is the highest-leverage window.

"Tornado" = doing all stages simultaneously. "Hammer them" = doing the specific stage most impactful for your current problem.

Benefits: Increases show rate, close rate, average order value, reduces payment plans and financing needs, condenses buying cycle, turns salespeople into cashiers.

SALES TEAM BEST PRACTICES

1. Pre-call outreach: Salesperson texts lead from an iPhone (not green text, not automated — looks like a bot; people ignore green texts). Send a selfie video: introduce yourself, mention the upcoming call time, reference something from their application, offer to send a relevant video. Show rates spike. If they stop doing it, show rates drop within weeks. Sales reps need repetition of the same lessons — don't add new training on top; keep repeating the core.

2. Hire ahead of demand: Most people fill the calendar first, then hire — this means 3–4 week delays every scaling cycle, limiting you to ~6 scale events per year. Instead, hire when you're halfway to capacity. Goal = always have more calendar availability than booked calls. 12 scale events per year = double the revenue of 6. Condensing the timeline also means you run into scaling obstacles sooner and solve them faster.

3. Consistent sales training: Non-negotiable. Business owners who stop training (due to travel, etc.) make less money — consistently. Repeat the same 2–3 week core training cycle.

4. Proper sales management: Covers consistent training, consistent hiring, and analytics reporting.

5. Dispositions over vague feedback: "Leads are bad" is useless. Useful dispositions: financially unqualified, uncertain about offer, objection about spouse, blamed macro event, too many questions, etc. These feed directly into content strategy, funnel changes, and marketing adjustments.

Key sales analytics to track: Show rate, close rate, rebooking rate, cancellation rate.

TRANSCRIPT 4: THE ONLY GUIDE YOU NEED TO BUILD WILDLY PROFITABLE VSL CALL FUNNELS

FUNNEL & TECH SETUP

Same core setup as above: Headline + VSL + Typeform/Calendly application. Qualified → confirmation page (pixel fires). Unqualified → DQ/drop-sell (no pixel). Some organizations have setters call unqualified people too.

Advanced pixel setup: When salespeople flag that people marked qualified are actually unqualified, switch from automatic pixel firing to Conversion API linked to the CRM. Sales rep manually moves deal stage to "qualified" in the CRM → CRM pushes data back to ads manager via CAPI with a slight delay. This ensures only genuinely qualified people get sent to the pixel. Don't let the results column stay empty too long — the AI will rotate to a new audience. It takes only a couple of conversions now (not 50–100 as older guidance suggested) to signal the algorithm who to target.

AD MESSAGING

The biggest quality problem is bad messaging in direct response ads — income claims, guarantees, and audacious numbers attract unqualified buyers (high-skeptic, low-trust, financially unqualified).

Income claims: Never. Sophisticated buyers see through them and create objections. Jeremy's channel consistently avoids income claims → attracts buyers already doing $200K–$1M/month.

Guarantees: Signal to skeptics that there's safety → attract the most skeptical, least financially serious buyers. Rich people have been burned by guarantees and treat them as a red flag.

"I've done $100M in sales": Nobody believes it. Creates skepticism, not credibility.

Correct messaging approach: Describe the conditions of your ideal customer, not bragging numbers. Example: "If you're stuck at a couple thousand dollars/day in ad spend and every time you try to scale, costs spike and ROAS drops — pay attention." This calls out someone doing ~$200K/month and wanting to hit $1M, without making an income claim.

Direct response ad framework: Hook → Body → CTA. Messaging throughout must be congruent with what your ideal buyer cares about.

CONFIRMATION PAGE BEST PRACTICES (Due Diligence Framework)

The confirmation page is a research hub — its job is to pre-answer questions so people show up further along in the sales process and with higher intent to buy.

Structure:
- Confirmation headline ("Your call is booked")
- Main confirmation video (short — welcome, explain what they'll see on this page, tell them the goal is for them to be "in or out" before the call)
- Breakout videos (4–12 videos, 30 seconds–5 minutes each)
- Testimonials (below breakout videos — believable, original source)

Breakout videos: Pull from (1) recurring questions/objections from sales calls, (2) things you wish people knew before the call that they'd get from the VSL if they watched it fully, (3) objections, expectations, and the 4 quadrants of hammer-them content.

Why confirmation pages matter: After booking a call, people either (a) want to do research and will do it on their own — help them or they may conclude your offer isn't for them, or (b) don't do research and deprioritize the call when it comes. The Dubai tax company example: booked a call 9 days out, sent zero useful information, Jeremy did his own research, found the claim was false, didn't show up. Companies that help people do their due diligence get dramatically better show rates and call quality.

EMAIL SEQUENCE (Pre-Call)

Aggressive research-oriented sequence. Can be as many as 6 emails/day for a 72-hour window (18 total emails). When people actually want the information, open rates hit 60–70%+ per email.

Include: Breakout video links, research SOPs, FAQ documents, answers to common questions, objection handling content. Mirror what's on the confirmation page. Repurpose breakout videos here.

Best practice: Intentionally space the call 2–3 days out from booking (not same day) to allow enough time for this research sequence to run. Salespeople who complain about lead quality without giving detailed dispositions should be ignored — get real feedback.

HAMMER THEM STRATEGY (detailed)

Targets: 30–50 pieces of short-form content, 20–30 pieces of long-form content. Retarget people 15–20+ times within the key pre-call window. Half-assing this (e.g., only short-form, or too few pieces) does not produce results — you need the full volume.

The most common failure: Executing halfass with content that doesn't actually answer what your buyers want to know — making random videos about things you think they need rather than what they're genuinely asking. Get real question/objection data from sales calls. Content should match the quadrant (questions, objections, expectations, follow-up questions).

Without guidance on which content to make, most people "execute in the dark" — they make the wrong stuff and see no lift.

Done correctly: Near impossible not to hit 60–70%+ show rates.

TRUST ASSETS & SALES ASSETS

Trust assets = content built to answer the specific high-level questions serious buyers have. Capital raise example: accredited investors asked about deal history, average returns, reinvestment rates, failed deals, team bios, site walkthroughs. The company turned every question into a trust asset video — Looms, site walkthrough recordings, municipality meetings, GC walkthroughs. These get repurposed on confirmation pages, in emails, in hammer-them content, and used by salespeople to send manually pre-call.

Trust assets salespeople can send manually via selfie video + content link have the biggest impact on show rate.

SALESPEOPLE (expanded from Transcript 3)

Pre-call text from iPhone + selfie video = highest impact on show rate. Not automated. Not green text. Not blue-text software using fake numbers. A real person from an iPhone. Reference their application, offer to send a relevant video, keep it warm and helpful.

Sales training: Repeat the same 2–3 week core training indefinitely. Don't keep adding new content — the core things get abandoned when new training layers pile on.

Post-call tracking: Show rate, close rate, rebooking rate, cancellation rate. Dispositions for every outcome.
