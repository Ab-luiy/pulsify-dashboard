## 1. YouTube Flywheel VSL

- One long-form YouTube video, 1 to 6+ hours, that is a complete A-to-Z course on the business model. Claimed to replace the landing page, the VSL, the setter and often the closer.
- Flow: YouTube views -> flywheel -> Typeform -> qualified -> booked -> showed -> closed -> cash.
- Claims: prospects arrive already sold, calls become question-clearing; one flywheel yields many spin-off videos (his outreach section alone could be a 1-2 hour video); it compounds, a 6-month-old flywheel can stay the top lead source.
- Algorithm theory (his, unverified): YouTube optimizes for session time (time on platform after clicking you), which is not visible in analytics. Linking off-platform (Instagram etc.) gets a video throttled; linking to another long YouTube video extends session time. So every other video's description links to the flywheel, never to Instagram. A Typeform click is an acceptable cost; another social network is not. Over time YouTube learns who watches the flywheel and recommends it to cold matching viewers. Niche, technical, low-view videos work because only qualified people click them.
- His content layers: TOF = lifestyle/vlogs (most views, lowest lead quality); MOF = the flywheel (high, compounding views, high quality); BOF = in-depth breakdowns for people who already understand the model (fewest views, very high quality); Proof = testimonials and case studies. Cadence: roughly 1 lifestyle video per 3 to 5 videos.
- Build: written outline -> Miro board -> film -> post -> link it from every video.
- Outline: two parts. Part 1, hook and proof (first few minutes): claim it is the most complete video on the topic, scroll the whole Miro board to show scale, real proof numbers, "flex lifestyle briefly", say who it is for, show a left-to-right map of what is covered. Part 2, the business model section by section in the order a beginner would do it, mirroring the board. Testimonials, case studies and client interviews go inside the section they support, never bunched at the end. His example: a mentee interview placed inside the outreach section because that mentee signed both clients with that exact outreach method.
- Outline format: columns side by side, one per major section; main bullets then sub-bullets; no paragraphs; written so a designer with zero context could build the board from it. His trimmed example columns: opening hook (biggest video on the topic, show the board, profit numbers, who it is for), who I am / story, choosing the niche (skill vs bizop, dollar-per-follower, 1-2 niches max), landing the creator (platform ranking, profile first, 38-character opening DM "tripwire", 30-35 DMs a day, Loom uploaded unlisted to YouTube), creating the offer (high ticket $2-8k upfront 1:1, low ticket $20-100/month, concrete guarantees, downsell/upsell ecosystem), launching and selling (Typeform disqualification, budget stated directly, processors and financing, a case study with full funnel numbers), scaling and retention (closer only at 4-5 qualified calls a day, setter ~5% and closer 8-12%, posting cadence per platform, treat the creator as a partner), proof and case studies placed in context, community and next steps (1:1 only, Typeform is the only CTA).
- Miro step: one board section per outline column in the same order; supporting visuals for every bullet (simple diagrams, real screenshots); mostly images; short notes only, so filming needs few cuts.
- Filming: 1 hour absolute floor, 1-2+ hours target, "the longer the better"; up to about half can be previously recorded material (old videos, interviews, full coaching calls), at least half must be new. Hook only a few minutes. Plug the program lightly throughout, one hard CTA at the end.
- Posting: title and thumbnail lean on a specific number and on the length ("people click a 6 hour video because it's 6 hours"); lowercase casual title to match raw filming; thumbnail should make people think "who is this and how does he know this much"; A/B test titles and thumbnails. Description: line 1 the Typeform or landing page, then full timestamps; no Instagram or outside links anywhere.
- Claim: upload from the US, or have a US team member upload it (YouTube channel permissions, no login sharing), because upload location affects which countries it is shown to.
- After launch: add it to the description and end screen of every past and future video. Remake only when the model changes or the proof is stale (about a year). Track the flywheel's own views and lead attribution separately from channel totals.

## 2. 1-Prompt: Infinite Content Ideation

- Find 2-3 competitors (adjacent niches count) by searching the way the ICP searches.
- Pull only their top 5-10 pieces by views relative to their own average, not everything. Feed them into PoppyAI (pulls transcripts from any link).
- Also feed a folder with everything about your own offer (ICP docs, offer breakdown, past content, testimonials).
- Prompt structure: study hooks, angles, formats and structure of the winners; generate ideas in the same patterns adapted to my offer and ICP; for each give hook, format and core point.
- Test several styles over 1-2 weeks; keep the one that is easiest to produce consistently and brings cash, not views. Re-feed the pieces that drove revenue.
- Track cash collected per piece with UTM links (plug for InfoOS).

## 3. Instagram Story Sequence: "The Decoded Result"

- One format he says converts best: take one specific result and break down every component that produced it, slide by slide. The credibility comes from showing the moving parts, not the result.
- Steps: pick one specific, ideally complex result with screenshot-able parts ("$6,000 PIF off one YouTube video", not "closed a client"); list every component in order before writing; slide 1 states the result plainly, then tease "here's exactly what went into it"; one slide per component in chronological order, analytical not excited, each slide = component + why it worked; if it gets confusing, add one simplifying line early (he added an AI-context analogy on slide 3).
- Formatting: one image per slide (lifestyle shot or screenshot), Instagram native text, the only edit is a drop shadow behind text (done in Photoshop first); no gradients, filters or custom charts ("overplayed").
- Close: low-pressure CTA, e.g. reply a keyword to get the breakdown.
- His example is the $28k PIF sequence from the InfoOS video.

## 4. Detailed ICP Identifier Prompt

- Core principle: help people who are already winning, not beginners ("helping rich people get richer is easier"): fewer headaches, higher close rates, more PIFs, premium pricing. His own shift: from beginner operators to operators and offer owners already making money. Niche examples given for fitness, ecom, real estate, content, coaching.
- Workflow: client fills an ICP questionnaire (niche, problem and cause, current vs desired, deep drives, promise, timeframe, process, proof, pricing now and target, how they sell, top objections, voice and tone, revenue and goal); operator pulls social media (10-15 recent posts, 3-5 top posts, result posts, comment sections, bios, shared testimonials) into a folder; one Claude conversation runs an ICP prompt, then an "offer architect" prompt (9 sections: buyer, transformation, timeframe, named system, evidence, pricing, funnel path from opt-in to post-call automations, false beliefs and headline angles, compliance), then five follow-up docs (USP, voice reference, power offer, direct offer copy, offer clarity with FAQ and objection map). No social presence: use 3-5 voice memos instead.
- The collapsed questionnaire is a template (problems, deep problems, desires, USP, "the bridge" of 15-30 steps, transformation, life story, features and benefits). The avatar prompt is the "Steve" style avatar interview. Generic.

## 5. Create High-Converting Miro Boards

- Think first, build fast. Gather raw material you have already said somewhere (calls, DMs, voice memos, podcasts, repeated audience questions, past content).
- Outline in pillars -> bullets -> sub-bullets; typical shape: hook, core sections, proof, close. Every bullet must pass "could someone with zero context picture it". Mark skipped pillars as skipped on purpose.
- Open with proof. Results-based proof pulls a higher quality viewer than lifestyle proof (his words: lifestyle "drags in a lower quality, less serious crowd"). Example: a board opening with how a $246k profit month was generated.
- Subconscious selling: give something real, leave one natural gap. Example: show DM scripts that worked but are slightly dated, say the current ones are inside the 1:1.
- Testimonials right after the method that produced them; stacks of screenshots work too.
- Collect every image into one folder before building. Build one box per pillar, branch a box per bullet, image first with a one-line note. Consistent style. Mostly images: "if the text is longer than the visual, it's wrong." Real screenshots, not stock.
- One hard CTA at the end only: reinforce proof, state confidence, name who it is and isn't for, one action, what happens next, one line of differentiation; soft CTAs only after.
- Final review against the outline. Outsourcing: Upwork or Fiverr Miro/presentation designers, test with one outline and a reference board; or his in-house team, under 24 hours.

## 6. Instantly 2x Existing Client Results (testimonials)

- Two levers: who you work with (already-winning clients produce bigger, faster wins; he notes many "$100k/month" testimonials were already at $80k) and when you ask.
- Timing: the window is the moment of the win; emotion fades within days.
- Spot the moment in DMs, Slack, group chats and on calls ("this actually worked"); check in around known milestones.
- Ask by referencing something specific from where they started and letting them react to the contrast; then get them on a call (run a normal coaching call, ask for a 5-10 minute interview segment at the end), same day if possible.
- Fallback: a DM screenshot, framed with an objective record of the "before" (a call recorder summary beats your own claim), get the client to state the timeframe themselves, keep the tone light; the testimonial line usually comes unprompted.
- Systemize: milestone check-ins, a running overdue list, a new testimonial for every new milestone.

## 7. 1-Prompt: Write Human-Like Copy

- Three kinds of context: offer details (ICP, what's included, price, awareness level), 2-3 real examples of the exact format (the most important part: "AI mimics patterns"), and the copy fundamentals stated in the prompt (hook, pain in their words, proof, one CTA).
- Prompt structure: act as a direct response copywriter; audience; offer; awareness level; pasted examples; study tone, rhythm and structure; write the piece in the same style; write how a real person would say it.
- Read it out loud; if it sounds templated, the examples were weak, so feed better examples instead of line-editing. He uses PoppyAI.

## 8. 90%+ Dispute Win Formula

- Claimed 90-100% dispute win rate on client offers. Two parts: every client signs a service agreement after paying, and every call is recorded.
- Clause ideas: payments non-refundable once signed and paid, client waives the right to initiate a chargeback; for bizop offers, a no-guarantee-of-income clause.
- His own caveats: he is not a lawyer (uses Claude for contracts); chargeback rights generally cannot be fully waived; the clause's value is written evidence that terms were knowingly accepted. Refund justified requests anyway.
- Workflow: master template, deal terms, client-specific edits, e-sign ready on the closing call, signed live. Record every call for the life of the engagement (Fathom); in a dispute submit recordings, signed contract, written correspondence and deliverables log.

## 9. UTM Tracking Links

- utm_content is the tag that matters: source tells you YouTube works, content tells you which video works.
- Free version: a Google Sheet with columns base URL, source, medium, campaign, content, term, final link, and a formula that builds the link (the formula code blocks did not render in the capture). Lock naming in a Key tab; lowercase.
- His YouTube defaults: source youtube; medium description, or pinned-comment when the link is there ("they perform differently"); campaign = the offer; content = a recognizable video name (e.g. ep-042-cold-email).
- Never put UTMs on links between your own pages (it overwrites the real source). Labels only letters, numbers and hyphens.
- Catching them: Typeform needs four hidden fields named exactly utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content; Calendly reads UTMs off its own URL automatically. The chain page -> form -> calendar drops the tags silently unless each hop carries them forward (button/embed params, Typeform redirect-on-completion with recalled hidden fields, UTMs on the Calendly embed URL).
- Test end to end with a unique test tag through opt-in and booking; a symptom table says where it broke. GA4 as a free backup (traffic only, not people or cash).
- Limits of the sheet: manual, naming drift, long ugly links, no click -> lead -> call -> cash. InfoOS version: Create Link form, short branded link on your own domain, per-link row of clicks, visits, applications, booked, cash (his example: one video 400 clicks, 312 visits, 41 applications, 12 booked, $18,400; the next 900 clicks and $0).

## 11. 24-Hour Client Onboarding Protocol (operators)

- Speed over perfection: the client only sees the front metrics, so create visible momentum immediately (story sequence live same day, one better piece of content in 24 hours, fix one obvious broken thing same day and say what was fixed, deliver one tangible asset fast).
- Onboarding call: record it; sign the contract live early; duplicate the client Notion template beforehand; get every visual asset and testimonial into one shared Drive during the call; audit the tech stack and get access with each tool explained; upgrade software and set up a payment processor; one dedicated login or Chrome profile per client (or one shared email for accounts you create); learn the ICP and current offer; decide who closes (keep, add a closer, or group chat with existing setter and closer); get the warm list but don't contact it until the offer is ready; set 30-day and 90-day goals and their definition of success; end with the schedule and something concrete (first story sequence same day); log everything into the dashboard before the call ends.

## 13. 1-Prompt SOP Builder

- SOP everything once scaling: it makes the offer look premium and answers most repeat questions.
- Build SOPs only from real questions on real calls. Trigger: any question answered on a call that no SOP covers becomes an SOP the same day; plus a weekly pass across all calls.
- Flow: record (Fathom) -> transcript -> Claude or Poppy with one example SOP for format -> extract every question, pull the actual answer, write one standalone SOP per topic, fill only obvious gaps without inventing. 5-10 minutes each. The library can double as marketing or a lead magnet.
