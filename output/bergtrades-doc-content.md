## Your VSL, rewritten

You've got more than one of these running and they're doing different jobs. The free training one shows what you do. The other one is you telling people the mentorship is good. The second one is the one to replace, and this is the structure Jeremy Haynes uses on every high ticket VSL. Six steps, in order, nothing else in between.

**1. Hook. Say what you'll do for them, in one line.**

> "I'm Berg. I'm going to teach you a mechanical day trading model you can run in about 45 minutes a day, and you'll be able to check my own results against yours the entire time."

No build up, no backdrop, no "what's going on guys." If people drop in the first fifteen seconds this line is the reason.

**2. Why you. Immediately.**

This is where your story goes, and it goes here rather than at the start because they need to know why they should listen before they'll sit through it.

Beat one, where you were. You were running 10 pip stops and 30 pip targets. Some days that was a swing trade, some days you got wicked in and out inside a minute. You had no idea which one you were about to get.

Beat two, what changed. You started measuring the range with the fib tool and basing the entry, the stop and the target on that range instead of a fixed pip count. Your words: the most important change you ever made.

Beat three, what it bought you. Three years profitable. 40 years of backtested data. 60 accounts, three model variations, about 45 minutes a day on the charts. Nine winning months a year and three losing ones, and you say the three out loud.

Beat four, the life. Thailand, the apartment, the team, traders flying out to Koh Samui to trade next to you. [Add whatever personal detail you actually want in here. Don't let me put words in your mouth on it.]

Then plant the objection handler while you're here: "I lost 27R between April and May this year. It's on my channel. That's the worst run I've had on record and I published it, because if I hide that from you then nothing else I show you is worth anything."

**3. Buying motives. The longest section.**

Cascade through why people actually come to you, most common first:

They've been at this a year or two, they know the content, they still aren't profitable. Your own video title. Address it first because it's most of them.

They're stuck on the prop firm treadmill. Pass, fail, pass, fail. You've got a real answer, split the capital, risk high enough on evals to actually clear them.

They can't hold a system for more than a fortnight, so it's psychology, not strategy. Mechanical removes the decision.

They're doing it alone. Lone wolf syndrome. This one's yours, use the name.

**4. The offer. State the price.**

What it is, how long, what they get, what it costs. Say the number out loud.

> "It's [PRICE - fill this in]. Here's what that gets you: [weeks, calls, group access, what's included]."

Hiding the price gets you more calls and worse ones. Saying it means everyone who books already decided they could pay.

**5. Objection handling. Real ones only.**

You already answer these on camera, they just aren't in the VSL:

"I'd have to be at a laptop all day." You spend 45 minutes. Limit orders, phone alerts, four levels.

"How long before I'm profitable." You teach the model in under four weeks and you're straight that the skill takes one to two years. Say both. The people who leave over that were going to quit in week three anyway.

"I've tried loads of strategies." That's the point of showing the data rather than the claim.

Pull the rest from your actual sales calls. The ones you hear most are the ones that go in.

**6. Qualification and CTA.**

You already do this better than almost anyone selling in this niche. From your own video: if you're not serious and you won't put the hours in, please don't apply, I don't want you involved. Keep it word for word.

Then one action. Application form, then the call. Not two links, not a Discord detour.
