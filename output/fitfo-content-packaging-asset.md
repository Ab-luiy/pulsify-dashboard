Your titles are good, and that is the part most people cannot fix.

Almost everyone in this niche writes for beginners who want to get rich, and that audience does not buy anything and does not stay. You write for someone already in the seat and already down. ## What is costing you

**Nothing reads at phone size.** Ninety-plus percent of your impressions are a thumbnail about the width of a thumb. Three of yours are a bedroom, and the two screen recordings carry a browser window, a sidebar, a taskbar and a weather widget all competing with the thing you actually want seen.

**The pair is half missing.** A title and a thumbnail are one unit. The thumbnail says the short emotional thing, the title says the full context, and together the viewer gets the whole message. Right now the title is doing all of it alone and the thumbnail is doing nothing.

**Three of your five videos are the same video.** Surviving losing weeks, dealing with losing weeks, and surviving bad price action are one idea in three hats. Two of them make the same promise, so they compete with each other and the weaker one drags the channel average down.

**Two titles have a specific fixable problem.** The plus sign in the risk-management title welds two promises together, so the viewer commits to neither. And "You make trading harder than it has to be" is true, but it names nothing concrete the viewer walks away with, so there is no reason to click today rather than never.

## How to structure the videos

Not scripts. Bullets you read and then talk off, because the second this is written as sentences you will perform it, and a trader performing lines is the fastest way to lose a trader watching.

### The first thirty seconds

Confirm, do not set up. The opening confirms the exact promise the title made. "What's up guys, welcome back" is the version that loses people.

Show the thing. If the thumbnail says `still funded`, the account is on screen inside thirty seconds. If it says `chop proof`, the chart is up. A hook that talks about a number without showing it breaks the promise the thumbnail made.

Three jobs, nothing else: confirm the promise, signal briefly why you are the one saying it, open a loop that only closes later.

Keep it short. Your audience is money-motivated and time-poor. The long empathetic intro is for a different niche.

### The body

Every video runs the same shape regardless of length: the problem, the struggle, the discovery, the result.

Five to nine bullets. Each one a thing you know cold. Read the bullet, look up, talk.

Open a loop early and close it before the end. An unresolved loop costs you the next video, not just this one.

Reference your mechanism in every video, indirectly. Your mechanism is whatever repeatable thing keeps five accounts alive at once. Name it once, then let it show up inside stories rather than pitching it. When someone eventually asks what you do, they should already know.

Where you can, film while doing the work rather than sitting down to record it afterwards. Same information, noticeably higher authority, and it kills the ideation problem that has you making the same video three times.

### The close

Three calls to action in every video, doing different jobs:

1. Buried in the story. "the rule I run across all five accounts", told as narrative, never as an ask. This one does the trust work.
2. Mid-video, one line, framed as optional for whoever wants the next step.
3. Last sixty to ninety seconds, explicit, tells the viewer exactly what to do.

Never end on "that's everything for this video." Give two doors: one for the viewer who is ready now, one for the viewer who needs another video first, and hand that second viewer a specific next title rather than a shrug.

## The five skeletons

**`how i lost $20k in one week`**
- Open on the drawdown on screen, then the loop: every decision that got you there, and the rule you wrote on the Monday after
- The week before it went wrong: size, cadence, what was working
- The first red day and what you told yourself that morning
- The decision that turned one red day into a red week
- What it cost, shown on the platform
- The rule you wrote afterwards
- The following week under that rule
- What you would tell someone sitting in week two of a drawdown right now

**`5 funded accounts, $2k payouts each`**
- Open on the cadence, accounts on screen, loop on the one rule that does most of the work
- Why five accounts instead of one large one
- What is identical across all five
- The daily risk cap per account
- What happens the day one goes red
- The payout cadence and what you do with the money
- The mistake that blows accounts at exactly this stage

**`the risk plan that gets me 4r on nq`**
- Open with the chart up, box drawn, ratio named in the first sentence
- The conditions that have to be true before you even look
- Where the entry goes
- Where the stop goes, and the reasoning most people get wrong
- How the target gets set
- What you do when it moves against you immediately
- The version of this setup that does not work, and the filter that catches it

**`stop trading after a red week, do this instead`**
- Open on the Monday after a red week being what actually kills funded accounts, and the routine you run instead of trading it
- What that Monday feels like, honestly
- What you used to do
- The routine now, hour by hour
- When you are allowed back on full size
- How you tell the difference between ready and forcing

**`you don't need more setups, you need one`**
- Open on trading one setup, everything you deleted, what happened to the numbers after
- What your chart looked like before
- Everything you removed, named specifically
- The one setup that survived and why that one
- What you gave up by simplifying, stated honestly
- How long before the numbers moved
- How someone else picks their one
