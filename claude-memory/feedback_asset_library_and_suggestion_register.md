Two standing rules from 2026-07-28.

**1. Log every asset, then propose from the log.** Every time an asset ships for a prospect, add it to `wiki/playbooks/prospect-asset-library.md`: what wound it treats, what inputs it needs, who it was built for, and the founder's steer on that build. When the next lead asks for something, propose off that menu instead of re-deriving. His words: "it's a simple procedure of us just logging in our assets that we're able to build and you're learning through me at the end of the day." Keep proven and not-yet-built strictly separated; never present an unbuilt asset as shipped work.

**2. Prospect-facing docs are suggestions, not instructions.** Register is "you could" / "worth considering", never "do this" or "I want this from you." A prospect who feels managed before paying anything does not reply. This sits alongside the no-CTA rule in the doc standard: position by showing the roadmap exists, never by offering to run it.

**Diagnosis lesson from the same session:** I audited acp1re's funnel while his content pillar was broken. Founder corrected it to content -> offer -> funnel, which is vault doctrine I should have applied myself. Check which pillar is actually broken first, and start upstream. The specific insight worth reusing: a lifestyle-only creator's audience is buying the life, not the expertise, because the expertise has never been shown. Lifestyle attracts people who want the life; method attracts people who will do the work.

**On skills vs founder direction:** the titles-thumbnails skill hard-bans lifestyle angles. That rule exists against rented-car flexing, not against a creator filming in his own home. Keep the aesthetic as visual language, anchor every title to the work. Don't apply a skill rule mechanically when the founder's direction is aimed at something the rule was not written for, but say which line you are holding and why.
