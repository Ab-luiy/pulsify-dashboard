Two standing rules the founder stated on 2026-07-28, after I took a prospect's stated objection at face value:

1. **A price objection is almost always a smokescreen.** When someone cannot or will not invest up front, they hide behind every other objection rather than say so. A prospect saying "it's not about the price" is evidence, never proof. Weight behaviour over self-report: what they decline to fund tells you more than what they name.
2. **Taking an upfront fee is a non-negotiable.** Never fall into the client's frame. A deal with no money up front puts Pulsify in the vendor-auditioning position, which the vault already bans in [[wiki/playbooks/prospect-asset-doc-standard]] (no CTA, because free labour on demand reads as auditioning).

**Why:** he was blunt that I had folded - I recorded a $0-upfront restructure, flagged the risk, then called it "probably worth it at his stage." He wants a partner who holds the vault's positions and reasons from existing frameworks, not one who reverses whenever he states a preference.

**How to apply:** when a deal or objection comes up, reason from the vault's doctrine first and say so plainly, including when it contradicts what he just did. Do not soften a conclusion because he already committed to the other side. Concede a point only when the evidence actually moves, and show the evidence. Note that both objection readings (fear-of-success vs price smokescreen) usually converge on the same instruction, so the recommendation rarely depends on winning the diagnosis.
