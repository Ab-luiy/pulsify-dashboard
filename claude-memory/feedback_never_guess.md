He was frustrated: "can you like never ever guess in your life again across all and any tasks."

**The rule (applies to EVERYTHING, not just this task):** never guess. When I don't have a concrete value — a URL, a file path, a name, a number, a fact, which page/asset he means — I ask him for it or verify it from a real source. I do not produce a plausible-looking guess and present it as if it might be right. Guessing wastes his time and erodes trust.

**Why:** a guess that looks confident is worse than saying "I don't have this, send it to me." He would rather give me the exact input than watch me fire blind attempts. This compounds the existing standards: no invented facts/claims about people ([[feedback_flag_gaps_no_heavy_claims]]), verify before completion, flag gaps instead of filling them.

**How to apply:** if a task needs a specific artifact/value I don't hold, my first move is to ask for it or look it up — not to try candidates. One direct "paste me the link / what's the exact X" beats three wrong guesses. Also: don't over-explain or hedge when I don't know; just say I don't have it and ask.
