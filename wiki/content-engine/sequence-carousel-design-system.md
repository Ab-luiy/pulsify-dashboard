The distinctive part of a story sequence or carousel is the design as much as the copy. This page locks the visual grammar, read directly from the captured Niksetting frames (18 sequence slides, 3 full carousels), so the generators can output design direction per slide, not just words. The rule that governs the whole thing: keep every design move, swap the currency. His frames sell with lifestyle; ours sell with student proof. The design language is currency-neutral and ports 1 to 1.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Two creators can post the same words and get different results because one of them designed the frame and the other typed into a template. The annotation layer, the pill boxes, the ghost numbers, and the chip code are what make the content read as authored and premium. They are also cheap: none of them require a nice apartment or a supercar, which is exactly why they are the part we copy wholesale. If our generators hand a creator copy without design direction, we have shipped half the asset.

## The annotation layer (the distinctive, most-copied feature)

A marker or scribble overlay sits on top of the text, deliberately imperfect so it reads as hand-drawn rather than typeset. This is the red-underline look that spread across the niche. The colour logic is consistent and it is the whole trick:

- **Red underline** under the pain or the wrong path ("No strategy").
- **Green underline** plus a green check under the winning path ("The Profile Funnel").
- **Red X** on the wrong option, green check on the right one.
- **Red scribbled box** around the key receipt or number ("I spent $17,932 on ads").
- **A single word flipped to red** inside an otherwise black or white headline for emphasis ("ONE", "NICHE").
- **Blue hand-drawn curved arrow** pointing from a before state to the proof (a follower count, a result).
- On carousels, **hand-drawn underlines under the key nouns** in a headline ("coaches, consultants, info").

Rule: red is pain, wrong, or emphasis. Green is the right move or the win. Blue is a directional pointer. The stroke looks like a digital marker brush (imperfect ends, slight wobble), never a clean vector line. One to three annotations per slide, never a scribble on every word.

## Type treatment

- **Sequences.** A heavy bold grotesque sans (Helvetica or Montserrat weight class). Headlines sit in white rounded pill-boxes with black text. Body copy and metric waterfalls are white text with a drop shadow straight on the photo. Lowercase "i" throughout as a casual signature. Text alignment flips left or right per slide to sit in the photo's negative space.
- **Carousels.** Covers pair a high-contrast display serif italic with a giant bold sans word or number, woven together ("How I made $964.853", "grow acquisition" with "acquisition" in italic serif). Interior slides use a heavy sans headline plus a lighter sans description, with a giant light-gray ghost number behind.
- Exact typeface names are not confirmable from screenshots. The treatment and the weight class are. Lock Pulsify equivalents in one template and hold them across every asset rather than chasing the exact fonts.

## Image classes (what the pictures actually are)

Every slide has a background image doing a job. There are only a few classes:

- **Proof screenshot.** Profile before and after, a revenue dashboard, an ad-spend chart, a live notifications feed, a wall of client wins. Real UI used as the receipt, usually dimmed and sitting behind the text. This is the class that does the selling.
- **Lifestyle or aspiration frame** (his currency, which we swap). One cinematic luxury shot per lesson: penthouse, supercar, fine dining, first class, team dinner, stage, podcast. The subject is small in the frame, inhabiting the scene.
- **Device mockup.** A floating 3D phone showing a real story or post, often flanked by faded real screenshots. Used on taxonomy and "here is the format" slides.
- **Proof wall.** Dozens of real testimonials or content thumbnails tiled and dimmed behind the subject. The density is the argument. Used on carousel covers and pillar slides.

**The Pulsify swap:** keep the proof screenshot, device mockup, and proof wall classes exactly. Replace the lifestyle frame with our currency: student dashboards, build screenshots, client-call frames, whiteboards, real workspaces. The composition stays, the contents change.

## Carousel structure grammar (the standout design)

Two moves are worth naming because they are the "crazy design" that reads as premium:

1. **The editorial cover.** Display serif italic fused with a giant bold sans number over a cinematic photo or a proof wall, with a story-highlight ring and a follower count overlaid, and the key nouns hand-underlined. Magazine-grade, not the usual creator carousel.
2. **The pillar or type slide.** A giant light ghost number behind the text, a bold headline, a lighter description, then either a colour-coded chip row (cadence in purple, function in gray, audience in green) or a set of category pills carrying platform glyphs (YouTube, Instagram), and a real artifact showcase (a device mockup of an actual story, or a tiled wall of real content behind the team).

The three carousel structures these compose into (methodology, taxonomy, philosophy) live in [[frameworks/carousel-system]]. This page is the design layer under all three.

## The chip code (the reusable innovation)

Colour-coded pills that let a reader absorb metadata without reading prose. On the 964k taxonomy carousel the code is cadence / function / audience. Adopt it for any "types of X" asset: pick three metadata axes, assign one colour each, hold the mapping across the set. It is the single most portable and useful design innovation in the capture.

## How Pulsify uses this

1. **Generators output design direction per slide, not just copy.** For every generated slide the studio returns: the annotation (which colour, which mark, on which line), the image class plus a specific artifact slot ("student dashboard showing MRR", "before/after profile"), the chip scheme where relevant, the ghost number, the text treatment (pill-box vs drop-shadow), and the alignment. Copy without this is an unfinished asset.
2. **One locked template.** A single Pulsify design template encodes the pill boxes, the marker annotation set, the ghost-number style, the chip colours, the device-mockup frame, and the type pairing. Every creator's assets inherit it, so the brand is coherent and the editor is not designing from scratch each time.
3. **The currency guardrail is enforced in design, not just copy.** The image-class picker offers proof screenshot, device mockup, proof wall, and real-workspace. It does not offer lifestyle flex. The rule is structural.
