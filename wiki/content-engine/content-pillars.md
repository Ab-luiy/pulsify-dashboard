# Content pillars

Content pillars are the 3–5 topic categories that generate all of a client's video, reel, and story content. Every piece of content belongs to a pillar. The pillars are defined before a single video is planned and do not change week to week based on inspiration.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Random content is the default for most coaches. They post what feels relevant, or what a competitor just posted, or what they think their audience wants to see. The result is a channel that covers too many topics, confuses the algorithm, and produces zero compounding library effect.

Pillars create the structure that makes a content library searchable, purposeful, and connected. Every pillar maps to the micro niche and the unique mechanism. Every video in a pillar builds on the others. After 20–30 videos, the library begins to work as a funnel by itself — new viewers can enter at any pillar and trace their way through to the mechanism and the offer.

## Core mechanics

### Pulsify's three content categories for its own channels

For Pulsify's own YouTube and social content (as specified in CLAUDE.md), the three categories are:

1. **Breaking down a coaching content concept** — explaining how a specific element of YouTube growth, content strategy, or funnel building works. These are mechanism-based, Miro-board-style breakdowns.

2. **Arbitrary case study showing how someone achieved X** — documented proof that the mechanism works in practice. Uses Miro, Gamma, or Google Docs as the visual demo. Specific numbers, specific niche, specific steps.

3. **Breaking down common beliefs around coaching** — addressing and reframing the widely held assumptions that keep coaches stuck. These are belief-shifting videos: "Why YouTube doesn't work for most coaches (and what actually does)," "The real reason your content isn't booking calls."

### How to define pillars for a client

The pillar definition process:

1. **Map the micro niche** — what is the specific problem this creator solves for their specific avatar?
2. **Map the customer journey** — what does a new follower need to believe, understand, and see proven before they will book a call?
3. **Identify the five content jobs** — within that journey, there are 3–5 recurring question types that every prospective client has. Each question type becomes a pillar.
4. **Name the pillars** — short, clear, referencing the mechanism and niche where possible

Example for a dropshipping coach (the SBT reference in the Offer-Funnel-Content SOP):
- Pillar 1: Product research and validation
- Pillar 2: Supplier sourcing and negotiation
- Pillar 3: Paid ad strategy for e-commerce
- Pillar 4: Student results and case studies
- Pillar 5: Business model evolution (from $0 to scale)

Every video, reel, and YouTube upload is assigned to one of these five pillars before scripting begins.

### Pillar-to-funnel mapping

Each pillar naturally contains TOF, MOF, and BOF content. The funnel layer is determined by how much trust is required to accept the premise:

| Funnel layer | Pillar content type |
|---|---|
| TOF | How-to searchable videos within a pillar — "How to find winning products using the supplier-first method" |
| MOF | Deep dives and breakdowns within a pillar — "Why the supplier-first method outperforms product research tools" |
| BOF | Proof within a pillar — "How Marcus went from $3K to $20K/month using the supplier-first product system" |

See [[content-funnel-tof-mof-bof]] for the full layer mechanics.

### The weekly content system

Once pillars are established, the weekly content system runs the same output every week:

- **1 reel per day** — topic pulled from the active pillar rotation. Mix of TOF hooks and MOF proof.
- **1–2 YouTube videos per week** — one searchable TOF how-to + one MOF deep dive (alternating)
- **Daily stories** — primarily MOF/BOF: testimonials, backstories, behind-the-scenes, social proof

The pillar rotation: if there are 5 pillars and the channel produces 2 YouTube videos per week, each pillar gets a YouTube video approximately every 2–3 weeks. More frequent pillar cycling produces a shallower library. Consistent deep coverage of each pillar produces the searchable, comprehensive library that compounds.

### Idea generation within a pillar

Once a pillar is defined, idea generation is systematic rather than creative:

**Ayman Arab's approach (searchability-first):**
- Type the pillar keyword into the YouTube search bar and read the autocomplete suggestions — these are what people are actively searching for
- Filter by outlier score in VidIQ: any video with a score above 7–8 on a competitor's channel is a concept to target
- Each step of the niche process becomes a video: "how to find a product" → "how to validate a product" → "how to source a supplier" → each step is a searchable title

**Alexei's approach (concept angle):**
- Find a saturated outlier from the broader market (a proven concept with many high-view versions)
- Apply the micro niche angle: the same concept, but for the specific avatar
- Test searchable variations until one hits outlier performance, then iterate from there

**Pulsify's synthesis:** Use Ayman's search-based approach for TOF content (it is optimized for discoverability). Use Alexei's outlier approach for MOF content (it is optimized for trust-building and conviction).

## How Pulsify uses this

Pillar documentation is step 7 in [[pulsify-order-of-operations]]. It happens after the unique mechanism and offer are locked (steps 2–3) because the pillars are derived from the mechanism — they are the questions a prospect needs answered before they believe the mechanism is real and worth pursuing.

Pulsify documents the pillars in a client's `content-pipeline.md` file. The first batch of content (step 8) is built from the pillar list, not from brainstormed ideas.
