# The reel format ladder

The canonical Pulsify framework for Instagram reels: eight formats mapped to revenue stages, each with a job, a production mode, and a rating on growth / production ease / buyer intent. Reels never close — they make strangers problem-aware and redirect attention ([[synthesis/cross-creator-synthesis]] law; "a one minute reel can't close anyone"). The ladder decides WHICH reel a creator should be making at their stage, which is the question clients actually ask.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Most clients copy whatever reel format is trending, which means they run late-stage formats (reach plays, lifestyle vlogs) at early-stage accounts where the job is buyers, not eyeballs. The ladder is the diagnostic: match format to stage, accept the growth/buyer-intent tradeoff each format carries, and alternate reach phases with conversion phases deliberately. Source proof: 131 reels over 2.5 years -> $5M (Niksetting); the anti-volume law is founder-ratified (dial 2: weekly originals, daily derivatives).

## The governing rules

- **One job per reel.** Traffic reels reach strangers; nurture reels warm followers; neither sells. Selling happens in stories, DMs, and long form.
- **Topic beats hook mechanics.** "When your topic is [bad] nothing will work." Hooks matter (outcome + specificity + speed), but idea selection is the lever.
- **Niche the topics, always** — with the ratified TOF exception: top-of-funnel may lean broad enough for the algorithm (the ICP-x-dream-follower overlap), never below it.
- **Formats saturate in 6-12 months.** Copying last year's winning format fails; the meta-skill is reinvention ([[playbooks/format-invention-pipeline]], pending).
- **Raw beats polished** (founder-ratified dial 4). Production touches are amplifiers (music, light edits), never the baseline. At high credibility, deliberately cycling back to raw earns respect every 3-6 months.
- **Cadence:** ~1 original recording session feeding derivative clips; reels post daily-ish as derivatives of long form, not as daily inventions.

## The ladder (stage -> format)

1. **Social-based reels** — stage: starting out (~3K followers). Client-proof and live social proof; side-angle/interview style, filmed with real students/clients (Zoom works). Growth: poor. Buyer intent: high — these get the first $10-20K months. Boostable with simple ads.
2. **Achievement-based reels** — stage: multiple six figures. Income milestones and growth indicators as credibility scaffolding ("how I went from 0 to..."), standing-format, any decent location. Growth: decent (people love numbers). Buyer intent: decently high. Retire the number-leading once proof exists — numbers are early-stage scaffolding. **Pulsify note:** milestones framed on student results wherever possible; own-revenue claims only when verifiable.
3. **Demonstration reels (Miro-style)** — the transformation-on-a-screen format: a system, belief, or framework shown technically (screen share + board). Desk + laptop production. Growth: excellent while the format variant is fresh. Buyer intent: excellent. The format that built the source's brand; saturates fastest — needs the invention pipeline behind it.
4. **Authority-pairing clips** — stage: ~70-100K/month. Stage talks, podcasts (own or guest), masterminds, in-person client events, team-in-content — clipped into distributable moments. "Just providing value isn't enough" at this stage. Growth: can spike massively (the brand-making 1.1M-view podcast). Production: easy once credibility opens doors.
5. **Talking head (raw)** — stage: established credibility. Strategy breakdowns and insider systems ONLY — broad talking heads go viral and buy nothing. Camera on, idea sharp, post. Growth: modest. Buyer intent: excellent. This is the sophistication-inversion play: max-raw at max-credibility.
6. **Proof-of-positioning clips** — podcast-style credibility moments and client-result clips that "reconfirm" everything prior; world-view content, not how-to. Often lives on the second/proof account. Growth: slow but viral-capable. Buyer intent: excellent.
7. **Reach-based reels** — stage: industry leader. Maximum distribution on niche-specific topics (never generic routines/motivation): contrarian takes, conviction to camera, institutional validation, cinematic allowed here. Growth: the fastest (100K -> 139K followers in ~2 months). Buyer intent: deliberately lower — this is the "hot audience" manufacturing phase.
8. **Vlog-style conversion content** — the harvest after a reach spike: behind-the-scenes niche stories (the fired-setter arc), systems + massive pain shown. Views intentionally drop ~5x; conversions spike ("this is the actual strategy"). Growth: good. Buyer intent: maximum. **Pulsify register:** BTS of real work, not lifestyle.

**The alternation:** reach phases (7) make the audience hot; conversion phases (8 + stories) harvest it. Judge each phase by its own metric — views in reach, attributed DMs/calls in conversion — never one by the other's.

## Format taxonomy (cross-cutting axes)

Every reel sits on three axes (from the messaging system): philosophy-driven vs technical-driven delivery; transformational / educational / value-driven job; direct vs indirect mechanism reference (mostly indirect — say the belief, not the mechanism's name). Brand constraints: a maximum of ~3 recurring formats at a time (more dilutes the brand), consistent outfits/settings as recognition anchors.

## How Pulsify uses this

1. **Client diagnostic:** place the client on the ladder from revenue + credibility inventory; prescribe the 2-3 formats for their stage (this slots into the Brand Architect-equivalent motion and the dashboard's prescriptive Action Engine).
2. **The second channel rule:** experiments and niche-deep formats go to the proof/second account so the main brand never dilutes.
3. **Derivative pipeline:** long form -> clips chosen at retention spikes -> reels; original recording stays weekly (ratified).
4. **Packaging:** titles-thumbnails doctrine applies to reels as text hooks; hook triad outcome/specificity/speed; one idea per reel.
