# Miro Proof Board v1

Miro Proof Board v1 is the approved thumbnail direction for Pulsify's AI content/workflow videos: a whiteboard-style AI system breakdown with a real creator face, clean Claude UI proof, flat tool icons, and a bold yellow headline. The thumbnail should feel like a business operator showing a working system, not a gimmick, guru set, or MrBeast-style spectacle.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

This profile sits directly inside content -> offer -> funnel. The thumbnail sells the click for the content, the content demonstrates the mechanism, and the mechanism creates trust for the offer and funnel. The viewer should understand in one second that Pulsify is not selling AI hype. Pulsify is showing how AI becomes installed growth infrastructure for coaches, creators, and B2B/B2C operators.

## Core mechanics

The approved thumbnail has five layers.

First: the headline. Use a large yellow highlight bar with bold black uppercase text, subtle black drop shadow, and crisp edges. The approved headline style is the strongest part of the asset. Do not soften it. Do not redesign it when only the supporting layout changes.

> ⚠️ Scope narrowed 2026-07-21: "do not soften it" applies to **this profile only**, which is a board thumbnail (Pattern B in [[content-engine/thumbnail-layout-patterns]]). The yellow bar is not the house treatment for every thumbnail. Raw photos and screen recordings go quiet: no bar, no all-caps headline, no exclamation marks. Founder-ratified.

Second: the whiteboard grid. The background should be a clean white/light grey Miro-style board. The grid needs to be visible, but only slightly. Fully white looks empty. A heavy grid looks cheap and distracting.

Third: the AI workflow. Use flat, matte tool icons with subtle drop shadows. No glare. No glossy 3D tiles. No warped logos. The workflow should imply a system: icons feeding into YouTube and content operations. Arrows can look hand-drawn, but they still need clear spacing and direction.

Fourth: the Claude UI stack. The front screenshot should read `Good morning, AB`. It should not have browser traffic-light dots if it is meant to look like the Claude app/home state. The behind screenshot should be a separate full rectangular UI panel, not chopped on the right side. If browser dots appear, they belong on the behind/upper panel, not the front Claude greeting panel. The behind screenshot should feel busy and relevant: prompts, documents, content planning blocks, AI outputs, workflow panels.

Fifth: the creator frame. The face must stay real. Do not AI-generate, beautify, reshape, smooth, puff up, or reinterpret the face. Keep the original camera identity, beard alignment, AirPods, cap, hoodie, posture, and room feel. Fix artifacts only: beard misalignment, cap dents, awkward head-down posture, distorted AirPods, distracting background clutter. The background should stay close to the original room unless the user explicitly asks for a replacement. Avoid guru studios, luxury sets, heavy blur, and bedroom clutter as the focal point.

## Exact iteration path that produced the approved direction

1. Started with the raw board: `AI CONTENT` headline, three AI/tool icons, YouTube hub, six workflow labels, Claude screenshot area, and creator webcam frame.
2. Strengthened the headline into the approved yellow-bar treatment: huge black uppercase text, thick yellow highlight, subtle black drop shadow.
3. Removed gimmicky thumbnail effects: shiny icons, exaggerated strokes around the person, toy-like app tiles, and overproduced creator cutout styling.
4. Restored the icons toward the original shape language: Claude-style orange tile, Perplexity-style black tile, ChatGPT-style green tile. Flat, matte, proportional, with only subtle drop shadows.
5. Built the right side around Claude proof: one front Claude panel with the greeting, one separate behind panel showing a busy AI/content workflow interface.
6. Corrected the Claude greeting from `Good morning, Abdul` to `Good morning, AB`.
7. Removed the unnecessary traffic-light window dots from the front greeting panel. Allowed them only on the behind browser-style panel.
8. Replaced the AI-generated human look with the original camera identity. The person must remain recognizable and natural.
9. Corrected facial details: do not make the face chubby, do not lower the head, fix the beard line, keep AirPods Pro accurate, smooth cap dents without changing the cap style.
10. Removed or minimized distracting microphone/background issues depending on the specific version. The rule is not "always remove mic." The rule is: do not invent or replace gear, and do not crop real gear awkwardly.
11. Rejected the fake guru background. The creator frame should stay close to the original real room, cleaned only enough to remove distraction.
12. Brought back the small original orange Claude pet in the same top-right position as the original image. Do not redesign it into a cute mascot. Use the exact original-style pixel pet.
13. Made the rear UI panel full and whole so the screenshot stack looks intentional instead of cropped.
14. Kept everything else locked once it worked. The winning version came from conservative edits, not reinvention.

## Prerequisites before making a new thumbnail

Do not start from a blank generated image unless there is no real source material. For this profile, gather:

- A real creator camera frame with the face clearly visible.
- A clean version of the original room/background, preferably unblurred.
- The actual app screenshots to feature, especially Claude or workflow proof.
- The exact headline before design starts.
- The exact workflow labels before design starts.
- The tool icons or clear references for them.
- The intended audience and funnel layer: coaches, creators, B2B/B2C operators, conversion-focused authority content.
- A clear decision on what must be locked: face, headline, UI screenshots, icons, layout.

The non-negotiable: the face and identity are source assets, not creative clay. The AI can clean artifacts. It cannot invent a better-looking version of the operator.

## From-scratch build sequence

1. Create the canvas at 16:9.
2. Lay in the faint Miro-style whiteboard grid.
3. Place the headline first and lock it.
4. Place the creator frame in the lower-right and lock identity.
5. Add the front Claude screenshot with `Good morning, AB`.
6. Add the behind screenshot as a full separate panel, offset so it reads as layered proof.
7. Place the flat app icons on the left/mid-left with subtle drop shadows.
8. Build the YouTube hub and workflow arrows around the labels.
9. Add the original-style orange Claude pet in the top-right, same role as a small accent.
10. Run a small-size readability check: headline, face, Claude greeting, icons, and central workflow must still read at thumbnail size.
11. Make only conservative cleanup passes: spacing, shadows, beard/hat/AirPods artifacts, background clutter, UI cropping.
