# Pain-point content bank

The pains our leads feel, in their words, each tied to the part of Pulsify that takes it off their plate. It feeds outreach copy and Pulsify's own long-form and short-form content. It came out of the 2026-10-22 outreach planning session.

**Standing rule (founder 2026-10-22):** everything we work out while answering prospects and building DM sequences also becomes content. When a new pain, objection, reply or angle comes up in outreach, add it here the same day in this format (their words, what we do, the relief, status, 2 long-form titles, 2 short-form hooks). Written up late: it was finished on 10-22 but a folder guard kept it out of the vault until 2026-10-27.

Who this speaks to: money-skill creators (trading, ecom, TikTok Shop, Amazon FBA, high-ticket sales, Airbnb, general make-money-online) who want to grow and make more money from their content.

## Rules before anything here goes public

- Only claim what the status line says is live. If a feature is marked "not deployed" or "assisted", say exactly that or leave it out.
- No private source names in public copy. The concept pages behind points 9 to 11 are ours to learn from; in a video, the idea is told in our own words with no names.
- Figures in titles are either real (Nick's, anonymised, or the public example in point 9) or a `[X]` slot filled from real data before posting. Never invent a number to fill one.
- Titles follow [[content-engine/packaging-rules]]: lowercase, the core readable in about four words, a parenthetical kicker, and a hook that proves the promise inside the first 30 seconds. Copy follows [[content-engine/copy-voice-guide]].

## The pains

### 1. Views vs money

In their words: "This video blew up, but did it even bring me any cash?" They judge every video by its views because that is the only number they can see.

What we do: the app ranks their videos by the money each one brought in (cash by video).

The relief: they finally know which videos pay them.

Status: live. It needs their tracked links installed in descriptions and pinned comments before it shows anything.

Long-form titles:
- `my most viewed video made me $0 (and my smallest one paid the bills)`
- `how to see which of your videos actually make you money (step by step)`

Short-form hooks:
- "Your best video by views is probably not your best video by money."
- "Stop checking views. Check this number instead."

### 2. The remake trap

In their words: they remake the video that went viral, it flops, and they are back to hoping the algorithm is kind this week.

What we do: we find what actually brought in money, then do more of it through small tests and changes, one video at a time.

The relief: growth stops depending on luck.

Status: this is how we run the service. The cash-by-video ranking (point 1) and the outlier score (point 7) are the live tools behind it.

Long-form titles:
- `why remaking your viral video keeps flopping (and what to test instead)`
- `the testing system that grows a channel without going viral (full breakdown)`

Short-form hooks:
- "You remade your viral video and it flopped. Here's why."
- "Going viral once is luck. This is how you make it repeatable."

### 3. The blank page

In their words: they sit down to film with no idea what to make, so they post something random or skip the week, and it eats at them the whole time.

What we do: the app plans the next videos from what already worked for them and writes them in their own voice.

The relief: they sit down already knowing what to film and what to say.

Status: live (copywriter chat and the YouTube flow).

Long-form titles:
- `how i plan a month of videos in one sitting (from what already worked)`
- `never stare at a blank page again (my exact content planning system)`

Short-form hooks:
- "If you don't know what to film this week, you're planning from the wrong place."
- "Your next video idea is already sitting in your old videos."

### 4. Calls with people who were never buying

In their words: two minutes into the call they can tell this person was never going to buy, then they spend half an hour being polite.

What we do: before each call they get a short brief on the person: which videos they watched, what they clicked and what they said in DMs.

The relief: their best energy goes to the people who are serious.

Status: live. Pre-call briefs and the full lead journey (every step a lead took before booking, pulsify-saas PR #81) both shipped; the journey went live on 2026-10-23.

Long-form titles:
- `stop taking sales calls with people who were never buying (do this first)`
- `how to know who's serious before the call even starts (full breakdown)`

Short-form hooks:
- "You knew in two minutes they weren't buying. Then you stayed for thirty."
- "Read this before every sales call and you'll stop wasting your evenings."

### 5. Stories get views, nobody replies

In their words: their stories get views but nobody replies, and they feel they have to come up with something clever every single day.

What we do: the story builder makes full sequences around the one thing they sell. Each slide pulls into the next, and the last one gives people a reason to DM.

The relief: no daily scramble, and the people who do reply are already warm.

Status: live (story studio).

Long-form titles:
- `why your stories get views but zero replies (and the fix)`
- `the story sequence that turns viewers into dms (slide by slide)`

Short-form hooks:
- "Two hundred people watched your story. Nobody replied. Here's why."
- "Your stories don't need to be clever. They need to go somewhere."

### 6. One video becomes a second job

In their words: they film one video, then lose the rest of the week making stories, carousels and captions out of it.

What we do: the app helps turn what they filmed into the week's stories and carousels, written in their voice.

The relief: they film once and most of the week is sorted.

Status: live, but assisted. They still review and post. Never call it automatic.

Long-form titles:
- `how i turn one video into a week of content (without a second job)`
- `film once, post all week (my exact repurposing workflow)`

Short-form hooks:
- "You don't need more ideas. You need more out of the one video you made."
- "One video, seven days of posts. This is how."

### 7. Not knowing why one video popped

In their words: one video takes off and they have no idea why, so they can't do it again on purpose.

What we do: the outlier score flags any video doing well above their own normal, so they can see which ones broke out and study them.

The relief: they stop guessing.

Status: live.

Long-form titles:
- `why one of your videos popped and the rest didn't (how to find out)`
- `how to spot your outlier videos and make more of them (step by step)`

Short-form hooks:
- "One video popped and you don't know why. That's the problem."
- "Compare your videos to your own average, not to anyone else's."

### 8. The growth-operator contrast

In their words (the operator side): everything runs by hand in Excel and Notion, the tools sit miles apart, and nobody can connect the numbers back to the money.

What we do: in Pulsify-AI every metric is already connected, from the video to the click to the call to the sale.

The relief: this is why Pulsify-AI exists. Use it as the founder's own story, not as a feature list.

Status: the connected workspace is live. Keep any specific claim to the pieces marked live in points 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7.

Long-form titles:
- `why i stopped running clients on spreadsheets (and built this instead)`
- `your numbers live in [X] different tools (here's what that costs you)`

Short-form hooks:
- "If your numbers live in a spreadsheet, you can't see what makes you money."
- "I got tired of copying numbers between tools, so I built one place for all of it."

### 9. "I don't have enough followers"

In their words: creators sitting at 500 to 5k followers think the count is why nothing converts, and they quit a system that was working.

What we do: reframe it. The foundation matters, not the volume. The example in the vault ([[concepts/micro-niche-youtube]]) is a channel of about 4,500 subscribers making about $58k a month with a simple board and a face on camera. In public, tell it without naming the person.

The relief: they stop quitting something that works.

Status: a reframe, not a feature.

Long-form titles:
- `you don't need more followers to make money (here's what you need)`
- `how a 4,500 subscriber channel makes $58k a month (full breakdown)`

Short-form hooks:
- "You don't have a follower problem."
- "Small channels make serious money every month. Follower count isn't what decides it."

### 10. Trust recession

In their words (the buyer side): they are tired of cash screenshots, rented cars and lifestyle flexing. They don't believe any of it any more.

What we do: show the work on screen: the charts, the boards, the actual process. See [[concepts/trust-recession]]. A real figure inside a real interface is fine; a figure next to a watch or a car is not ([[content-engine/packaging-rules]], thumbnail rule 5).

The relief: buyers trust what they can see being done.

Status: content doctrine, not a feature.

Long-form titles:
- `why nobody believes your income screenshots anymore (and what works now)`
- `stop flexing, start showing (how to build trust that sells)`

Short-form hooks:
- "Your audience has seen a thousand cash screenshots. Show them the chart instead."
- "The flex doesn't sell anymore. The process does."

### 11. No recognisable method

In their words: people watch and watch and still don't book, because nothing tells them this creator can fix their exact problem.

What we do: help the creator name their own method and use a format people recognise instantly, so leads book once they've seen enough. See [[concepts/unique-mechanism]].

The relief: viewers know what they're buying and why it's this person.

Status: part of the service, not a standalone feature.

Long-form titles:
- `why people watch your videos but never book (you're missing this)`
- `how to name your method so people buy from you (step by step)`

Short-form hooks:
- "If people can't name your method, they can't buy it."
- "They watched ten of your videos and still didn't book. Here's what's missing."

## Case study examples

Public creators used only as teaching examples. They are not our clients, and we don't contact them from this page.

**dovytrades (YouTube).** Recent videos run about 7k to 16k views. The top ones are "exact setup and result" videos:
- "i made $7,962 on my funded with this simple strategy" (about 16k)
- "The Wick Theory Strategy I Will Use For Life" (about 15k)
- "I made $29,162 Trading This One Setup" (about 15k)

Opinion and live-loss videos sit lower, for example "Trading Live Losing $3,234" (about 6.9k). Views pulled 2026-10-22. We know nothing about his sales, so this only teaches what gets watched in trading content (points 1, 2 and 7), never what pays.

**tb.tradez (Instagram).** The founder's estimate is about +7,000 followers in 3 months. Unverified, because we can't pull other people's IG data. Needs the founder's screenshot before any public use.

**Nick (our client).** Always anonymous in public: "a trading creator". Went from $10-20k a month to $50k a month, $98k in total sales.
