# Story design reference standard (read at full resolution, 2026-10-08)

> Current shared standard: [[content-engine/reference-replication-profile]] and [[playbooks/reference-replication-sop]] merge the full [[synthesis/carousel-reference-forensic-audit-2026-10-08|76-carousel-image examination]] into story production. Current founder direction: preserve real environments; no blanket blur or mirrored/blurred extension; intentional original-pixel subject restoration supports text, cards, figures and brand objects behind people. Purpose/copy first, one registered photo/mask transform, essential labels clear, genuine app glyphs and semantic figures. Older whole-photo/extension assumptions below are historical and cannot override this direction.

What the reference sequences actually do, slide by slide, so Pulsify stories copy the craft and not a guess of it. Written after the founder rejected abstract charts ("they don't read at all"). This is the bar the app's story builder must meet for clients.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Stories connect content, offer and funnel. The references combine photography, editorial type, recognizable artifacts and meaningful abstract figures. Each element should help explain the slide's point. ## The rules the references follow

1. Choose the visual for the relationship being explained. Chat receipts, profile headers, dashboard crops, thumbnails and recognizable app marks carry evidence or identity. Price tiles, funnels and paths explain comparison or sequence. A measured graph requires real data and clear labels; a conceptual figure must not imply measured results. (Nik seq 2 slides 1-4; Nik seq 3 slides 4-9; Siggy 2, 5, 8; Kobey's Shelby sequence 3.)
2. Type has several modes. Nik seq 2/3 mix regular sans, selected bold and serif asides. Brez and much of New here use bold sans throughout. Siggy varies name/result/qualifier sizes within one headline. Exact source font names and universal pixel sizes are not established. Use phone-size reading tests and deliberate phrase breaks; preserve the active highlight's Helvetica decision.
3. One emphasis device per phrase: red underline under one word or phrase; a sharp red or black marker box behind one word; a word turned red; a thin red outline rectangle around a bullet block; a white hand-drawn circle around one detail inside a screenshot; a lime or purple pill once.
4. Big numbers carry a light suffix or date underneath: "$14k" bold, "3rd October" light; "$12,5k" bold + "PIF" regular.
5. Progressions use coloured dots joined by thin lines to cards, colour shifting along the path (yellow -> orange -> red -> pink in Nik seq 2 slide 2).
6. Translucent edge-bleeding forms are one option: Nik's teal funnel bands run off the right side, with outlined stage labels joined by thin white lines. Opaque receipts, text-only frames and solid tiles also occur. Preserve complete essential labels even when a figure bleeds off-frame.
7. Depth: the person can stand in front of cards (Nik seq 2 slide 4), or remain in color against a desaturated background (Siggy 5). Nik seq 3 slide 1 partly hides a teaser line behind the head. Keep essential words readable. Screen-plane product placement in seq 3 slides 4-6 is another distinct technique.
8. Photos vary from bright to heavily dimmed. Brez 1 uses black type on sky; Brez 2 switches to white as text reaches trees. Kobey's Shelby sequence dims earlier layers to introduce a new point. These observations do not override current natural-environment production rules: useful crops, no blanket blur or manufactured extension, narrow approved contrast where needed.
9. Lines have different jobs. Curved white arrows identify the person or destination in Kobey's Shelby sequence; red arrows link Siggy's journey; lime curls point to figures. Thin straight connectors and progress lines also occur. Match the weight to the job and preserve the founder's near-horizontal red underline direction for the active set.
10. Lists: red X marks for what is missing (Brez), dot bullets with bold keywords, colour-coded columns for a comparison (Nik seq 2 slide 1: grey heap of small tags vs big mustard blocks).
11. Repeated photos can carry progress. Brez 1/2, 3-5 and 6/7 use additive copy; Nik seq 3 slides 3-6 retain the presentation scene and advance the product explanation. Change or repeat the scene deliberately rather than forcing a unique photo on every slide.
12. A sequence needs a reason to continue. Use a finite lesson/feature promise, an objection answered on the next frame, a principle followed by evidence, or a visible artifact leading to a deeper asset. Aesthetic variation alone does not supply that reason.

## Shipped-set rules and tiers (2026-10-09)

The SaaS highlight set (see [[content-engine/pulsify-saas-highlight-final-2026-10-08]]) added: one photo reused with a blurred, dimmed carry-over and a soft shadow on layered pieces; designed cards instead of raw screenshots; real brand glyphs; red, black, white and a good-number green; no sample-data labels. ## Pulsify translation

- Artifacts must be truthful: our own app screens, our own photos, or clearly illustrative UI of the viewer's own situation (their browser, their calendar, their profile). Never a fabricated client message or result.
- Palette: red for pain and emphasis, emerald for the good number, white text; the homepage graphite for cards. Coin mark only on slide 1 and the video slide.
- Fonts on this PC: Helvetica (regular, light, bold) for sans, Georgia for the serif voice.
- These Pulsify choices are local production decisions, not the source creators' verified font or color tokens. Red also emphasizes positive claims in the sources; it is not universally a negative code.
- Separate a source claim from a verified result. Keep sample data labeled, metric definitions clear, comparison arithmetic correct and the source detail readable around any subject overlap.
