# Packaging rules

Packaging is how a video concept is translated into a specific title and thumbnail. The concept determines whether a video is worth making. The packaging determines whether anyone clicks on it. The best concept with bad packaging produces no views. Mediocre concepts with strong packaging outperform brilliant concepts with weak packaging every time.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Pulsify's clients spend time and resources producing content. Packaging failure wastes that production entirely — the video gets made, posted, and ignored. Packaging rules are the set of constraints that Pulsify applies before any video is titled or thumbnailed, so that production effort is spent on ideas that have a documented path to performance.

## Core mechanics

### The four-word title rule

Ayman Arab's most specific packaging constraint: the title should be compressible to four words or fewer that carry the full weight of the concept.

The test: if you cover the full title and show someone only four words, do those four words make them want to click?

- "How to find winning products for your Shopify dropshipping store in 2025" → the four-word core is "Find Winning Dropshipping Products" → does that work? Yes.
- "My honest thoughts on whether YouTube is worth your time as a high-ticket coach" → the four-word core is... unclear. The title is over-written. Compress it.

This rule applies differently to complete vs. repeat title formats:

**Complete title:** States the full concept so clearly the viewer knows exactly what they will get. No ambiguity. High search optimization. Example: "How I Booked 14 Calls From a 400-View YouTube Video."

**Repeat title format (not recommended for Pulsify's core content):** Titles that are ambiguous or curiosity-based without completing the promise. These optimize for click-through rate but reduce watch time from viewers who feel misled. Ayman recommends against repeat titles for authority-building channels.

### Face on camera

Both Ayman and Alexei agree: the creator must be on camera. Not a cartoon avatar. Not a generic stock photo. The face.

For channels competing in the trust recession environment, a face on camera is not optional — it is the primary signal that a real person with real credentials is behind the content. AI voiceovers, stock footage, and anonymous production signal the opposite.

**Thumbnail face requirements:**
- Clear, recognizable face (no small insets)
- Eye contact with the lens — not looking at the whiteboard, not looking off-screen
- Expression matched to the emotion of the topic (confident for results-based topics, curious for breakdown topics)

### The content angle formula

Alexei's content angle formula: take a proven outlier concept from the broader market + apply the micro niche angle + verify the angle is different from existing coverage.

The formula prevents two failure modes:
1. **Creating content nobody is searching for** — unique content with no demand produces zero views regardless of quality
2. **Creating content that blends in** — copying a proven concept without a new angle produces a worse version of something that already exists

The sweet spot: a proven concept (someone else already validated that viewers want this) + a new angle (a different creator would not have made this exact video) + micro niche framing (speaks to the specific avatar, not the general market).

### The hook and first-30-seconds rule

The packaging does not stop at the title and thumbnail. The hook — the first 30 seconds of the video — is the final packaging layer. If the title and thumbnail earn a click, the hook decides whether the viewer stays.

Alexei's rule: the first 30 seconds must confirm that the video delivers exactly what the title promised. Not setup. Not context. Confirmation.

Failing hook structure: "Hey everyone, welcome back. Today I want to talk about something I've been thinking about a lot..."
Working hook structure: "In this video, I'll show you the exact three-step framework I used to book 14 calls from a single 400-view YouTube video — and why a 400-view video can outperform a 2-million-view video if it is built for the right funnel layer."

The hook must do three things in the first 30 seconds:
1. Confirm the specific promise from the title
2. Signal that the creator has authority to deliver on that promise (briefly — not a bio)
3. Create an open loop: why is it worth watching all the way through?

### The open loop

An open loop is a piece of information that cannot be resolved until the viewer watches more. Alexei uses it explicitly as a retention device.

Examples:
- "Most people who try this make one mistake that eliminates all the benefit. I'll show you the mistake at the end." (Forces watch to the end)
- "The third reason is the one nobody talks about." (Forces forward progress to find the third reason)
- "Before I show you the system, I need to show you why the obvious alternative doesn't work." (Frames everything that follows as necessary context)

Open loops must be resolved. An unresolved open loop damages trust — the viewer feels misled, which damages retention on future videos.

### Editing rules by audience type

Ayman's specific editing guidance based on audience financial profile:

**B2B / high-income audience:**
- No jump cuts
- No intense music
- Clear speech at natural pace
- High-value content over entertainment value
- Minimal visual effects
- The viewer's time is money — do not waste it with production flair

**B2C / general public:**
- Jump cuts acceptable
- Background music adds energy
- More visual variety tolerated
- Entertainment value matters alongside information value

Most Pulsify clients serve B2B-adjacent audiences (coaches, operators, creators). Default to the B2B editing standard. A high-ticket operator does not want to feel like they are watching a TikTok content farm.

### Thumbnail rules

Derived from both Ayman and Alexei's frameworks:

1. **One clear focal point** — face, text, or a single graphic. Not three competing elements.
2. **Title and thumbnail must be connected but not redundant** — the thumbnail adds a visual element the title does not say, or vice versa. If the thumbnail just restates the title as a text overlay, one of them is wasted.
3. **Contrast matters** — the thumbnail must pop in a sea of other thumbnails at small size. Test at 120px width.
4. **Curiosity or proof** — thumbnails should create either curiosity ("what is that result?") or demonstrate proof ("that number is real and I want to know how"). Generic images accomplish neither.
5. **A number is fine on its own. It turns toxic when you couple it with status.** Founder ruling, 2026-07-21, replacing the earlier blanket "no income screenshots as thumbnails."

   A real interface showing a real figure, on its own, reads as blunt transparency and it is catchy. A payout ledger with account IDs and dates, a revenue dashboard, a platform P&L: these are evidence, and evidence is what the trust recession actually rewards. The viewer can go and check.

   The same number becomes obnoxious the moment it is coupled with watches, cars, or lifestyle. At that point the number stops being evidence and becomes a status claim, which is the signal that pre-categorises the creator as another scam. **The variable is the coupling, never the figure.**

   Still banned: a figure with no interface behind it, a number the creator cannot show inside the video, any figure paired with a lifestyle signal, and hype framing around it (exclamation marks, "PRINTED", shock faces). Consistent with [[concepts/trust-recession]], which already scoped this correctly as "income screenshots without context or audit trail."

6. **Layout comes from [[content-engine/thumbnail-layout-patterns]]** — four patterns (raw photo, board breakdown, screen recording, two people in a real location) with the text treatment scoped per pattern. Added 2026-07-21.

## How Pulsify uses this

Packaging rules apply to every piece of content Pulsify produces or reviews for clients. Before any video enters production (scripting, recording), the title must pass:
1. The four-word compression test
2. The hook confirmation test (can a 30-second intro be written that delivers on this title?)
3. The micro niche alignment test (does the title speak to the specific avatar?)

Packaging review is part of the weekly content system — titles and thumbnails are reviewed and approved before scripts are written. This sequence prevents the situation where a script is written for a concept that cannot be packaged well.
