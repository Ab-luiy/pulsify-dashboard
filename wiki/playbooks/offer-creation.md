# Offer creation

Offer creation is step 3 in [[pulsify-order-of-operations]]. It produces the packaged, priced, positioned mentorship product that every piece of content, every sales call, and every funnel element is designed to sell. The offer is not "what the coach does" — it is the named transformation, delivered through a specific mechanism, for a specific avatar, at a specific price.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

A vague offer is unsellable. "I help coaches scale their business" is not an offer — it is a category. An offer specifies: who, what transformation, by what mechanism, in what timeframe, for what price, with what level of access to the operator.

Every Pulsify client who stalls at $5K–$15K/month either has a vague offer or no offer at all — just a service they describe during a sales call and price on the fly. The offer creation playbook exists to fix this before building anything else.

## Core mechanics

### Offer architecture

A complete offer has six components:

**1. The target avatar**
Named, specific, with a revenue range and niche. Not "coaches who want to grow." "E-commerce operators doing $5K–$25K/month who have tried Shopify dropshipping and are stuck on product-market fit."

The avatar determines the pricing ceiling (what can they afford?), the content angle (what problems are they discussing online?), and the proof type (what transformation will make them believe?).

See [[marcus-avatar]] for the full 28-point template. Every offer must map to an equivalently specific avatar.

**2. The unique mechanism**
The named proprietary methodology the client uses. Not the outcome — the system that produces the outcome. "I'll help you grow your coaching business" is the outcome. "The Micro Niche YouTube Funnel + Profile Funnel + Tracking Stack" is the mechanism.

See [[unique-mechanism]] for the full build process.

**3. The transformation**
The specific, measurable result the client can expect. Stated in the avatar's terms, not the coach's terms. The transformation is not "build a YouTube channel." It is "$10K/month from YouTube in under 90 days."

The transformation must be:
- Specific and measurable (not "more clients" but "5 new clients per month")
- Achievable within the stated timeframe
- Connected logically to the mechanism

**4. The delivery model**
How the offer is delivered. Options:
- **1-on-1 weekly calls** — highest price point, most personal, operator-time-intensive
- **DWY group calls** — 10–20 clients per cohort, weekly group call, async support
- **DIY course + community** — lowest price point, highest leverage, requires strong curriculum

At the Marcus stage ($5K–$35K/month), Pulsify defaults to 1-on-1 or small group because:
- Operators at this stage cannot yet build curriculum that replaces their live guidance
- Marcus-stage prospects are willing to pay a premium for personal access
- The close rate is higher when the operator is personally involved

**5. The price**
Determined by the avatar's financial capacity and the transformation's perceived value. Not by what the operator is "comfortable charging."

Financial qualification criteria for offer pricing:
- **High-ticket ($3,000–$15,000):** Avatar is already earning $5K+/month, has a business, and is investing to scale something that already works
- **Mid-ticket ($500–$2,500):** Avatar is pre-revenue or early-stage, may not have $5K+ to invest, needs the lower barrier
- **Upsell path required for mid-ticket:** A $1,500 offer with no upsell path is a revenue ceiling, not a scaling strategy

The default for Marcus-stage clients with an existing revenue base: $3,000–$5,000 starting price with a clear path to $8,000–$12,000 as results compound.

**6. The guarantee or risk reversal**
Jeremy Haynes explicitly warns against income guarantees (legal risk). Pulsify does not advise income guarantees. Acceptable risk reversals:
- Effort-based guarantees ("If you complete all modules and do all the work, we will work with you until you get results")
- Access guarantees ("Lifetime access to all materials and calls")
- Money-back on attendance ("If you attend all calls for 30 days and see no progress, we will refund")

The guarantee must be designed so that the only people who can claim it are people who actually did the work — which filters out bad-faith refund attempts.

### The offer positioning statement

A complete offer can be expressed in one sentence:

> "I help [specific avatar] achieve [specific transformation] using [unique mechanism] in [timeframe] through [delivery model], starting at [price]."

Example:
> "I help e-commerce operators doing $10K–$30K/month break through their scaling plateau and reach $50K+/month using the Supplier-First YouTube Funnel system in 90 days, through bi-weekly 1-on-1 strategy calls, starting at $5,000."

If the sentence cannot be written cleanly, the offer is not complete.

### High-ticket vs. mid-ticket decision

The decision between high-ticket and mid-ticket entry point is a function of the audience's financial qualification:

**Choose high-ticket when:**
- The audience is already generating revenue ($5K+/month)
- The audience has disposable budget to invest in growth
- The avatar is a business owner, not a beginner
- The mechanism can credibly produce a transformation worth 3–5x the offer price in 90 days

**Choose mid-ticket with upsell path when:**
- The audience is pre-revenue or early-stage ($0–$3K/month)
- Financial objections are the primary sales barrier
- The mechanism works for beginners, not just operators with existing infrastructure
- A $1,500 entry point creates enough proof and momentum to upsell to $5,000+ at 90 days

**Never offer mid-ticket without an explicit upsell path.** A mid-ticket offer with no upsell is a business model with a ceiling.

### Offer audit checklist

Before the offer goes live, check:

- [ ] Avatar is named and specific (revenue range, niche, exact problem)
- [ ] Unique mechanism is named and documented
- [ ] Transformation is measurable and stated in the avatar's language
- [ ] Delivery model is defined (what exactly does the client get, how often, through what format)
- [ ] Price is set based on avatar qualification, not operator comfort
- [ ] Risk reversal is defined without income guarantee language
- [ ] The one-sentence positioning statement can be written cleanly

## How Pulsify uses this

Offer creation is the bottleneck in the majority of day-one client audits. When Pulsify takes on a new client, step 3 is almost always the first creative work — because even clients who "have an offer" typically have a vague description they are pricing on the fly, not a packaged, positioned, priced product.

Pulsify builds the offer collaboratively with the client: the client provides the delivery model and the result data; Pulsify provides the mechanism naming, the positioning statement, and the pricing strategy.

Once the offer is complete, it becomes the anchor for every downstream page, script, and sequence. The VSL is built around it. The Profile Funnel bio references it. The hammer them content is built from its objections and questions.

Added 2026-09-29, cross-check from Dustin Varano ([[synthesis/dustin-varano-the-keys]]): his ICP rule is to build for people already winning, not beginners (fewer headaches, higher close rates, more PIFs, premium pricing), which matches our own targeting of coaches already doing $10-35k a month. His offer breakdown runs nine sections (buyer, transformation, timeframe, named system, evidence, pricing, the funnel from opt-in to post-call automations, false beliefs with headline angles, compliance), a useful completeness check against the offer architecture above. His intake also pulls the client's social posts and comment sections to capture voice and objections, and uses 3-5 voice memos when a client has no social presence.
