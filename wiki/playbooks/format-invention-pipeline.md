# The format invention pipeline

How new content formats get invented, tested, launched, and retired. This is the adaptability engine: formats saturate in roughly a quarter, so the durable skill is not any format but the machine that produces the next one. Single-source (Niksetting 01) at the core, with SooWei's AI-assisted research grafted where it strengthens steps. This playbook is why Pulsify never gets stranded on a dead format — ours or a client's.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Every format in [[content-engine/reel-format-ladder]] has a shelf life ("if it takes longer than 3 months, it's not successful anymore"; copying a year-old format "won't work"). The two-clock rule governs everything: offer and delivery iterate daily; acquisition systems and content formats run in ~3-6 month structures and then get reinvented. Pulsify sells content systems — this pipeline is the part that keeps them alive, and it is also how we invent Pulsify-native formats instead of arriving second to trends.

## The five roles (the pipeline's spine)

Creative -> strategist -> developer -> innovator -> analyzer. One person can hold all five (early Pulsify clients will), but the mode-switching is explicit: each stage is a different job, and confusing them ("confusing yourself along the way") is the normal failure.

## Stage 1 — state (the ideation precondition)

Clear mind before research: whatever the founder's meditation form is (walks, journaling, stillness). Two supporting laws: you consume far more than you register (~250+ reels/day, ~20% consciously) so the raw material is already in the head; and self-review beats buying more information — rewatch your own content and calls before buying anyone's course. Calm beats speed in the current economy; the filter is the advantage.

## Stage 2 — cross-industry research

1. Pick an industry OUTSIDE personal branding/education (clothing, software, ecom) — the home industry is "extremely undeveloped" next to industries with a century of marketing; import proven concepts instead of swiping neighbors.
2. Identify its core driver (clothing = creativity; ecom = distribution; software = technical).
3. Two buckets, three companies each: the biggest players + the ones you genuinely love. Proven + authentic — "if you only follow the proven path, you'll lose yourself... only authentic, it won't go viral."
4. ~15 minutes per company across their Instagram, site, campaigns. Screenshot everything into a Miro mood board: visuals, text styles, shapes.
5. Pattern-spot: gather, then connect ("this one fits with this one").

SooWei graft: run the outlier scan in parallel (competitor top-performers via research tooling, cross-vertical hook transplants — "Amazon FBA is drop shipping in 2016" -> "posting content in 2026 is Bitcoin in 2013"). Machine finds outliers; the founder still picks by taste.

## Stage 3 — strategy (verbal before visual)

Answer the two questions in words before touching design — verbal clarity is "75% of the clarity":
- Attention: what do I say that makes people listen?
- Conversion: what do I say that makes people pay?
Same message can serve either by delivery alone (calm minute-33 YouTube vs shouted 60-second reel). Do NOT invent new topics for a new format: "what you're going to talk about is what you're already talking about now." Then return to the mood board and package videos/text/shapes into the format. (This is the packaging-first law — title/thumbnail before production — in format form.)

## Stage 4 — development (V1/V2/V3 on a 3-day cycle)

Assemble the puzzle: V1. Then the highest-ROI nothing: a full 24-hour pause ("this break is one of your most valuable hours"). V2 with fresh eyes, pause again, V3 — and V3 is the first version that ships. Three days total where the untrained process takes months (the Miro reels took 2 months to invent; with the system, "two days, maybe a week").

## Stage 5 — launch and the gate

Beta-launch V3 to the market. The window: 1-6 months, with ~3 months as the practical saturation point. The gate: iterate ONLY on success — "the only moment you move to V2 [next iteration] is if V1 was successful. It's a test." Failure means back to research, not polishing.

## Stage 6 — innovation (not invention)

"Innovating doesn't mean inventing something. It means making something better." Successful formats get adjusted, not replaced — a new angle, a production twist — extending the run. The brand law behind it: constant visible innovation is why audiences stay ("I don't give people a reason not to watch me").

## Stage 7 — production moat (team execution)

Weekly planning with realistic hour estimates agreed live — pre-agreement kills excuses ("they don't have a single excuse... because they approved it"). Pipeline: edit -> wrap -> founder review on the phone ("change this, this, this"). SOP-out every format's editing rules (music, b-roll, color, non-negotiables) so revisions approach zero; pay editors bases, not per-video; retention runs on vision, not just pay.

## Stage 8 — analysis (the highest-paying role)

Weekly data loop; the role most people skip and the one that compounds: gather -> analyze -> improve -> repeat, "and in three months you invent a new format again and you add it to the process." A brand becomes magnetic (a guaranteed view floor) when the analyzer role runs; it stays "native" when it doesn't. The dashboard's metrics layer (P3) is this stage productized.

## The saturation clock (operating rule)

Track per format: birth date, performance trend, and the 3-6 month review. At review: innovate (if working), retire (if decaying), or reinvent (pipeline from stage 2). The founder's calendar must actually contain the pipeline time — call-batching (2 call days, 3 thinking days) exists precisely to fund stages 1-3.

## How Pulsify uses this

1. **Per client:** the ladder prescribes today's formats; this pipeline schedules their replacements. Client dashboards carry the format birth dates and the review clock.
2. **Pulsify-native formats:** our own inventions (vault-build BTS, receipt-walls, student-proof formats) run through the full pipeline — cross-industry research included — so our content never reads as a Niksetting clone.
3. **AI division of labor (ratified):** machines do research scans, outlier detection, clipping, and analysis; the founder does taste, state, and the verbal strategy. Intention stays human — that is the moat.

Extra input, added 2026-09-29 ([[synthesis/dustin-varano-the-keys]]): when feeding competitor content into ideation, feed only their outliers (top 5-10 pieces by views relative to that account's own average), plus a folder of the client's own offer, ICP docs, past content and testimonials. Then keep the format that brings in cash, not the one with the most views ([[synthesis/cash-by-video-spec]]). This sits beside the pipeline above and changes none of its stages.
