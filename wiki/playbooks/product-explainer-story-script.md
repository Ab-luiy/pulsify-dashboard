# Product explainer story script

How to write the casual "here is what the software does" video or story that sits in the middle of a sequence. Learned writing slide 9 of the SaaS highlight. The product is the subject. The founder is the voice, not the topic.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

A sequence about a product needs one moment where a human explains it plainly, between the problem slides and the product slides. The first drafts failed because they were about the founder ("here is what happens when you hire me") and because they were too trimmed. The version that worked is short declaratives, a clear turn, and a close that hands off without pointing at something that may not be there.

## Core mechanics

### The shape (borrowed from SooWei's IG walkthrough, structure only)

SooWei's pitch runs as one hook promising the exact process, then three numbered steps: get in and answer onboarding, then a roadmap call; analyze the numbers and fix the lowest-hanging fruit before top-of-funnel content; amplify with the content system. Pulsify keeps the shape and replaces the content with what the app and the service actually do. Do not carry over his named clients, his income figures, his account-manager cadence, or his post counts. His numbers are claims, not ours.

### The Pulsify translation

1. Hook that names the problem or the product (not the founder). Casual: "Okay, so what does this thing actually do?" or the heroic form: "Most creators are flying blind."
2. The turn, one line, with a beat before it: "This ends that."
3. Step one: the app asks questions (who you are, what you sell, who you sell to, how you talk). Everything after comes from the answers. Then a call goes through it together.
4. Step two: the numbers. Every post tracked from first view to payment. Who is closing and who is not.
5. Step three: it writes in your voice and tells you what to make next. A weekly call looks at what is paying and does more of it.
6. Close: "You keep doing what you are good at. This does the rest."

### Rules

- Product is the subject. "It tracks every post," not "I track."
- Services stay "we": the onboarding call and the weekly review are people, not software.
- Only claim what is live. Confirmed live: onboarding questions into the client brain, voice-matched writing, tracking from view to payment, closer and setter numbers. Not confirmed: automatic leak detection, call-transcript objection mining. Say "we" for anything not automatic.
- No income figures, no client results, no "guaranteed."
- Never end on "watch" or any pointer to media that may not exist. End on a statement.
- Casual and heroic together: short lines, rising stakes, one slow beat before the turn. Not louder, slower.
- Voice rules from [[content-engine/copy-voice-guide]] apply: no em-dash habit, no exclamation marks, no negative-parallelism.
- Off-the-top version: five anchors (problem, turn, tracking, voice and weekly call, close). If the speaker blanks, say the turn, the tracking line and the close.

## How Pulsify uses this

The shipped Pulsify script is in `output/highlight-saas/slide-9-script.md`. For a client, the app fills the same six beats from their own offer, customer and tracking setup, and marks any claim it cannot back as a flagged line.
