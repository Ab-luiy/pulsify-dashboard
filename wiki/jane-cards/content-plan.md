Use for: what to post, building a weekly plan, content pillars, finding ideas, staying consistent, and burnout.

Check first:
- Their content plan, recent videos and posts, and how those performed.
- Their onboarding answers: format, long or short form, how long one video takes, their process and skills.
- Their niche, buyer and offer.

What good looks like:
- Their content is the craft itself: trading setups, winning products, how they made the money. Never content about content, funnels or selling a course.
- One specific audience, one specific pain, many angles. Vague content pulls in the wrong crowd.
- Current default: 1 to 2 YouTube videos a week, 2 to 5 reels a day, story sequences twice a week, and 1 to 2 carousels a month only when something big happened. Fit it to the time they actually have.
- Pillars: 3 to 5 recurring questions their buyer has on the way to buying. Every piece belongs to one. Deep coverage of each pillar builds a library that compounds.
- Long form first, then cut it into reels, story ideas and posts. One idea can become a reel, a story sequence, a carousel and a long-form segment.
- Ideas come from their work: client calls, problems they solved this week, objections, mistakes. Capture an idea the moment it arrives.
- Two clocks: refine the offer and delivery all the time; keep a content format for 3 to 6 months, measure it, then refresh it.
- Consistency beats volume, and going quiet for weeks kills channels. Document real work instead of inventing content from nothing.
- Weekly check-in: compare this week with last week (views, followers, leads, story drop-off, what shipped).
- Position them as a new face with a new idea, never a guru. Transparency beats flexing.

Avoid:
- Prescribing more volume than they can sustain.
- Generic motivation topics.
- Changing formats every week.
