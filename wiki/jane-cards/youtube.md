Use for: video ideas, titles, thumbnails, outlines and hooks, which video to make next, and why a video flopped or worked.

Check first:
- Their videos and numbers: views, and above all which videos brought calls or cash if that is tracked.
- Their niche, their buyer, their offer and their proof.
- How long one video takes them and how they like to film (from onboarding).

What good looks like:
- YouTube is the most important channel to get right. Long form is the master asset that reels, stories and posts are cut from.
- Ideas come from what they have solved, a common mistake in their niche, something they struggled with and fixed, what took longest to figure out, the video they wish someone had made, and objections from their sales calls. Videos that did 10x or more of a channel's usual views in their niche are proven concepts: give one a new angle for their exact buyer.
- Micro niche: within the first 15 seconds a viewer should know whether the channel is for them. A few views from exactly the buyer beat big views from the curious.
- The order is idea, then title, then building the video. Packaging comes before production.
- Titles: cover the title and keep four words. Do those four words make someone click? Clear beats clever. Offer 2 titles and 2 thumbnail ideas per video.
- Thumbnails use real photos or real screens only, never AI people or backgrounds. One focal point, readable at 120px wide, connected to the title without repeating it. Four layouts: a raw photo with no text where one prop carries the tension; a board breakdown (the only layout that gets a short headline on a yellow bar); a screen recording with one huge real number and a large face, clutter cropped; two people in a real place. A real number on a real interface is evidence. Paired with cars or watches it becomes a status claim.
- The first 30 seconds confirm the title's promise, give one quick reason to trust them (not a bio) and open a loop that gets closed later.
- One video can serve every stage: the first fifth speaks wide, the middle speaks to the dream follower, the end speaks only to the buyer. Problem, struggle, discovery, result. Mention their method without over-explaining it, drop a light mid-video mention ("this is what we solve for clients") and finish on one clear next step.
- Script only the hook and the call to action. Give the body as bullet points they glance at and talk through naturally.
- Formats that fit: a board breakdown, face to camera, both together, a student call, a group call, in person with a student. Raw production reads as "showing the system", which this market trusts.
- For business-minded viewers: no jump-cut frenzy, no loud music, clear speech.
- Judge a video by the calls and cash it brought. Its real stage is what its viewers do next.
- Current standard: 1 to 2 videos a week, a default that can change per client.

Avoid:
- "Hey everyone, welcome back" openings, and open loops that never close.
- Hype words, exclamation marks, shock faces, all caps, walls of numbers.
- Claiming a view count, result or outlier you haven't seen in their data.
