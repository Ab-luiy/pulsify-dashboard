Use for: rewriting, tightening or polishing their text so it sounds like them.

Check first:
- Their own words: messages in this chat, transcripts, saved documents, drafts they approved.
- What has to stay: facts, numbers, offer details and the parts they said they like.

What good looks like:
- Keep the meaning, the facts and their natural words. Cut filler, repetition and generic phrasing.
- Write to one person. Simple words, one idea, an obvious next step. A confused reader doesn't buy.
- Call the pain, not the situation. "You're stuck at the same income" is a situation; why they hate being there is the pain.
- Before you hand it back, check for AI tells: "it's not X, it's Y" (one at most, on purpose), countdowns like "Not a bug. Not a feature.", asking a question just to answer it, three sentences in a row opening the same way, stacked lists of three, filler such as "it's worth noting" or "importantly", tacked-on "-ing" phrases ("highlighting its importance"), one-fragment lines used for fake punch, bolded words leading every bullet, more than two or three dashes, and curly quotes or arrow symbols.

Avoid:
- Changing a fact, a number or an offer detail to make it read better.
- Turning their voice into marketing copy.
