Use for: writing, planning or fixing an Instagram story sequence, a start-here highlight, or deciding what to put in stories.

Check first:
- What they want to talk about and what this sequence has to do: build trust, teach, answer questions or sell. Ask it in plain words. Never show them a list of sequence type names.
- Their real proof (saved proof items, client results, numbers they gave you) and whether they have permission to show it.
- Their offer and who it is for, so the close points at the right next step.
- Their story numbers if you have them (drop-off by slide, replies) and what they posted recently, so you don't repeat it.

What good looks like:
- Every slide earns the next tap. Each slide adds something to the one before it. If a slide can be skipped, cut it. People won't sit through 14 slides.
- The six steps under every sequence: curiosity (a pattern interrupt), the obstacle (name the pain, not the situation), a statement backed by proof straight away, one belief shift, the result, and a close made like someone who doesn't need the sale.
- A daily frame that works: slides 1 to 3 hook and give value or proof, 4 to 6 mirror their pain and show the gap, 7 to 9 offer access and the call to action. Nothing salesy before slide 7.
- Where the ask goes depends on the goal. For reach with a free lead magnet, the ask can sit on slide 1 before people drop off. To qualify buyers, put it after slide 7, because whoever taps there cares.
- Kinds of sequence, for your thinking only: value, credibility (an education slide first, then results that prove it), Q&A, day in the life (every moment carries value, a lesson or proof), breakdown (lead with what they walk away with, old way against new way), lifestyle (humanity, never possessions) and a selling sequence (rare, because asking too often burns the audience). Credibility does most of the work. Q&A builds relatability.
- Write like a person pouring their heart out, never a narrator. Short lines, their words.
- Copy first, design second. Everyday sequences stay simple: written copy on real photos. Heavy design is for big moments such as an offer launch, a VSL or a webinar.
- Current standard: story sequences twice a week. It is a default and can change per client.
- A start-here highlight is the permanent first highlight: their story, client proof, the framework, then the close.

Avoid:
- Inventing a result, a number or a client to fill a credibility slide. Leave a visible gap such as [add your real result].
- Lifestyle flexing (cars, watches, piles of money) as the hook or the proof.
- Copying a winning sequence from another niche. The structure transfers, the words don't.
- A call to action on every sequence.
