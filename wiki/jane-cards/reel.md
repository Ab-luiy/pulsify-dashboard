Use for: reel ideas, hooks, short scripts, which kind of reel to make, and why reels get views but no sales.

Check first:
- Their stage: how much proof they have, follower count and revenue if they told you.
- Their niche, buyer and content pillars, so the topic speaks to the person who buys.
- Their recent reels and numbers if you can read them.
- What is on their mind right now: a win, a strategy, an objection they just handled, a mistake. That is the raw material.

What good looks like:
- One job per reel. A reel reaches strangers or warms followers. It does not close. Selling happens in stories, DMs and long form, so never judge a reel by sales.
- The topic beats hook tricks. A weak topic sinks a good hook. Keep topics in their niche; a reach reel can open wider, but it still has to speak to their buyer.
- The first sentence is the hook and stands alone: an outcome, something specific, something fast.
- Raw beats polished. Reels are where the creator is natural. When they ask for a script, write the hook out, give the body as a few beats they can say in their own words, and finish on one line that asks for one action. Never a polished monologue.
- Ideas come from their head and their work: a win, a new strategy, an old one shown a new way, a client question, a clip from a long video or a sales call. One idea per reel. If the point needs two sentences, it is two reels.
- Which reel fits which stage: starting out, client-proof reels (low reach, high buyer intent); with some results, milestone reels (retire the numbers once proof exists); a system shown on a screen or board (high intent while the format is fresh); raw talking head only for real strategy once they are credible; contrarian reach reels when they lead the niche, followed by behind-the-scenes conversion content to harvest the attention.
- At most about three recurring formats at a time. Formats wear out in 6 to 12 months, so plan to refresh them.
- Repeat the core message past their own boredom. Most of the audience hasn't heard it once.
- Current standard: 2 to 5 reels a day, mostly cut from long videos and calls. A default that can change per client.

Avoid:
- Broad motivation or daily-routine content. It can go viral and sells nothing.
- Trends as the strategy. Trends are rented attention.
- Revenue numbers paired with lifestyle.
- Inventing a result or a statistic for the hook.
