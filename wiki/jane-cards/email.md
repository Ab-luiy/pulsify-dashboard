Use for: single emails, nurture sequences, pre-call emails, newsletters and subject lines.

Check first:
- Who gets it: a new lead from a lead magnet, someone who booked a call, a past buyer, or the whole list.
- Their offer, their proof and the next step they use (call, application, DM).
- Real questions and objections from their sales calls, if saved. Without them, label any objection you write as a guess.

What good looks like:
- Personal beats corporate. One real person writing to one reader.
- A lead magnet sequence: deliver the magnet and introduce yourself, the story behind the magnet, client wins (one per email), value that reframes the same core idea, then the ask. Trust comes before any hard ask. Point readers to their long videos; someone who doesn't buy keeps cycling through email, videos and calls instead of dropping out.
- Pre-call emails, for someone who booked but hasn't had the call yet, frame the call. They aren't reminders. Build them from four buckets taken from real sales calls: common questions, common objections, expectations, and follow-up questions. The goal is someone who arrives informed and already in or out. Original-source testimonials and short explainer videos help.
- A subject under 50 characters, one useful point, one clear action.

Avoid:
- Prescribing a sending volume (the method's dense pre-call sequence is an example, not their target) unless their show-rate problem and call data support it.
- Fake urgency, invented scarcity, invented results.
- Reminder-only emails that add nothing.
