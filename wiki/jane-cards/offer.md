Use for: shaping or fixing an offer, pricing, the unique mechanism, positioning, standing out, and answering objections to the offer.

Check first:
- Their saved offer: the promise, price, delivery, who it is for and what is included. Never change these facts; suggest changes as suggestions.
- Who they help and the buyer's stage (what they earn, what they've tried).
- Their proof, and their call outcomes or close rate if you can read them.
- What their buyers said about why they bought or hesitated.

What good looks like:
- An offer is a named result for a specific person, through a specific method, at a price. Its parts: the buyer (specific, with a stage and a problem), the mechanism, the result (measurable, in the buyer's words, achievable in the timeframe), the delivery (1-on-1, group or a course with a community), the price (set by what the buyer can invest and what the result is worth, not by what feels comfortable) and a risk reversal with no income guarantee.
- The one-sentence test: "I help [who] get [result] using [mechanism] through [delivery] at [price]." If it can't be written cleanly, the offer isn't finished.
- High-ticket fits buyers who already earn and are investing to scale. A lower price fits early-stage buyers and needs a clear upgrade path.
- A unique mechanism is named, logical, credible and different. Find it in what they already do that gets students results, then name it. It is observed, never invented.
- Position around a belief, not a service. Be a category of one. Pick one reframe and repeat it until the market links it to them: find the belief the buyer holds, reframe it so the old belief can't hold, and let the conclusion point to the offer.
- Any revenue figure in copy attracts people earning about a third of it. Numbers help early and get retired as proof grows.
- An objection heard three or more times is a marketing problem. Answer it with content before the call.
- Scale the price when demand beats supply (a full calendar, a stable close rate, documented results). New leads get the new price first and existing clients keep theirs. Watch whether the close rate drops.
- This market distrusts lifestyle proof and income screenshots without context. Showing the method and real student results works.
- Sell what they want, deliver what they need.

Avoid:
- Income guarantees, invented scarcity, invented deadlines.
- Generic promises such as "scale to 7 figures" with no mechanism behind them.
- Treating method benchmarks or example prices as advice for their exact price without their numbers.
