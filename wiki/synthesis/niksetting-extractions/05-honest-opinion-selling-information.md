# Niksetting 05 — "my honest opinion around selling information" — Claude extraction

The industry-thesis video: the three leverage types (unique / time / audience), the backtrackable-history proof doctrine, what changes daily (offer, delivery) vs what runs 3-6 months (acquisition, content), and the decade view — identities and communities replace information. His clearest statement of where the coaching industry goes.

# SOURCE INVENTORY

- **Title:** "my honest opinion around selling information..." — 2,817 words, raw sit-down, unusually reflective; recorded in Cape Town, South Africa.
- **One-line topic:** Where info-selling heads over 10 years and which of three leverage types to build on.

# CORE CLAIMS

**Claim 1 — Credentials, updated.**
Quote: "I sold $5 million, a little bit more, 5.2 M cash myself within this industry and I made my clients over $120 million."
[Client figure now $120M vs $85M in transcript 17 vs $90M in an 07 story slide — climbing across videos.]

**Claim 2 — 90/10 market concentration, zero-sum framing again.**
Quote: "90% of the market is fighting for 10% of the revenue, while... the top 10%... makes 90% of the revenue and takes 90% of the market because we don't make money. We all take money from each other and as harsh as it sounds, it's the reality."

**Claim 3 — Industry leaders have one of three leverage types.**
Quote: "it are either people that have a very unique brand, it are either people that have built their audience for a period of over 10 years. They had the time leverage instead of the audience leverage or the third component is they are unique in their own way... So, you can either have unique type of leverage, time type of leverage or audience type of leverage with the quality of the audience that you've built."
Routing: track record/proof -> time leverage; existing 100K+ audience -> audience leverage ("only five people watching this video will have that audience over 100,000 followers that actually matter"); everyone else -> uniqueness leverage.

**Claim 4 — The backtrackable history is the primary proof; testimonials are secondary.**
Quote: "The biggest proof is your own history. It's not the amount of clients that you've helped. It's not about amount of money that you've made." / "you can go back to my first YouTube video and you can backtrack every single step that I've taken... and you can see that every single word that comes out of my [ __ ] mouth isn't a lie... that is true proof. It's the ability for people to relate with a situation that they desire themselves. After that, all the testimonials come, all the client results come... That is the secondary importance of actual proof."
[= transcript 03's unfakeable-archive claim, generalized.]

**Claim 5 — Uniqueness applies to one of the three business components.**
Quote: "every time you either build on one of the most three important components of business, this is the offer that you have[/]who you're selling to, the traffic that you... attract, how you're finding them, and the content that you create on how you're converting them. Every single piece of content... every single acquisition source... every single offer... if you can showcase something that is unique related to the way you present yourself to the market within one of these three components, you will win."
Uniqueness surfaces listed: "the way you dress yourself, in the values that you share, in the actions that you take... your communication style... your vocabulary... the people that you surround yourself with... the level of access that you have, in the way you create your content formats."
[His offer/traffic/content triad ≈ Pulsify's content -> offer -> funnel.]

**Claim 6 — Easiest-to-understand beats best (offer edition).**
Quote: "it's not about who is the best. It's about who is the easiest to understand... don't think about how can I make my offer the best. How can I make my offer the easiest to understand and how can I be different in my offer compared to other people? Meaning, the actual tangible deliverables don't matter because it's not about the delivery, it's about the outcome that you sell people within your offer and the proof that you show around it."
[= 12's bio rule and 15's vision-over-deliverables, now applied to offers.]

**Claim 7 — The curiosity chain.**
Quote: "being able to be different is being able to create curiosity in people's mind. That's very simple. Curiosity creates attraction. Attraction creates loyalty. Loyalty creates influence. Keep this in mind. And influence is everything."

**Claim 8 — Offer/delivery iterate daily; acquisition/content run in 3-6 month structures.**
Quote: "Since the moment I've started, we have improved our offer over 100 times... Every single day we're improving our delivery... Your offer, your delivery changes all the [ __ ] time. Your acquisition and your content has one sustainable structure that runs for 3 to 6 months and then you improve it and you reinvent it. Just like my Myro videos, just like my new philosophy style video, the one that I'm making right now."
Recurrence: stated three times in this transcript alone.
Restated: Two clocks — daily refinement (offer, delivery) and quarterly reinvention (acquisition, content formats). [Consistent with 01's launch windows and 10's saturation rule.]

**Claim 9 — Give everything away; withholding is the failure mode.**
Quote: "Every single time I am feeling or thinking something, I am putting it out there in any type of content format... You have so much value to share to other people, but you're keeping it for yourself because you're only willing to give it away the moment people want to actually give you something back for it. And I've made so much content without seeing any single [ __ ] return for so long that ultimately God will give you the money back in any way."
Sales conviction: "I believe with the bottom of my heart that I do people a disservice not selling them the solution that I offer them."

**Claim 10 — The decade shift: identities, communities, access — not information.**
Quote: "this coaching industry has become so vacant... so extremely blurred... so extremely rational. And this moment in life, 2026, this slowly starts to change. People start to buy from identities and forget the offers. People start to buy into communities, into accountability and access. They stop buying into information. People start to truly follow leaders and leaders are people that can share their feelings, their thoughts, their beliefs their systems, their offers in the most unique way possible."
[The sharpest version of the trust-economy thesis; matches Pulsify's information-is-not-the-bottleneck axiom exactly.]

**Claim 11 — Think in decades; human touch is the terminal moat.**
Quote: "Stop thinking in minutes, days or weeks or months. Start thinking in decades. What is important in decades from now? Human touch is the only thing that matters. AI, Wi-Fi, internet... will all remove the most important components from people's lives. That's efficiency, time... You can get everything, every type of content format within under 2 minutes, within 5 years from now. You probably within 2 months from now as well with AI. So what does make you unique?... your human touch into building something around who you are, with your values and your experience and that's the only thing that matters."
[Third AI-era stance (19's lesson 33, 20's intention-arbitrage) — always the same answer: humanity is the moat.]

**Claim 12 — Share the emotional interior, not just the numbers (the Jade doctrine).**
Quote: "we always show nice [ __ ] in the front end within this info game... Everything always looks amazing. But true impact is being made if you can change the way people feel... And the most beautiful fact is when you start sharing that, you start winning because you're unique. You're not sharing just the numbers."

# FRAMEWORKS AND STEP-BY-STEPS

**Leverage-type selection:**
1. Deep track record -> time leverage (backtrackable proof).
2. 100K+ quality audience -> audience leverage ("How do I optimize my ability to understand conversion").
3. Everyone else -> uniqueness leverage, applied to offer, traffic, or content.

**The two-clock operating rhythm:** daily (offer, delivery) vs 3-6 months (acquisition systems, content formats).

**The curiosity chain:** different -> curiosity -> attraction -> loyalty -> influence.

# BELIEFS AND CONTRARIAN TAKES

- Deliverables don't matter; outcomes and proof do.
- Information's price is trending to zero; access/community/identity is the product.
- Anti-withholding: free value at scale precedes any return ("God will give you the money back").
- Selling as moral duty ("a disservice not selling them the solution").
- Openly disclaims guru status ("I'm definitely not like the person who made the most amount of money selling information... I'm not an OG").
