# Niksetting 01 — "the step-by-step process behind creating new viral formats" — Claude extraction

A live team/mastermind call teaching the full format-invention pipeline: mental state -> cross-industry research -> verbal-then-visual strategy -> V1/V2/V3 versioning -> launch -> innovation -> team production -> data. The five roles (creative, strategist, developer, innovator, analyzer) structure the whole thing. This is the meta-process that produced the Miro reels and every subsequent format.

# SOURCE INVENTORY

- **Title:** "the step-by-step process behind creating new viral formats" — 7,347 words, live call with chat interaction (Nico, Rodrigo, Angela addressed; Tony, Max, Wasim in the production walkthrough).
- **One-line topic:** How to invent, test, launch, and iterate a proprietary content format, and the team system that produces it at speed.

**Claim 1 — Format-building has distinct stages; confusion comes from not knowing them.**
Quote: "Now there are several stages to building a very profitable content format... I want you to understand these stages because if you don't, you might confuse yourself along the way."

**Claim 2 — Ideation requires a clear mind; the ego blocks the return to research.**

Brain taxonomy: "every human's brain exists out of valuable information and just vague information... vague information is normally information that doesn't make any point."

**Claim 4 — Self-reflection beats buying more information; dopamine misdirects.**

**Claim 6 — Decisions carry weight; small decisions spawn trap-cycles.**

**Claim 8 — Proven + authentic must combine.**

**Claim 9 — Verbal before visual: 75% of clarity.**
Quote: "when you're uh verbally aware about the things that you want to say, you give yourself 75% of the clarity already based on how you want to present it. [Direct match to Pulsify's packaging-first doctrine — title/thumbnail before production.]

**Claim 10 — Optimize the format for attention AND conversion; same message, different delivery.**
Quote: "We're inventing a new content format here... We want it to bring us attention. New attention. New eyeballs... And then we want to position it the right way where we put in certain lines for... conversion as well." / "If I would talk about one concept in my YouTube video in a very calm way in the 33rd minute or I would scream about it in a one minute reel. The two questions: "How do I get people's attention? I get people's attention by doing X while I'm saying why [Y]. How do I get people to convert? I get people to convert by talking about X specifically showing why [Y]."

**Claim 11 — Don't invent new topics for a new format.**

# FRAMEWORKS AND STEP-BY-STEPS

**The full format-invention pipeline:**

**Stage 1 — State/ideation prep:** clear brain via personal meditation form (walks, journaling, stillness, prayer — audience answers gathered live); "I practice my mental state to get myself in the right state to get these ideas... it all starts with your state."

**Stage 2 — Cross-industry research (4 steps):**
1. Pick an industry (clothing, software, ecom).
2. Identify its core driver/lane: "Clothing is all about creativity"; software = technical; ecom = distribution.
3. Segment into two buckets: "three of the biggest [X] companies... And I have three of the most likable brands that I like that fit with who I am" (proven + authentic; e.g., Zara vs Nude Project).
4. Research each of the six across "their Instagram, their LinkedIn, their website" — "you scroll on each for 15 minutes times six."

**Stage 3 — Pattern recognition (2 parts):**
1. Gathering: "visual screenshots that you can add to a myro [Miro] mood board... save videos. You can save text styles... look at a shape... rounded... square."
2. Spotting: "you'll see that this one most likely fits with this one... You will see patterns. You will see similar companies using a similar style of creativity."

**Stage 4 — Strategy (verbal first, then visual):**
- Verbal: answer the two questions (attention: "What is something that I say that will make people listen to me?"; conversion: "What we need to talk about to make people pay us money").
- Visual: return to the mood board — "it becomes very easy to package these videos, visuals, text, and shapes into a new format that is desirable."

**Stage 5 — Development (the puzzle + versioning):**
- "You just connect them. It's very easy... you now are the software developer of your own format."
- V1 -> 24h full pause -> V2 -> 24h -> V3. "V3 is the first touch point where we actually start to distribute... Format V1 test V2 test V3 and when we have done the third test internally within... a 3day cycle we actually do the launch. Okay, you can call it a beta launch."

**Stage 6 — Launch:** 1-6 months; 3-month saturation threshold; success gates V2.

**Stage 7 — Innovation (second launch):** adjust, don't reinvent; "a product is only good if a product innovates."

**Stage 8 — Production process (the team moat):**
- Monday planning call: per-person tasks with realistic hour estimates, agreed live (Tony: "Four hours of editing, two hours uh four hours of downloading, two hours of editing" -> done in a day: "one final 2hour and 17 minute mastermind video... three small YouTube videos... for the Mr. Nick setting channel... four Instagram reels").
- Pipeline: Tony (edit) -> Wasim (wrap) -> "Send it to Nick for review. I'll review quickly. WhatsApp. I say change this, this, this. Boom. Done."
- Dubai project benchmark: "800 gigabytes... one day download 4 hours of... cutting raw cuts. Uh seven different angles. Two hours visual animations... six hours... Most editors do that in a week."

**Stage 9 — Data/analysis (the analyzer role):** weekly gather-analyze-improve loop; a new format invented and added "in three months"; team grows ("You get another editor on board").

**The five roles:** "We have been a creative, we have been a strategist, we have been a developer and we have been an innovator... we are now analyzer." Pay framing: "We get paid 500k a year. You know we go from 100, 200, 300, 400, 500" (illustrative escalation per role).
