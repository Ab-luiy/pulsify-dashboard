# CORE CLAIMS

**Claim 1 — People fail at story sequences for three reasons: no understanding, no design skill, no data.**
Quote: "People don't fail with this because they are too lazy to do it. First of all, they don't understand how to do it. They don't understand how to effectively design each of these story sequences. And the third one is they don't have data. So they don't understand what to do accordingly to improve it."
Restated: The blockers are system-level (know-how, design, measurement), not effort.

**Claim 2 — The founder storyline is the deepest follow hook.**
Quote: "The reason this works so well is because people love to follow a storyline of a founder... You would love to watch to know and to figure out what I'm doing on a daily basis for you to have the ability to replicate that process."
Restated: Day-in-the-life sequences work because the audience wants to replicate the founder's daily process.

**Claim 3 — A documented day must carry three ingredient types, not just documentation.**
Quote: "Now what I'm doing here is not just explaining what I'm doing on a daily basis. I actually add three things. Value based content, educationalbased content, and proof."
Restated: Day-in-the-life is a delivery vehicle; value, education, and proof are the payload.

**Claim 4 — Views and conversions trade off by sequence design.**
Quote (evergreen photo version): "This is more evergreen. This will get you more views but it will get you less conversions. That's very important to understand."
Quote (timeline version): "You will decrease story views. I'm going to be honest, I'll tell you right now. But you'll have the possibility to convert a lot more because it works so [ __ ] well."
Recurrence: 2 — stated once per direction of the tradeoff.
Restated: Simple/passive sequences maximize views; deep value-chained sequences sacrifice views for buyers. Pick per goal.

**Claim 5 — Timeline sequences capture the viewer mentally.**
Quote: "People are tapped in. They're in a flow state. You have them mentally. You just have them by their [ __ ] head mentally. You see what I mean? Meaning, they're extremely adaptable because you have influence over them."
Restated: Story-to-story value chaining creates a flow state that makes the CTA land.

**Claim 6 — Q&A creates more relatability than any other story type.**
Quote: "Every time you do this, you touch a very, very important part of the brain, your prospects and audience mind, because you create so much relatability. This adds 10 times more relatability than any other [ __ ] story ever will."
Restated: Q&A is the relatability engine; run it at least biweekly.

**Claim 7 — Education slides prime the audience to associate the client results that follow.**
Quote: "I leverage these numbers to associate the next stories of results with these outcomes. So when you read this and then you'll see a client result, you relate that client result with this outcome and with this system and with this process, you see how smart that is."
Restated: Sequence order is engineered association: the education slide's claims get "proven" by the results that follow it.

**Claim 8 — Unstructured days are themselves a framework, and they build trust.**

# FRAMEWORKS AND STEP-BY-STEPS

**The three requirements (closing summary):**
Quote: "You need a system. You need [ __ ] seven different frameworks and you need data to be tracked for you to have an understanding and then ultimately these reports give you the right data to make the right decision."

**Framework 1 — Founder day-in-the-life (timestamped documentation + value/education/proof)**
- Structure: timestamped slides across a real day — "10:00 a.m. I started my morning recording a testimonial with my client Jade... then 12:00 p.m. 2 p.m. 4 pm 4:30 8:30 9".
- Each slot carries one of the three ingredients: value, education, or proof ("Proof that here at 4:30 I got a lot of client results. And here I was also interacting with a client right later on the day at 8:30. Here I was talking about fulfillment.").
- Simplified variant: "Just make one like this where you just post the picture and you add the time to the picture meaning people can follow your day." — more evergreen, more views, fewer conversions.

**Framework 2 — Timeline-based sequences (the conversion weapon)**
- Rule: "Every next story adds value to the prior story where it becomes a timeline, right? A structure of progress."
- Worked example (with Brian): "Step one, step two, step three, step four, step five, step six. And you know what? You don't have this system yet. So, here is the exact system that we use for conversion. Then this happened, that happened, and ultimately you can add a CTA."
- Mandate: "If you want to make money and you want to leverage a CTA for inbounds, you must leverage such a story sequence... you will get an insane amount of buyers and actual conversions in the DMs."
- Cost: lower story views, accepted deliberately.

**Framework 3 — Q&A**
- Cadence: "every two weeks, at least once you do a Q&A."
- Content: "just how do you feel? What is going on in your life? How how is business doing? What is going on in business?"
- Function: 10x relatability.

**Framework 4 — Client result stacking**
- Fixed order: "I post one lifestylebased um story then I post one educationalbased story and then I post three or four client results."
- Slide 1 (lifestyle hook): "The first one is imagine you wake up to this. The reason I'm doing this is to catch an insane amount of views first." — lifts views (example given: 20,000 -> 30,000).
- Slide 2 (education): warms up, filters "the wrong and the right people," and creates the association anchor for the results.
- Slides 3-5/6: three or four client results, sourced from community shares.

**Framework 5 — The no-idea day (niche important topics)**
- Trigger: "the day you don't know [ __ ] about what to post."
- Method: "I take five to 10 niche important topics... Appointment setting is a niche important topic. Sales is a niche important topic. Uh acquisition, right? Marketing specifically when it comes story sequences is one. So I do that and I transcribe these into a message."
- Worked example: "I just took six pictures and one I talked about my day 30k a day 90 million in client results this that one I talked about a system right and ads. One I talked about content and making a 12-hour uh free course. One I talked about meditation."
- Deliberately structureless; the personalization is the point.

**Framework 6 — Story launch (the money sequence)**
- Definition: "A story launch is where you put up a it's technically a short webinar for your stories to have the ability to sell something."
- Proof: "we made over $600,000 ourselves purely from story launches" / "This specific story sequence you see here brought us in over 250 grand from only our stories."
- Shown example: ~9 stories, 71.9% completion rate ("which is very high for a sequence of around nine se uh nine stories").
- Warning: launches are built on accumulated per-account data, not copied ("the only reason we made this is because we knew exactly what worked for us at the time we posted this").

**Framework 7 — Lifestyle**
- Function: "create um personal bonding with your audience. See, make sure that your audience knows what you're up to."
- Governed by the 80/20 split: lifestyle lives in the 20% human share.

**The data system (the layer under all seven):**
- Tracked per daily sequence: "the total amount of views... the percentage of completion... the drop off rate... the amount of DMs and interactions... the profile and story views from it... the compared performance."
- Copy intelligence: "it capitalizes on the copy. So AI technically the system capitalizes on all the copy that we have actively here for us to have the possibility to see what works and what doesn't. It will give us a full report."
- Cadence: a full sequence posted every day, founder-independent.
