The cadence doc: which surface does which job (reels nurture, stories close, CTAs convert, collabs expose) and how often each fires. Short video, dense on frequencies — the direct input for Pulsify's posting-frequency hypotheses in [[playbooks/instagram-growth-workstream]].

# CORE CLAIMS

**Claim 1 — The schedule's attributed results.**
Quote: "This is the posting schedule that makes me $300,000 a month and made my clients over $85 million in the last three years. And to give you a little bit of reference, I work with one of the biggest clients. I partner up with four clients that make over $2 million a month. And I have 85 clients that make anywhere between 10 up to $200,000 a month."

**Claim 2 — Reels are for nurturing, not converting; people use them for the wrong job.**
Quote: "the biggest problem that people have with reels is they use it for the wrong thing. Reals are purely optimized for nurturing." / "reels are purely optimized to nurture prospects and not to convert them."
Recurrence: 2 in this short video.
Restated: Reels warm existing attention; conversion happens elsewhere. [Note: nuances transcript 02/10, where reels are traffic tools — see CONTRADICTIONS.]

**Claim 3 — Never let volume outrun quality.**
Quote: "If you cannot keep up with posting once a day, that's completely chill. Don't overpower volume if the quality isn't right. You have to keep that in mind because otherwise you'll do everything wrong."

**Claim 4 — Visual proof must match verbal claims.**
Quote: "You've probably seen my Instagram pictures and you look at them and you're like, Nick is living a top quality life. What he visually shows makes lots of sense with what he verbally is saying. Clear?"
Restated: The verbal-visual alignment rule (the masterclass's most-repeated idea) applied to static posts: the grid's job is congruence between shown life and claimed results.

**Claim 5 — Stories deliver the last 10% that turns a potential buyer into a buyer.**
Quote: "I post it every single day. And the reason why this is extremely important is because it gives the last 10% to a potential buyer to become a [ __ ] buyer, to actually hand over their credit card to pay you."
Restated: Stories are the closing surface. (Consistent with transcript 07.)

**Claim 6 — You can always ask, provided you give more than you ask.**
Quote: "people always say you cannot ask for something. That's wrong. As long as you give enough, you can ask for something. If you give 100% and you ask 50%. You get it, right? If I tell you, yo, you know what? I'll give you $2. Just give me one back. You'll accept it. But if I ask you $2 and I'm only giving you one back, you'll probably hesitate."
Restated: CTA permission is earned by value surplus; the give/ask ratio governs CTA volume.

**Claim 7 — CTA density is a brand-positioning decision.**
Quote: "Depends on if your brand is optimized for quality, you do less CTAs. If your brand is optimized for volume, you do lots more CTAs. Now, my brand is optimized for quality."
Restated: Fewer CTAs signal a premium brand; CTA frequency is strategy, not habit.

**Claim 8 — Collab posts buy another audience instantly.**
Quote: "When you collab with someone, you instantly attract and interact with their whole audience. Meaning you create maximum exposure within a matter of minutes."

# FRAMEWORKS AND STEP-BY-STEPS

**The posting schedule ("seven different steps" — six delivered; he numbers them in descending priority):**

**Reels** — job: nurture. Frequency: "something you want to post once a day" (quality permitting).

**Normal posts** — job: status/credibility. Two modes: (1) "purely to gain status and credibility" (the congruent-lifestyle grid photo); (2) "you can add text specifically to one post, mostly with one CTA if it's just one singular post."

**Carousels ("Number five")** — two modes:
1. Life update: "let's say I've been for a week to Dubai. I post only pictures of me being in Dubai having fun... I capture that moment specifically... for people to experience."
2. Storyline/storytelling: "you can say one headline and then six different steps or one headline, five different steps, one CTA. Right? This is how I made $4 million as an online consultant. Step one, step two, step three, step four, step five, and then a CTA. DM or comment the word consultant and I'll send you over a private asset, a 60-minute asset to showcase how you can do the exact same thing."

**Stories ("Number four", "one of my favorites")** — job: the last 10% / closing. Frequency: "every single day", "3 to seven a day". The four story types: "valuebased, transformational, educational, and lifestyle. That is what you want to post accordingly every single day in a following format."

**CTA ("number three")** — job: "it actually gets you buyers." His quality-brand setup: "I'm doing one soft CTA to a lead magnet, which then leads to a booked call. And I do one hard CTA, DMing the word X, quick conversation with the setter, and I get them ultimately into my calendarly [Calendly]. And that's the reason why we book lots of calls."

**Collab posts ("Number one... probably one of the most important ones")** — job: maximum exposure. Example: the podcast reel — "it got 1 million views as well as that specific video is the second best podcast after Iman Gadi [Gadzhi] that is viewed... Mine was number two. It got 26,000 views. And you have to understand for a 1 and a half hour podcast, this is ridiculous." Plus: "I had a very good structure for me that I've prepared before to get on a podcast to maximize the potential of it."
