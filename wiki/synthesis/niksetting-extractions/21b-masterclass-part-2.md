# CORE CLAIMS (deltas only)

**Claim 1 — Reels build the audience; story sequences close them.**
Quote: "the left side posting reels builds the audience. The right side story sequences closes them."
Audience member's confirmation (the invisible-buyer effect live): "I probably viewed five to seven of your story sequences. And then I was like, 'All right, I'm ready.' You know, without liking them, without sending you a message."
[The cleanest one-line platform-role statement, and live proof of 20's invisible traffic.]

**Claim 2 — Story sequences are built for credibility, not views; consistency is unfakeable.**
Quote: "we don't build story sequences for views. We build it for our credibility. The biggest form of credibility is the understanding of knowing that people can backtrack my success. Some people like me, some people hate me... That doesn't matter. The only thing that they know for sure for a thousand% is that I've been consistent. I've been posting for three years. I show shared everything of my journey. That is more valuable than any of the client results I have with all of you." / "otherwise I wouldn't be able to fill up a room with 20 people here... everybody flew out[.] it's like impossible right like how [would] I want to fake that."
[= 03/05's archive doctrine, now applied specifically to stories.]

**Claim 3 — The payers are the daily-story watchers, not the reel-stumblers.**
Quote: "the people who pay you are not the people... who once stumbled upon your reel[.] it are the people who can[not] wait to see your daily story sequence every single time. They just won't express it. They just won't share it."

**Claim 4 — The last-10-posts audit.**
Quote: "Open your last 10 post[s]... For each one, what was that post[:] to get a follower or to convert into a buyer?... if you don't know an answer to the question, that's the [ __ ] problem." Plus peer review: "go sit down, go through each other's reels and ask, 'What is my perception on this?' because everybody here in the room has actual real value to share. Your... 167,000 follower[s] doesn't give a [ __ ]"
[A ready-made diagnostic exercise for client onboarding — every post must declare its job.]

**Claim 5 — The five story-sequence types (masterclass version), type 1: Q&A.**
Quote: "Now, there are five types. Let me walk you through each one of them. Q&A..."
- Cold-start trick: "when you do a Q&A story sequence and you don't get enough questions, you can ask yourself questions on the story sequence. You can answer them... When I started, I didn't have any views on my story. I did the same thing... I could just figure out what my pain points were." Rule: "if you don't get enough questions[,] write your own[.] nobody can see the [difference]."
- Mechanics: "You open a question... You let your audience ask anything. And then... 12 hours later, you answer the question your I[C]P would actually care about."
- Psychology: "Questions are how the audience invites you to perform your expertise. They're not asking to be educated. They're asking to see like how you think... what is your desk setup? Like how do you wake up in the morning?" / "every answer is a free consult."
- Balance rule (answering an AI question from the room): "let's say I have eight questions... four are technical and four are personalized... technical... it's more systematized, something that somebody else can do... it maintains the consistency."
- Proof: "He runs a sales coaching offer. His Q&A questions pull more DMs than his reals do because it's that personal touch that gives people that last 20%."
[Q&A cadence in 07 was biweekly; here it's one of five rotating types. The "last 20%" here vs stories' "last 10%" in 17.]

**Claim 6 — Stories are the safe playing field; reels/feed are not.**
Quote: "content works in their favor, but it also works in reverse. When you share the wrong things, sometimes you'll [ __ ] it up for yourself... It deludes [dilutes] the brand. But with stories, it's all like it's like an open playing field. You cannot really do wrong things."
[Pairs with 16's content-works-against-you; stories are exempted — the experimentation surface.]
