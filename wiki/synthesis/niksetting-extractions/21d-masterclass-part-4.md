# CORE CLAIMS (deltas)

**Claim 1 — Owned channels: email is the most valuable asset; WhatsApp beats everything on opens.**
Quote: "email list is the most valuable asset you'll ever [ __ ] build because it separates itself from all the other platforms." / "your data like your phone number. You don't get a phone number with Instagram... imagine like DMing someone on WhatsApp. Like it's like a 90% open rate, a 89% uh response rate."

**Claim 2 — Experience above value and destination.**
Quote: "two things matter. It's value and the end destination, but the thing that's even more important is the experience. It's like the thing, the small things do it... that personal touch that people get that makes them feel safe, that makes them feel invited... that is how they become part of something." (Welcome package in build: new logo, "a little pen and like a card" — the same pen from 08.)

**Claim 3 — Content assets: the one-minute reel redirects, the long form converts.**
Quote: "A one minute reel can[not] lose [close] anyone. There's not enough time to build trust. But a one minute reel can redirect the attention perfectly because it has an insane amount of comments. Like 4,000 people commenting. Okay, realistically, this included with Machi the half of it. So it's like... 2,25 [2,250?]... the people that actually commented and then we send another text with like okay cool... you got the lead magnet[,] send it to you."
[Machi appears here as part of the comment/DM automation pipeline — halves the raw comment count. Combined with 16's "$100K MRR software company," Machi is plausibly his automation/tracking product. Still unverified.]

**Claim 4 — Expectation-setting is the YouTube conversion variable.**
Quote: "the only thing that matters when it comes down to your YouTube video is that you position it well and that people get what they expected... Imagine you see a thumbnail on a YouTube video [and it] is completely different... than what you expected... That's expectation setting."
Warm-time math: "they already saw 15 minutes of my content so far. They saw my face like for a period of an hour, right? By clicking through everything... And that improves conversion." Efficiency: "I make a reel, I send people to my long form... they can watch a longer version of that exact same thing. Meaning, you don't have to make double content."

**Claim 5 — Right content raises questions; engagement manufactures FOMO.**
Quote: "the right content brings up questions in people... If you can make people think for themselves." / "if we have 7,000 views and we have 200 people on our YouTube video commenting... the more engagement you can create the better because it creates that scarcity[,] it creates FOMO."
Framework CTA: "a one-page PDF a checklist a script template[,] something they keep[,] something they refer back to all the time. Every time they open it, they think of you specifically. That's the asset." CTA choice rule: "Use the long form CTA when the topic is too deep for a real... Use the framework CTA when you have a specific deliverable they can save."

**Claim 6 — Two-brand separation: company page vs personal brand.**
Quote: "we have a secondary grow acquisition page... grow acquisition is uh a company[,] Nick setting is the brand so we separate each from each other... On grow acquisition, we just share value... but also everything related to proof and credibility. N[i]ck setting is where the education is being shared... People love me for me and people love the product for the transformation."
Sellability: "if they keep buying into me and I remove myself out of it... people don't want it anymore because they want it for me. But if I sell the system, the profile funnel that has proven to bring in millions... you can sell it if you want to at a certain point... That won't be a 20x as a software, but even if it's a 2x, it's like why not?"
[Strategic: the mechanism brand (Grow Acquisition + profile funnel) is built to be an asset separable from the person — the answer to personal-brand unsellability.]

**Claim 7 — Testimonial economics: intention when few, volume when many.**
Quote: "I can make a $5,000 payment look... more engaging... than a $50,000 transformation... It's like how you share it matters so much." / "If you have one result, you need to be intentional. The more results you get, the less intentional you can be because people project the value not on the testimonial... but the amount... you see a client testimonial channel, you see like, oh, 50 clients. You're like, ah, this... should be good. You don't even watch a testimonial... 95% of the people they don't actually watch your testimonials... They just see it and they believe it."
[Extends 12's testimonial-positioning claim with the phase-shift: intention -> volume as proof accumulates.]

**Claim 8 — The 4-part screenshot-proof structure.**
Quote: "A screenshot alone is just noise. A screenshot with the story is proof. The structure is four specific parts. Hook, so it's the result specifically. Then cont[ext], where they started and how they got into this journey. The mechanism is what they did change while they worked with you... Invitation. So who is this actually for?"
Indirect invitation rule: "I don't have to mention Mark's name for people to understand what he does... you go into the pain points and you mention their situation... You don't name the direct thing. You name the pain points that make people think that direct thing... that's the new age of marketing."
[= hook/context/mechanism/invitation. Slots directly into result-stacking (07) and credibility sequences (21c). The invitation rule is 16's indirect doctrine applied to proof.]

**Claim 9 — Posting cadence: minimum 3 sequences/week, target 5-7.**
Quote: "Minimum three sequences a week, right? All the time[.] you schedule it." / "So target is five to seven, right? Uh this is specifically with stories." / "which means one [a] day or close. It sounds like a lot until you realize that people will pass you posting a story every single hour... Not because they have more time, because they decided this is the work."
Self-audit: "If I scroll down your stories from this past week, how many of the five types would I find?... Most likely not a lot."
[RESOLVES the cadence contradiction flagged in 17: 3/week is the floor, 5-7/week the target, and these are SEQUENCES per week — 17's "3 to seven a day" is slides/stories per day. Both hold.]

**Claim 10 — The call-batching calendar (the "seven figure question").**
Quote: "If you schedule all your sales calls and client calls on only Monday full day[,] only Tuesday full day[,] 9 10 calls with one two breaks[,] you have Wednesday Thursday Friday Saturday and Sunday to think about the most important thing... it's been worth a [ __ ] seven figure question. I would have paid a million dollars to notice a year earlier."
Recovery logic: "let's say you take eight calls in a row. You're exhausted. It takes you an hour to calm down... if you only give yourself maximum a half day of time, you don't have that time to recover."
Cap: "only take max amount of 20 calls on these two days." Closer exception: "with closing... you minimize the potential of availability... you can just miss out on a $20,000 payment because they wouldn't be able to schedule in on Wednesday."
Cascade: "imagine I take my calls all over the place and I won't be able to make good content... my setter has less lead flow[,] Herman has less calls... it goes in stages."
Highest-leverage rule: "look at everybody in the company[,] just think what is the highest leverage activity each person can do."
States: "you have two... state[s] of mind[:]... survival state... It's very difficult to get into a strategic mind when you're constantly survivaling... when you make strategic decisions[,] that's where the [ __ ] needle is being moved."

**Claim 11 — Speed beats waiting; burnout comes from passionless drag, not hours.**
Quote (Elon Musk cited): "if you decide you can clean your house in 30 days you'll get it done in 30 days. But if you decide to get it done in 3 days you get it done in 3 days." / "Burning you out isn't working on something quick, doing that for 16 hours a day and being excited... I burn out the moment I do something that I'm not passionate about."

**Claim 12 — The system is the five assets running together for months.**
Quote: "none of these assets work alone at all. One story sequence isn't a system. One lead magnet isn't a system. The system is what happens when all five run on a schedule for months."

**Claim 13 — Compounding = eventually pointing in the right direction.**
Quote (workshop-ad splice, but doctrinally real): "It didn't happen because I did more reps. It happened because at some point I stopped doing the wrong reps. Three years of compounding doesn't mean three years of work. It means three years where eventually the work pointed in the right direction. Most founders spent two of these years... pointed in the wrong thing."

**Claim 14 — The two terminal variables: audience accuracy and desire.**
Quote: "the only difference between me and you guys... is the level of accuracy and how good you understand your audience. Because if you understand your audience, you can build the best course in the world... You can do everything. Literally everything. It's the only thing that matters. And then the second thing is your desire... what is your end goal, right? And like why is that your goal truly?... it always changes. And that's also okay."
