# Niksetting 12 — "how to create a perfect instagram bio that converts" — Claude extraction

The bio doctrine: 3-second decision window, broad-but-specific positioning, category-of-one framing, and the attract/repel filter test. Positioning theory disguised as a bio tutorial — half of this transcript is about uniqueness framing generally.

# SOURCE INVENTORY

- **Title:** "how to create a perfect instagram bio that converts" — 1,745 words, talking-head with his own bio reverse-engineered on screen.
- **One-line topic:** Why the bio is the profile's first conversion mechanism and how to write one that answers three questions in three seconds while filtering out non-buyers.

# CORE CLAIMS

**Claim 1 — Strangers decide in under 3 seconds.**
Quote: "When a stranger lands on your profile, their brain makes a decision in under 3 seconds if they either interact or ignore your bio completely. And this will be completely dependent on the amount of volume that you get every single month based on the lead flow that you're currently getting."
Recurrence: also quoted inside transcript 10's reel clip.
Restated: The bio gates all profile-level conversion; its quality scales monthly lead flow.

**Claim 2 — First impressions are 10x harder to change than to make.**
Quote: "it takes 10 times more [ __ ] effort to change your first impression than to make one."

**Claim 3 — Easiest to understand beats best.**
Quote: "It's not about having the best bio, it's about having the easiest to understand bio." / "You don't need to be the best. You just need to be the easiest to understand by knowing how to position your message."
Recurrence: 2 (opening and closing of the argument).
Restated: Clarity outranks cleverness; comprehension speed is the metric.

**Claim 4 — The bio is the only thing a new visitor converts on.**
Quote: "It's the only conversion mechanism that people see the moment they follow you. They see nothing else. They don't see your reals yet. They don't see your stories yet... Your bio is that razor thin slice of information."

**Claim 5 — Don't box yourself with a number or an identity callout.**
Quote: "I don't put myself into a box by either announcing a specific group of people, announcing a specific number to fall into the trap of niche selection... $10 million, $100 million, 100K a month, $10,000 a month. Or the second thing, entrepreneurs, business owners, freelancers... because that means that I'm dislecting [deselecting, sic] certain people... your bio needs to be broad while still letting that specific message that you want come across."
Restated: Stay broad on the who, specific on the what — naming revenue bands or job identities deselects buyers.

**Claim 6 — Generic titles get brain-skipped; unique naming creates curiosity.**
Quote: "social media strategist about me brain skips it because so many people are doing it. You need to name what you do so uniquely that nobody has ever seen it before... What is the thing that matters about [ __ ] bios?... It's [ __ ] curiosity... If you use the same words... I'm a social media strategist. I'm an influencer. I'm a business coach. People are tired of that [ __ ]."
Restated: The label for what you do must be a coinage, not a category.

**Claim 7 — A bio must attract and repel equally.**
Quote: "Both reactions are very intentional. Your bio should attract and repel equally." / "Your bio should either trigger this is exactly what I need or this is absolutely not for me. If it doesn't do one of these two things, then you are not specific enough."
Recurrence: 2.
Restated: Polarization is the design goal; neutrality is failure.

**Claim 8 — The lukewarm middle creates followers who never buy.**
Quote: "The fun fact is that the middle is the most dangerous. If people get to realize this is kind of interesting, but I don't really think this is interesting enough... That's the moment you create a follow. That's the moment nobody will ever intentionally feel like they're buying."
Restated: Mild interest produces dead followers; the bio must force a decision.

**Claim 9 — Proof must be backtrackable.**
Quote: "you want to leverage specific proof. So, as an example, I got featured on Forbes, meaning people can always [ __ ] backtrack my results... people can just go to my Forbes page... and they can check it out themselves. Then specific activates trust."
Restated: Verifiable proof only; the claim's power is that it can be independently checked.

**Claim 10 — Category of one: wrong words enter you into invisible competitions.**
Quote: "The reason you're invisible isn't your content. It's that you position yourself in a competition you didn't even know [ __ ] existed by using certain words in your bio... the most important thing is you want to be a category of one. If you're a category of one, you can't be compared with other people... If you use what other people use, you just enter a weight list [waitlist, sic]."
Restated: Word choice determines which comparison set the brain files you into; unique framing removes you from all of them.

**Claim 11 — The 5K-to-50K difference is positioning, not skill.**
Quote: "The difference between 5K and 50K is in the skill. It's positioning." [sic — corrects himself mid-sentence] / "It's not even about the fact of if I'm more skilled or you're more skilled. It's about the fact that I am able to position the word more uniquely for me to be perceived higher value than you are."

**Claim 12 — Testimonial positioning beats testimonial volume.**
Quote: "how is it possible that some people with two testimonials make more money than those with 50 testimonials? It's because it's not about the volume of testimonials. It's about how you position a testimonial... these two testimonials are super intentional that actually change a belief that change your perspective."
Restated: One belief-shifting testimonial outsells a stack of rushed ones.

**Claim 13 — The bio is a filter as much as a magnet.**
Quote: "Your bio isn't just mean to attract, it's mean to [ __ ] filter it out. You don't give a [ __ ] about an audience that technically is highly unqualified... If people outside your ICP are following you, the filter is absolutely broken."
Restated: Off-ICP followers are a diagnostic that the bio filter has failed.

**Claim 14 — Speaking to everyone connects with no one.**
Quote: "A brand that speaks to everyone connects with no one." / "Nobody feels chosen because the way you are positioning your words is not unique at all... The moment that thought ['just must be another guy like that'] is happening in people's brain, you've lost."

# FRAMEWORKS AND STEP-BY-STEPS

**His bio, reverse-engineered ("I help you build a brand selling your expertise"):**
1. "I help X" — states who you help, but deliberately unboxed (no number, no identity label).
2. Unique naming of the craft — "Selling your expertise. It's uniquely strategically positioned for me to have the availability to target everyone while still being very niched."
3. Backtrackable proof — the Forbes feature.

**The three levels of a bio:**
Quote: "There are three levels of your bio that are important. Replaceable. You want to make sure that the first level where you call out what you do and who you help, you are irreplaceable. The second level is respected. I built a system that did X. Or you can say something like, I'm the creator of why [Y]... I'm the creator of the profile funnel."
[Note: only two levels are actually delivered — irreplaceable (what/who, uniquely framed) and respected (creator-of/system-that-did proof). The third is never named. See GAPS.]

**The uniqueness test (ecom coach example):**
Quote: "if you say, I'm an ecom coach and I help you build an ecom business, how many other people can do that? At least [ __ ] 500 times x the amount of people that can do what I just did... It's because what I just told you is differently framed."

**The rewrite protocol (the 3-question stranger test):**
Quote: "Look at your current bio... as a complete stranger... Can they instantly answer in 3 seconds, what is in it for me? Is this person credible? What do I do next? If these three questions are [ __ ] answered, you're done... If the answer is no, rewrite it today."
