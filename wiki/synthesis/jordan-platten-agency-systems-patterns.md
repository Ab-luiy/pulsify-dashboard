# Jordan Platten agency systems patterns

This page extracts the useful patterns from Jordan Platten's 2025 agency interview. It is not a VSL analysis. It is strategic context for how Pulsify should think about trust, expectation setting, retention, agency operations, and stage maps.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Pulsify will lose trust if it sounds like every other operator promising a giant outcome from a shallow audit. Jordan's strongest contribution is the opposite: set the boundary, name what can and cannot be fixed, show the stage, then earn trust by taking the real problem seriously.

## Core mechanics

### Communication is the agency survival skill

The interview repeatedly returns to communication with clients and team. The claim is blunt: if an agency founder cannot communicate, the business breaks. This matters because Pulsify's cold outreach VSL should not be a monologue. It should show the prospect that Pulsify understands the real business relationship.

### Retention beats acquisition after momentum

Acquisition creates motion. Retention, referrals, and relationship quality create durability. That matters for Pulsify because content and funnels can generate more leads, but weak onboarding, unclear expectations, and bad delivery will leak the gains.

### Expectation setting increases trust

The strongest Jordan pattern is telling the buyer what will not happen. In a trust recession, bigger promises can make the buyer more skeptical. Clear limits can increase close rate because they feel honest.

For Pulsify, this means personalized VSLs should include one boundary:

- what Pulsify can fix
- what the prospect still owns
- what would make the prospect a bad fit

### Own more of the real problem

An agency that only delivers a narrow service is easier to churn. Jordan's pattern is to understand the client's wider problem: financials, margin, creative, sales, delivery, and next bottleneck. Pulsify's version is content, offer, funnel, backend, and sales infrastructure.

### Stage maps create authority

The interview supports a map-based selling motion: show the prospect the phase they are in, the phase they skipped, and the next phase. This is directly useful for [[playbooks/pulse-system-outreach]].

## How Pulsify uses this

In cold outreach:

- Do not overpromise.
- Diagnose the stage.
- Name one constraint.
- Show the map.
- State the buyer's responsibility.
- Offer the next diagnostic step.

On client delivery:

- Set expectations before the work starts.
- Make the buyer's role explicit.
- Use phases so the client knows why the current work matters.
- Tie retention to future stages, not random upsells.
