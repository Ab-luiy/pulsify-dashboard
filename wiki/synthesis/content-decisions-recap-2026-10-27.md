# What we decided about content (recap, 27 Oct 2026)

Pulled from every chat since June where you talked about content. Where you changed your mind, the newest decision is the one that stands, and the change is written next to it with both dates.

## The big picture

- Content is the engine. "Content is the mover of everything" (14 Jul). The app's selling point is selling through organic content and traffic (15 Oct).
- YouTube is the most important thing to get right (26 Sep). Instagram builds the audience, YouTube builds the depth, because long form is where people really connect (30 Jul, FITFO call).
- A creator's content is their craft itself: trading setups, winning products, how they made the money. Never content about content, funnels or selling a course.
- Position creators as a new face with a new idea, never as a guru (8 Oct). Transparency and consistency beat lifestyle flexing now (3 Oct).
- Speak to one specific audience about one specific pain, from many angles. Vague content pulls in the wrong crowd (3 Oct).
- Inconsistency kills channels. You fired two clients who went a month without posting (24 Jul).
- People always want new faces. The top of a niche stays with 2 to 4 people, but newcomers with new ideas keep breaking in every month (4 Oct).

## How much to post

Standard from 15 Oct. It's a product default and can be changed per client (26 Oct).

- 1 to 2 YouTube videos a week
- 2 to 5 reels a day
- Story sequences twice a week
- 1 to 2 carousels a month, only when something big happened

## YouTube

- The flow is ideation, then the title, then building the video (28 Sep).
- Ideation asks the creator questions: what have you solved, a common mistake in your niche, something you struggled with and fixed, what took longest to figure out, can it be broken down on a Miro board, is it mindset, does it break a limiting belief, is it wide or niche, what video do you wish someone had made.
- Formats: Miro board, face to camera, both together, a student call, a group coaching call, an in-person video with a student (28 Sep).
- Never script the body, it makes creators sound robotic. Script the hook and the CTA. Give the body as bullet points they can glance at and then talk naturally. Drop CTAs in mid-video, like "by the way, this is something we solve for our clients" (14 Jul, 21 Jul).
- Funnel stages changed. On 28 Sep you said there are no bottom-of-funnel videos (BOF meant the landing page or VSL). On 15 Oct you replaced that with Kobey's idea: every video serves all three stages. The first 20% speaks wide, the middle speaks to the dream follower, the end speaks to the buyer. The app has to use the words TOF, MOF and BOF and explain them to clients with examples (multiple choice in onboarding).
- On 23 Oct: a video's real stage comes from what its viewers do. If people who watch it book calls or pay, it's bottom of funnel, whatever the title says. The app guesses the stage and the creator taps OK or changes it. The clickable funnel on the Content page was built on 25 Oct.
- Titles use our formulas only. You rejected the app's generic formulas (13 Oct). Give 2 titles and 2 thumbnails per video (14 Oct).
- 7-Figure Packaging library: thumbnails, titles, reference videos and real outliers from 10x to 100x, measured against the channel's usual video the way vidIQ does it (14 Oct). Only from our niches: trading, ecom, TikTok Shop, Amazon FBA, high-ticket sales. Never unvetted channels (15 Oct).

## Thumbnails

- Real photos only. Never AI-generated people or backgrounds; that was switched off on 14 Oct.
- Light retouching only: brightness, contrast, cutout, blur or darken the background. Make the person stand out without changing their face (21 Jul).
- The goal is to look normal so viewers think "this guy is not a guru". No all caps, no wall of numbers (21 Jul).
- Extra elements are welcome: a Shopify or TradingView logo, a payout screenshot, them holding their Stripe dashboard (14 Oct).
- Heavy editing happens in Canva. Ask about the video first, then suggest styles (14 Oct).
- Your 40 duplicate board-explanation thumbnails were removed from the app (24 Sep).

## Reels

- Changed. On 26 Sep you said "we're not working on reels". On 15 Oct reels came back as cadence (2 to 5 a day), but we don't script them. Reels are where the creator is natural, raw and themselves.
- Anything in a creator's head can become a reel: a win, a new strategy, an old one shown a new way. Clips from long videos and sales calls work too (14 Jul).
- Later: study how Joel Kaplan posts 5 times a day (order, timing, which video comes first) before we write a reels playbook (28 Sep).

## Story sequences

- The design matters as much as the copy. The red hand-drawn underline is the signature look (14 Jul, 8 Oct).
- Write the copy first and iterate until it's right, then design, with suggestions for each choice: font, underlines, gradients, logo, charts, visuals (9 Oct).
- Everyday sequences stay simple: written copy on real photos. Save heavy design for big moments like an offer launch, a VSL or a webinar (9 Oct).
- Reuse a photo once or twice, then a blurred, darker copy with a drop shadow, so few photos are needed (8 Oct).
- Colours: red, black, white, and green for good news. No yellow or orange (8 Oct).
- Write it like a person pouring their heart out, never a narrator. Every slide has to earn the next tap, because people won't sit through 14 slides (8 Oct).
- The seven types (value, credibility, Q&A, day in the life, breakdown, lifestyle, CTA) are for us. Clients don't see a wall of type cards; Jane asks what they want to talk about (12 to 13 Oct).
- Highlight names are free: "start here", "new here?", "clients", "student wins" all work (12 Jul).
- Landing page or start-here sequence: test it per audience. If Instagram stories get better interaction, use them. Otherwise the landing page almost never fails (11 Jul).

## Carousels

- The lowest-leverage format. Don't lead content or outreach with them (26 Sep, 23 Oct).
- Built from the creator's real photos, for example 9 photos with 2 scenery shots, staged as intro, build-up, hook, CTA (27 Sep).
- Generated from those photos and the client's data by answering a few questions, then edited in Canva (12 to 14 Oct).

## Jane and the app

- The app's job is to ease the thinking behind making something: ask questions with examples, never a wall of options or invented category names (12 Oct).
- Jane is a chat like Claude or ChatGPT (2 Oct, 17 Oct). She has to use our frameworks and the client's own data (13 Oct). As of 27 Oct she reads their videos, leads, calls, plan, projects, stories and proof, and talks like a person.
- You review drafts before they're marked ready to post (24 Sep).
- Onboarding is the strongest asset in the app (15 Oct). It asks about their format, long vs short form, how long one video takes, their process, their skill with copy, titles, thumbnails and editing, and it sets expectations: results need them to post.
- The main thing we deliver is making content easy and tracking the people it brings in (15 Oct).
