## Key frameworks from Alexei

### The micro niche YouTube approach

Go one level deeper than the obvious niche. Not "dropshipping coach" — "dropshipping specifically for supplements." Not "I help coaches scale" — "I help coaches under $30K/month in make-money niches reach their first $100K/month."

This follows Rogers' diffusion model: take a small, clearly defined segment, become the early adopter, refine product-market fit, then scale.

**What going micro niche achieves:**
- YouTube's algorithm pushes videos to your exact ICP automatically
- No real competition in your specific lane
- You don't get compared to lifestyle-based competitors
- One micro niche owned compounds over time into $200K–$500K months
- $800–$1,200 generated per 100 views (vs. lifestyle content that generates views, not sales)

**Production requirements:** Miro board ($300–$400/month VA to build boards), laptop, OBS Studio or Loom, no villa, no ads.

### Detecting market outliers (Step 1)

Reverse-engineer structural performance variables from competitor channels:
1. **Macro concept structure** — what was the underlying promise? You cannot out-package a bad concept.
2. **Packaging** — how was the idea translated into a clickable title and thumbnail?
3. **Hook and framing** — does the first 30 seconds confirm the video delivers exactly what was promised?
4. **Authority positioning** — what signals made the audience believe the creator was worth listening to?

### Creating exceptional videos (Step 2)

Three components:
1. **Content angle** — take a saturated outlier from the market, bring your own new market angle, create a common concept. Familiar enough to attract views, fresh enough to stand out.
2. **Storytelling** — every video follows: Problem → Struggle → Discovery → Result
3. **Unique mechanism** — reference it in every single video. If you stop mentioning it, nobody will want it. When viewers encounter your CTA, they must already know what your mechanism is and how it works — their only remaining question is whether to implement it with your help.

### The content funnel structure (Step 3)

**TOF — Attract:**
- Give the "wow" not the "how"
- Break limiting beliefs
- Create an open loop pulling toward MOF
- Viewer needs: "this is for me" signal, a broken assumption, a fast win or insight

**MOF — Nurture:**
- Contradicting content, social proof, in-depth breakdowns
- Sales calls start getting booked here
- Viewer needs: personal relevance, emotional certainty, removal of fears

**BOF — Convert:**
- Every BOF video treated like a VSL
- Client interviews, behind-the-scenes, proof the solution works
- Intentional CTA placement

**Ideal customer journey:**
```
1 TOF video → 2-3 MOF videos → 1 BOF video → Landing page → Booked call
```

### The three CTA types (Step 3)

1. **Indirect CTA** — embedded inside storytelling and results. Never directly asks. Most powerful for trust-building.
2. **Soft CTA** — subtle suggestion of next step. Framed as optional resource.
3. **Hard CTA** — direct, explicit, at end of video. 1–2 minutes. Tells viewer exactly what to do.

All three appear intentionally in every video.

### Landing page design by audience size (Step 4)

| Audience size | Landing page design |
|---|---|
| Large audience | Longer VSL, longer headline, more qualifying questions, more social proof |
| Mid-size (~10K followers) | Shorter headline, shorter VSL, shorter page |
| Micro niche (extremely specific) | Just a headline and a Calendly link — pre-sold |
| B2B seller | Just a Calendly link |

All landing pages include qualifying questions regardless of size.

### Data tracking (Step 5)

Always use the same KPI system:
```
Set KPI → identify data source → analyze → identify flaws → iterate
```

Track only highest-leverage metrics: close rate, show rate, CTA performance within videos, calls booked per video, conversion rate per content type.

**Track where every call comes from** — which YouTube video, which reel, which specific piece of content. This separates record months from plateau months.

### The execution problem

> "People who watch this content and spend 6–12 months trying to implement it on their own face an execution problem, not a knowledge problem. The information is not the bottleneck."

This is Pulsify's single strongest argument for why the service exists. Marcus already knows what to do. He needs someone to build it with him.

### The broken content cycle (what most creators do)
```
See competitor → copy their format → post → pray → get no feedback
  → repeat for months with no direction
```

The core failure: no strategy, no feedback loop, no mechanism to diagnose what's broken.
