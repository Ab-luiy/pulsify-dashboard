# Ayman Arab (Aean)

Ayman Arab (referred to as "Aean" in the transcript) manages 40+ YouTube channels across info products and B2B services — dropshipping, tech, agency, cold email, and others. He started making YouTube videos at 14. His framework covers the five operational areas for building a $100K–$200K/month consulting YouTube channel.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Ayman's system maps closely onto the content architecture Pulsify builds for clients. His customer journey model (story → free course → how-to → case study → call) mirrors Pulsify's content → offer → funnel logic. His packaging principles (thumbnail + title as a unit, face on camera, 4 words max) are production-level rules Pulsify enforces. His tracking insights — Trackio for attribution, booked calls per video as the key metric — define how Pulsify measures client content performance.

## Key frameworks from Ayman

### The three content types
1. **Action-based videos (TOF, 50% of content)** — Evergreen "how to" videos around component steps of the service. Each video is a property that generates leads indefinitely. Think of it as collecting rent.
2. **Conversion-focused content (MOF/BOF)** — Client interviews (transformation in first 30 seconds), case study breakdowns (viewers self-identify by location, background, situation). Always include a CTA toward the VSL funnel or webinar.
3. **The free course video** — 60–75 min, screen recording, CTA within first 8 minutes, give away almost everything. One client went from $200K/month to $430K/month within one month of posting their free course.

### Customer journey
```
Story video (who is this guy?)
  → Free course (he actually knows what he's talking about)
  → Specific how-to video
  → Case study breakdown
  → Book call
```

When someone calls having watched 3–4 videos, it is the easiest close in the world.

### Idea generation
- Ignore most tools — only the outlier score (in VidIQ and similar) is worth using
- The YouTube search bar is the real validation tool — second-largest search engine in the world
- Don't pull ideas from direct competitors — pull from other biz-ops, then go further (gaming, math, entertainment). Let others do the testing; take the proven winners and apply your spin
- Tier-list format originated with celebrities and Fortnite, entered business content through Rob the Bank, then was adapted for growth operating → drove a flood of qualified calls
- "12 hours to make money" challenge format → 61,000 views

### Packaging rules
- Thumbnail: always include the creator's face; 4 words or fewer; don't repeat the title — complete it; use recognizable brand icons (Shopify, ChatGPT) to save text space
- Title: under 50–60 characters; front-load the important part
- Title and thumbnail must complete each other, not duplicate

### Scripting
- B2B hooks: short, get straight to the point
- B2C hooks: can be longer, more empathetic
- Hook must visually deliver what the title/thumbnail promised (show Stripe dashboard if you mentioned money, PayPal/Cash App for B2C, Stripe for B2B)
- Never end with "Alright guys, that's everything for this video" — use a natural transition to the next video or CTA
- Outro gives viewers two options: buy now (ready), or go to the next video (still building trust)

### Editing
- B2B: clean cuts, zoom-ins, no heavy animation. Viewer cares whether you're well-presented, not whether you have flashy edits.
- B2C: heavier hook editing, lighter body editing
- A poor editor can completely destroy a good video

### Post-production
- Tags and descriptions no longer matter significantly (changed in 2019)
- What matters: title, thumbnail, watch time, transcript
- CTA link at top of description; pin a comment with the CTA
- Post around 11am–12pm Eastern weekdays — but consistency matters more than timing
- Say target keywords naturally in the video — YouTube indexes the transcript
- Promote on every platform after posting (Instagram stories, email, Twitter) — external traffic actively helps

### Tracking
- Use Trakyo (trakyo.io; transcribed as "Trackio") for organic content attribution
- Key metrics: CTR on CTA link, booked calls per video, booked calls vs. clicks ratio
- Without tracking, you are flying blind — views are deceptive
- A 400-view video can outperform a 2,000-view video on calls booked

### Team structure by stage
- At $10K/month: do everything yourself except editing and thumbnail design
- At $100K–$500K/month: add a creative director (strategist) who manages editor and thumbnail designer, develops ideas, writes scripts

### Why channels plateau
- Primary: they stop posting
- Secondary: they become boring — top creators in each niche capture most of the audience, generic content loses

## Contradiction to flag

> ⚠️ Contradiction: Ayman argues "give almost everything in the free course — the more value you give, the more buyers you get." This directly contradicts Alexei's "give the wow not the how" principle from [[micro-niche-youtube]]. Resolution: these operate at different funnel positions. TOF content = wow, not how (Alexei's rule). Free course video = give everything (Ayman's rule). Both are correct in their funnel layer.
