# Jordan Platten

Jordan Platten is a core study source for the "founder is the bottleneck" stage of agency scaling. His Elite Agency page is useful because it sells systems, advisory access, operations, and a roadmap to $100K/month without making the promise feel like a beginner course.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Pulsify needs to recognize when Marcus is not stuck because he needs more information. He is stuck because his business depends on him to manually create, sell, follow up, deliver, and manage. Jordan's public assets frame the problem as a systems and leverage issue.

That is close to Pulsify's backend infrastructure pillar. When a coach or creator has demand but no machine, the founder becomes the constraint.

## Core mechanics

The Elite Agency public page targets agency owners already around $10K-$50K/month and sells a move toward $100K/month through a scaling system. The architecture is clear:

- Name the stage: stuck between $10K and $50K/month.
- Name the promise: install a $100K/month scaling system.
- Name the proof environment: many agencies scaled, operator advisors, case studies.
- Name the assets: one-to-one coaching, systems, masterclasses, Agency OS, SOPs, funnel templates, paid acquisition accelerator.
- Name the application process: apply, get a roadmap call, then join if it fits.

The page sells not just knowledge, but access to people and systems. That is a different promise than "watch these modules."

## How Pulsify uses this

Pulsify should borrow the bottleneck framing:

- The issue is not that the founder is lazy.
- The issue is that the business has no operating layer outside the founder.
- Content, offer, funnel, and backend must become assets instead of founder-dependent tasks.

This is useful for coaches who already have proof but cannot scale because every content idea, sales asset, nurture sequence, and delivery improvement still comes from them manually.

Jordan also helps with page architecture. His page makes the stage obvious quickly. Pulsify should do the same. A vague page that says "we help coaches scale" is weak. A sharper page says exactly which revenue stage, which bottleneck, and which infrastructure is installed.

The transcript-backed agency interview adds three useful patterns: communication is the agency survival skill, retention beats acquisition after the business has momentum, and expectation setting can increase trust in a market polluted by overpromising.

## What Pulsify should not copy

Do not overuse agency-specific operations language for coach/creator buyers. Coaches care about content, calls, clients, retention, and fulfillment. Agency owners care more explicitly about SOPs, delivery teams, and client management.

Do not turn Pulsify into an "advisory board" offer before Pulsify has the bench to support that claim.
