# Webinar framework

The webinar framework is the high-volume funnel architecture used when a client has the ad budget, audience, and proven funnel to justify moving beyond a VSL + call funnel and into a mass-market education format. It is step 16 in [[pulsify-order-of-operations]] — the last step, not the first.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

The webinar is the mechanism that scales a proven coaching offer from $50K/month to $200K+/month. It requires cold traffic to work at scale. Most operators who attempt webinars fail not because the webinar is bad, but because they run it to warm audiences, spend on ad infrastructure before the offer is proven, or treat it as a shortcut around an underdeveloped funnel.

For Pulsify, the webinar framework sets the ceiling for what a fully developed client engagement looks like. It informs how earlier steps are built — every decision in steps 1–15 is in service of eventually being webinar-ready.

## Core mechanics

### Live webinar vs. evergreen

Two modes:

**Live webinar:**
- Real-time event with a defined registration window
- Higher show rates (20–40% of registrants)
- Creates genuine urgency: offer closes when the webinar ends
- Requires operator availability on a fixed schedule
- Best for initial launches and audience segments that respond to live format

**Evergreen webinar:**
- Pre-recorded, runs on a schedule (e.g., top of every hour, twice per day)
- Lower show rates but continuous flow of leads
- Manufactured urgency (countdown timers, limited seat claims) — depends on niche tolerance for this
- Best for scaling after a live webinar has proven the concept and the script

The standard path: run live until the conversion rate is proven and the script is optimized, then move to evergreen for scale.

### Cold traffic is the key

This is the single most important principle in Jeremy's webinar framework, stated explicitly:

> Cold traffic is the key to scale. Warm audiences are limited by the size of the existing list or following. Cold traffic is unlimited.

Most operators run webinars to warm audiences (email lists, existing followers) and wonder why they cannot scale. The warm audience converts well because it already trusts the operator. Cold traffic does not have that trust — the webinar must earn it in 60–90 minutes.

Implication: a webinar that converts warm traffic at 15% does not necessarily convert cold traffic at 15%. Cold traffic performance requires separate testing, a different hook, and typically a longer or more proof-heavy content section.

### The cost per lead (CPL) target

Jeremy's CPL benchmarks for webinar registration from cold traffic:
- Target: $5–$12 CPL
- Acceptable: up to $25–$30 CPL for high-ticket niches with strong ROAS
- Red flag: above $40 CPL unless the offer price and close rate justify it

CPL at this level is only achievable with a properly structured registration page (not a full landing page — a simple headline, brief benefit bullets, and a registration form) and a compelling offer hook for the webinar itself.

### Show rate and the follow-up sequence

Webinar show rates vary significantly:
- Cold traffic, no follow-up: 15–25%
- Cold traffic, email/SMS follow-up sequence: 20–40%
- Warm audience: 35–60%

The follow-up sequence for webinars uses the same mechanics as [[hammer-them-strategy]] — pre-event content that frames the viewer for what they are about to see and why attending matters.

### The house money strategy

When a webinar generates enough revenue from the first batch of registrants to cover the ad spend, every subsequent registration from the same spend is "house money" — the campaign is net-positive regardless of what follows.

This is the target state for a scaling webinar campaign: structure the offer and close rate so that the first few closes from each batch cover ad costs, and all remaining closes are profit. A campaign operating on house money can be scaled aggressively because downside risk is contained.

### Webinar content structure

The webinar itself follows a defined structure:

1. **Introduction and credentialing** — establish authority, context, and relevance fast. Not a life story — a credentialing statement tied to the specific result being discussed.
2. **Promise and roadmap** — state exactly what the viewer will learn and why it is worth 60–90 minutes of their time
3. **Content block (the core)** — teach real, substantive content. The trust recession rule applies: if the viewer cannot evaluate the logic of the mechanism by the end of the content block, the webinar will not convert
4. **Transition to offer** — bridge from content to offer using a natural logic: "You now understand the framework. The question is implementation. Here is how we work together."
5. **Offer reveal** — price, components, guarantee (if applicable), bonuses (if applicable)
6. **Q&A and close** — handle objections live, reinforce the mechanism, push to action

### The bottleneck analysis

Jeremy's approach to diagnosing a webinar that is not converting:

| Symptom | Bottleneck |
|---|---|
| Low registration rate | Hook or registration page is weak |
| Low show rate | Follow-up sequence is missing or weak |
| High show rate, low engagement | Content is not delivering on the promise |
| High engagement, low offer conversion | Transition to offer is broken or price is wrong |
| High offer clicks, low closes | Closer is the bottleneck, or the close-to-cash process has friction |
| High closes, low ROAS | CPL is too high or offer price needs to increase |

Each bottleneck has a specific fix. Changing ad spend when the bottleneck is the close is a waste of budget.

### What the webinar is not

The webinar is not a lead generation shortcut. It is not what a new operator does while building their offer. It is not a replacement for a proven VSL. It does not work without cold traffic infrastructure and enough budget to sustain testing.

The most common failure mode: a coach reads that "webinars scale to millions" and attempts a webinar funnel before their offer has been validated on organic traffic, before their close rate is known, and before they have the budget to survive the burn phase. The result is a $3,000–$5,000 ad spend experiment that confirms nothing because the audience was too small to draw conclusions from.

## How Pulsify uses this

The webinar framework shapes how Pulsify builds everything from step 1 onward:

- The unique mechanism (step 2) is built with the eventual webinar script in mind — it needs to be teachable in 30–40 minutes of content
- The offer (step 3) is built at a price point that makes webinar math viable — typically $3,000+
- The email sequences (step 11) are proto-webinar follow-up sequences — the same mechanics, just shorter window
- The content pillars (step 7) produce the evergreen proof stack that feeds webinar ad creative

When a client hits the criteria for webinar activation (proven funnel, proven close rate, ad budget $10K+/month), Pulsify builds the registration page, the email sequence, the offer reveal, and the ad creative. The live run comes first, then evergreen.
