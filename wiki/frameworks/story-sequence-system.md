# The story sequence system

The canonical Pulsify framework for Instagram story sequences: what they are, the five types, the universal slide structure, the daily 9-slide frame, the permanent assets (start-here, story launch), cadence, delegation, and measurement. Source is the Niksetting corpus (single-source, per [[synthesis/cross-creator-synthesis]]), cross-validated where SooWei's material touches it, with Pulsify deltas applied throughout. This page is the spec for the story-sequence generator.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Stories are the highest-trust surface on Instagram and the closing surface of the profile funnel: reels make strangers problem-aware, YouTube converts deep trust, stories close daily. The operating math: every watched story adds roughly 1% to conversion likelihood; a hundred watched stories makes a customer on average (range observed: 3 to 100). The people who buy are the silent daily-sequence watchers, not the reel engagers. Pulsify needs this system twice over: for its own profile and as a client deliverable inside the Content Engine.

## What a story sequence is

A designed multi-slide daily story arc where every slide earns the next one. The core mechanic: "every next story adds value to the prior story," holding the viewer in a flow state until the sequence's job is done. Sequences are built for credibility and conversion, never for views; a deep sequence deliberately sheds casual viewers to concentrate buyers. Stories are also the safe surface: feed content can damage a brand, stories tolerate experiments.

## The five sequence types

1. **Q&A.** Open a question box; answer 12 hours later, picking the questions your ICP cares about. Balance answers 50/50 technical (delegable) and personal (founder-only). Cold-start rule: not enough questions, write your own — you know the pain points. The psychology: questions are the audience inviting you to perform your expertise; they want to see how you think, and every answer is a free consult. Run at least biweekly.
2. **Educational.** One ICP topic walked across ~6 slides, belief-shift framed (old way / new way). Lead with what they walk away with, never with what they'll learn: the outcome, not the process.
3. **Credibility (result stacking).** Open with a result, stack wins across the board, close with a positioning statement that doubles as a belief shift. Materials: screenshots, client messages, revenue dashboards. Sequence order is engineered association: an education slide first makes every result that follows feel like proof of that exact claim. One win is luck, three is a pattern, ten is undeniable; never stop stacking. **Pulsify delta:** proof currency is student results, transformation receipts, funded-account passes. Never watches, cars, or lifestyle (see [[concepts/trust-recession]]; SooWei's prediction that lifestyle marketing dies is the confirming source).
4. **Value-driven.** A story that contains something genuinely useful: set a scene, build tension, walk through a framework or lesson. Goal: show your level of thinking and let them feel they got something for free. Visual quality is mandatory here; a value sequence with bad photos doesn't read as valuable.
5. **Lead magnet.** CTA on slide one, ends in the email list, which takes the relationship off the algorithm. Hook carries outcome, specificity, speed ("the exact 12 hooks I used to make $4M in client revenue, yours in 30 seconds"), never "here's a PDF about content."

Two auxiliary modes round out the week: **founder day-in-the-life** (timestamped documentation carrying value, education, and proof in its slots; the deepest follow hook because people want to replicate the founder's day) and the **no-idea day** (5-10 evergreen niche topics, deliberately structureless; the lack of structure itself reads as personal and builds trust). **Lifestyle frames** live inside the 80/20 rule: 80% expert, 20% human.

## The universal six-step slide structure

Every sequence, regardless of type, walks these steps:

1. **Curiosity** — the pattern-interrupt open.
2. **Obstacle** — name what blocks them; call out the pain, not the situation ("are you making 50 grand a month" is a situation; the reason they hate being there is the pain).
3. **Statement + proof** — the claim, backed immediately.
4. **Belief shift** — the frame transition; one reframe driven so deep they can't unhear it.
5. **Result** — show the outcome and the trust it earns.
6. **Close from abundance** — the ask, made like someone who doesn't need it.

After a few weeks this stops being a checklist and becomes instinct.

## The daily 9-slide frame

The default conversion sequence shape (from the Amer case, [[synthesis/niksetting-extractions/06-200k-months-amer-case-study]]):

- **Slides 1-3:** pattern interrupt, then proof/value establishing the thread.
- **Slides 4-6:** the pain mirror. Reflect their situation back until they feel it, then show the gap between their pain and the solution, so crossing it requires access to you.
- **Slides 7-9:** access, offer, CTA. 
- Why it works: slides 1-6 are pure value, no pitch. By slide 7 they already feel the problem, so the offer lands as help, not selling.

**CTA placement is a dial, not a rule:** volume goal (lead magnets) puts the CTA on slide 1 before drop-off eats reach; qualification goal puts it after slide 7, because whoever clicks there genuinely cares. Ask which you want before building.

## Permanent assets

- **Start-here sequence.** The first highlight on the profile, permanent, tapped first by every new visitor: the silent sales pitch. Structure (10 slides): your story (3) -> client proof (3) -> the framework (2) -> the close (2). Zero selling, purely the journey; each slide pairs a pain point with your insight from a belief perspective. Your story plus their pain points filters wrong-fit visitors automatically. **Pulsify note (founder-ratified):** start-here vs landing page is a per-audience test, not doctrine. Run both, measure where the audience interacts; if IG story interaction wins, the start-here carries conversion, otherwise the landing page does (the founder's prior: a landing page almost never fails). Cold outreach keeps pages regardless. Dial 5 in [[synthesis/cross-creator-synthesis]].
- **Story launch.** A short webinar run in stories to sell something; ~9 slides. His numbers: $600K total from launches, $250K from one sequence at 71.9% completion. Launches are built from the account's own accumulated data, never copied — the structure transfers, the copy never does.
- **Highlights.** Every well-performing sequence gets saved into its bucket ([[playbooks/instagram-growth-workstream]] buckets stand: My story, Start here, Results, Mechanism, Apply).

## Cadence and delegation

- Floor: 3 sequences per week. Target: 5-7 (near-daily). Practitioner level: daily. Slides per day: 3-7.
- Weekly type mix to rotate: credibility, educational, value, Q&A (biweekly minimum), lead magnet, plus day-in-the-life or no-idea days as filler that still fits the system.
- Delegation split: ~3 personal days a week the founder shoots from the phone; the team runs structured storylines the other days from a process they can follow. Full automation fails — the personal touch is the product and nobody can read the founder's mind. Technical/systematized content is delegable; personal is not.
- Batch production kills the growth signal: audiences buy from people they watch grow, and week-old batches can't carry this week's growth.

## Measurement

- **Drop-off rate** is the only per-sequence metric that matters: the view gap between first and last slide. Leave at slide 2 = hook failed. Leave at slide 7 = body went sideways. Improve one percentage point at a time.
- Track per sequence: views, completion %, DMs and interactions, profile visits, compared performance vs prior sequences. Log copy so winners are reusable. A VA can run the tracking; Claude can run the analysis.
- Account-level dashboard: drop-off rate, opt-in rate, and (per source, undefined) save rate. Supplement with SooWei's weekly set: net follower growth, ICP leads gained, follower-to-view ratio, dollar-per-follower. Week-over-week comparisons only.
- The 1%-per-story rule is a mnemonic, not a measurement: consistency makes the math inevitable, absence decays it ("when you don't post story sequences, you decrease the conversion potential").

## How Pulsify uses this

1. **Own profile first.** Run the weekly mix on Pulsify's IG; the tracking log doubles as the validation data for the generator.
2. **Client deliverable.** The system slots into the Content Engine component: per-client sequence calendar, type mix, delegation split, and the drop-off report in the monthly recap (mirrors the dashboard-product vision).
3. **The generator.** This page is the spec: inputs are creator profile + sequence type + topic/goal; output is slide-by-slide copy (overlay text, visual direction, which of the six steps each slide executes) with CTA placement per goal and two hook variants. Voice rules apply; the humanizer pass runs on every output.
