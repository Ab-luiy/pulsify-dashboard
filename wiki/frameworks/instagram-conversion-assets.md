# The five conversion assets

The canonical Pulsify framework for the conversion half of a personal brand: the closed set of assets that turn followers into customers, and the math that governs them. Traffic content and conversion content are different jobs; this page covers the second job only. Primary source is the Niksetting corpus, with SooWei corroboration and the founder-ratified dials applied.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Every dollar a creator collects flows through one of five assets, and most creators run one or two. The operators making real money run all five on a schedule for months — "one story sequence isn't a system. One lead magnet isn't a system. The system is what happens when all five run." For Pulsify this is both the diagnostic (which assets is the client missing?) and the delivery map (what the Content Engine installs). It also explains the classic client complaint — views but no buyers — in one sentence: they built the traffic job and never built the conversion job.

## The governing math

- A 10,000-follower audience decomposes to roughly 3,000 who know you, 800 who know your name, 100 who trust you, 18 who will pay. The last gap is where money is lost daily.
- Ceiling: no more than ~5% of an audience will ever buy; 1% is already good. The other 95% generate the likes and comments — which is why engagement feedback is worthless and only buyer feedback counts (law 3 in [[synthesis/cross-creator-synthesis]]).
- Buying windows expire: a lead warm today is gone in days. The assets exist so that whenever any follower's window opens, something is there to close it. Target conversion cycle once the system runs: 24 hours to 7 days from interest to decision.
- Each watched story adds ~1% to conversion likelihood; consistency makes the math inevitable.

## The five assets

### 1. Story sequences

The workhorse — "the one that does most of the work." Full system at [[frameworks/story-sequence-system]]. Job: daily trust accumulation and closing. The four functions: pre-call heavy lifting, automatic qualification, fastest trust-building of any format, and better client economics ("a pre-sold client pays faster, argues less, and stays for longer").

### 2. Personalized lead magnets

Generic PDFs fail because nobody cares about generic. The working pattern is a router: one big-promise lead magnet ("get a free personalized brand game plan"), a short intake (revenue, desired situation, niche/problem — expandable to a six-problem dropdown crossed with revenue), and stage-specific delivery that hits the prospect's exact pain. Sell what they want, deliver what they need.

Rules:
- **Divide by three:** whatever revenue number the copy promises attracts people earning about a third of it. Calibrate the aspirational number at ~3x the client you want.
- The magnet ends in the email list — the owned layer that survives any platform ban. Treat audience data like an investment portfolio: spread across email, phone, DM.
- Hook formula: outcome + specificity + speed.
- **Founder-ratified value doctrine (dial 6):** give everything away. The offer sells implementation, accountability, and access — never withheld information. SooWei's own numbers back this: his free course attributed $150K in a month.
- SooWei's variant worth testing later: the 30-day outcome-named challenge ("2x your business in 30 days") with weekly action items and a human accountability call — a book-a-call funnel wearing a freebie's clothes.

### 3. CTA assets (the second channel)

A second YouTube channel (and its IG twin, the proof page) built for conversion, where views genuinely don't matter. Two jobs:
- **Niche depth:** weekly pain-point-specific videos the broad main channel can't carry anymore ("how to move from 1-on-1 to group" beats "how to make $10M" for the person with that exact pain).
- **Brand-safe experiments:** try any format without diluting the main brand.
The main channel stays broad and grows the brand; the second channel converts. Team-run, founder-light. Corroborated: both creators run exactly this split (Mr. Nick Setting / Grow Acquisition page; SooWei's salesy consulting page with 30+ interviews).

**CTA doctrine (founder-ratified, dial 3):** engineered CTAs are primary — soft CTA to the lead magnet, hard CTA to a DM trigger word that routes to a setter and a booked call. Trigger words are named uniquely ("elevate"), never generically ("learn"). Content persuasive enough to act as its own CTA is a bonus on trust content, never a license to omit the ask. CTA density signals positioning: premium brands ask less often, volume brands ask more.

### 4. Bottom-of-funnel content (proof)

Niched content about clients, experiences, and transformations. People pay when they can imagine themselves in the screenshot. Structures:
- **The 4-part screenshot proof:** hook (the result) -> context (where they started) -> mechanism (what changed while working with you) -> invitation (who this is for — named by pain points, never by label, so the right people self-select).
- **The proof pin post:** a pinned transformation reel, before -> diagnose -> process -> result. Does both jobs at once (views and buyers).
- **The consumption ladder:** package every client win at multiple depths — 60-second talking-head testimonial, carousel breakdown, 30-minute interview, 40-minute value-front-facing timeline breakdown — so every sophistication level (and schedule) can consume proof. Value-front-facing is the rule: raw client interviews tank because only near-buyers watch them; wrap the proof in teaching and everyone watches.
- **Client documentaries** (SooWei's prediction of the future of testimonials): full arcs including the struggles, capped with the interview.
- Testimonial economics: with few results, position each with maximum intention; at volume, quantity itself becomes the proof and 95% never press play.

**Pulsify proof currency (standing delta):** student results, revenue receipts, transformation stories. Never lifestyle purchases — both the trust-recession thesis and SooWei's lifestyle-marketing-dies prediction converge here.

### 5. Email sequences

"The email sequence closes what the story sequence opens." The spec: 8-9 emails over 14 days, each reframing the same core idea.
- Email 1: deliver the magnet, real introduction, human sender profile.
- Email 2: the story behind the magnet.
- Emails 3-5: client wins, one per email.
- Email 6: value/education, the same material as the stories, reframed.
- Email 7: CTA to the DMs.
- Tone rule: everyone's email is corporate, so personalized wins by default. No heavy CTA until trust exists (a 25%-open weekly trust-first newsletter got explicit approval in the source).
- Traffic direction: emails route to YouTube long form, which routes to the call — the funnel is a web, not a line. A lead that doesn't close cycles back through email -> YouTube -> call rather than exiting.
- Owned-channel upgrade path: phone/WhatsApp (90% open rates) once volume justifies it.

## Matching the CTA to the viewer's state

The friction principle: meet the viewer where their state is. A two-hour masterclass viewer has questions — route to DMs, not a 17-question Calendly. A saveable deliverable warrants a framework CTA ("comment X"); a too-deep-for-reels topic warrants a long-form CTA. Small friction mistakes silently cost five figures.

## How Pulsify uses this

1. **Client diagnostic:** score every client (and prospect) on which of the five assets exist and run consistently. The gap list is the Content Engine work order — and doubles as outreach material ("you're running one of the five").
2. **The asset build order for a new client:** story sequences first (daily trust), lead magnet second (capture), email third (own the list), second surface and BOF proof as results accumulate.
3. **Measurement:** per-asset single metrics — drop-off (sequences), opt-in rate (magnets), attributed booked calls (BOF and email; instrument per dial 7 on our own tracking infrastructure).
