# Flywheel VSL

A flywheel VSL is one long YouTube video, 1 to 6+ hours, that teaches a creator's entire business model from start to finish and works as the funnel's conversion step. Every other video on the channel points to it, and its description points to the application. The term and the method come from Dustin Varano; this page records his version and marks where Pulsify would take it differently. It is a candidate format, not yet used with a Pulsify client.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

It is inbound YouTube taken to its end point: instead of a prospect watching 3 to 5 videos before booking (our usual journey in [[concepts/content-funnel-tof-mof-bof]]), one video does the whole pre-sell. Dustin claims it can replace the landing page, the VSL and much of the setter's job, and that prospects then arrive on calls with few objections. The Growth work's first job is creating a full YouTube video end to end ([[synthesis/growth-section-plan-2026-09-25]]), and a flywheel is one concrete shape that video could take. It also fits our Miro-board format, since his method builds the whole video on a Miro board.

## Core mechanics

**Flow.** YouTube views -> flywheel -> application -> qualified -> booked -> showed -> closed -> cash.

**Linking.** Every supporting video's description (and end screen) links to the flywheel, never to Instagram or another platform. The flywheel's own description has the application or landing page link on line 1, then full timestamps, and no outside social links. Dustin's reasoning is that YouTube rewards keeping viewers on the platform; that is his theory, not something he shows data for.

**Structure.**
- Hook and proof, a few minutes: say it is the most complete video on the topic, scroll the whole Miro board so the scale is visible, show real result numbers, say exactly who it is for, show a left-to-right map of what's covered.
- The business model, section by section, in the order a beginner would actually do it, mirroring the board.
- Proof placed inside the section it proves. A testimonial or client interview goes right after the lesson that produced that result, so it gets watched as part of the lesson and never feels like an ad. Screenshot stacks work the same way.
- Light program mentions throughout (for example, show a working but slightly dated script and say the current version is inside the program), one hard CTA at the end.

**Build order.** Written outline (columns per section, bullets and sub-bullets, no paragraphs, detailed enough that a designer with no context could build the board) -> Miro board (same order, mostly real screenshots and simple diagrams, one-line notes to film from) -> film segment by segment -> post -> add the link to every past and future video.

**Length.** Dustin's floor is 1 hour, target 1-2+ hours, with up to half made of previously recorded material (old videos, interviews, full coaching calls) and at least half new.

**Packaging.** A specific number plus the length in the title or thumbnail, casual lowercase title, a thumbnail that signals depth (a dense board, a dashboard, a real result), A/B test variations.

**Upkeep.** Remake it when the model changes or the proof goes stale (about a year). Track its views and lead attribution separately from the channel.

## How Pulsify uses this

Theoretical for now. If a client builds one, the Pulsify version:
- Opens with results proof, never lifestyle. > ⚠️ Contradiction: Dustin's flywheel page says to "flex lifestyle briefly" in the hook. Resolution: dropped. Pulsify's positioning is anti-lifestyle, and his own Miro page says results proof pulls the better buyer.
- Uses student results as the proof currency, per [[frameworks/proof-system]].
- Keeps per-video cash tracking alive: supporting videos carry the tracked landing-page link as well as the flywheel link, so [[synthesis/cash-by-video-spec]] can still rank them. Pending founder decision.
