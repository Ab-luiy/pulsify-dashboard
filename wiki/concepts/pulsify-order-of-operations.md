# Pulsify order of operations

The Pulsify order of operations is the 16-step sequence that governs how every new client engagement is built — from the initial profile cleanup through to paid ad scale. It enforces the content → offer → funnel dependency: nothing downstream gets built until everything upstream is in place.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Skipping steps is the most common reason agencies fail clients. A VSL built before the unique mechanism exists has no clear message. A landing page built before the offer is validated sends unqualified leads to a broken close. Paid ads bought before the funnel converts waste budget and damage client trust.

The order of operations is Pulsify's forcing function. It defines what can and cannot be done at each phase of an engagement and gives both Pulsify and the client a shared frame for what "progress" means. When a client pushes to run ads before step 12, Pulsify can point to the order of operations rather than argue about strategy.

## Core mechanics

### The 16 steps

**Phase 1 — Foundation (steps 1–4)**

**Step 1: Profile cleanup**
Rewrite the bio to speak to the ICP, replace the profile picture with a clean face-forward image, build out the story highlights with existing testimonials and results. This step costs almost nothing and produces the first visible signal that a client is operating with intent.

**Step 2: Unique mechanism creation**
Define, name, and document the client's proprietary methodology. This is not optional and is not cosmetic positioning — it is the core message that every subsequent piece of content, every video, and every sales page will reference. See [[unique-mechanism]].

**Step 3: Offer creation**
Build the high-ticket or mid-ticket offer around the unique mechanism. The offer must answer: what exactly does the client deliver, to whom, in what timeframe, for what price, with what guarantee of access to the operator. The [[marcus-avatar]] page defines the financial qualification criteria for deciding between high-ticket and mid-ticket entry points.

**Step 4: Content audit**
Audit the client's existing content library against the three content pillars and the TOF/MOF/BOF structure. Identify what exists, what is salvageable, what needs to be produced. The audit determines the gap between where the content library is and where it needs to be to generate booked calls. See [[content-funnel-tof-mof-bof]].

**Phase 2 — Conversion infrastructure (steps 5–8)**

**Step 5: Profile Funnel build**
Build the full Instagram Profile Funnel: bio CTA with trigger word, ManyChat automation, qualification sequence, setter handoff. See [[profile-funnel]]. This is the default conversion mechanism for Marcus-stage clients and becomes active immediately — it starts collecting leads from existing content before any new content is produced.

**Step 6: Weekly content system launch**
Launch the weekly content production rhythm: 1 reel per day, 1–2 YouTube videos per week, daily stories. Content topics are pulled from the content pillars. Every piece maps to a funnel layer. The system is designed to be repeatable, not heroic — the same output every week regardless of inspiration or creative energy.

**Step 7: Content pillars documentation**
Document the 3–5 content pillars specific to this client's niche and unique mechanism. Every video, reel, and story topic for the next 6 months derives from these pillars. Undocumented pillars produce inconsistent content. Documented pillars produce a searchable, compound library. See [[content-pillars]].

**Step 8: First content batch production**
Produce the first batch of content: 3–5 TOF YouTube videos (searchable how-to format), 10–15 reels, 7 days of story sequences. This batch gives the Profile Funnel something to send traffic to and gives the YouTube channel its initial library.

**Phase 3 — Funnel refinement (steps 9–12)**

**Step 9: VSL production (when warranted)**
For clients with meaningful existing audiences or enough early call data, produce a VSL following the 6-step structure. See [[vsl-framework]]. The VSL is the upgrade from the Profile Funnel — it automates more of the qualification work and allows paid traffic. Do not produce a VSL before the unique mechanism and offer are locked and before enough sales call data exists to write the script.

**Step 10: Landing page build**
Build the landing page connected to the VSL. Page length and complexity calibrated to audience size per the Alexei scale: micro niche = headline + Calendly; larger audiences = full proof stack. Always includes qualifying questions (Typeform) before calendar booking.

**Step 11: Email sequence build**
Build the pre-call email sequence: 6 emails per day for 48–72 hours, sourced from the four hammer them content quadrants (common questions, common objections, expectations, follow-up questions). The emails are framing, not reminders. See [[hammer-them-strategy]].

**Step 12: Confirmation page build**
Build the post-booking confirmation page with: framing video, 4–12 breakout videos from the four quadrants, original-source testimonials. The confirmation page is a research hub, not a receipt. See [[hammer-them-strategy]].

**Phase 4 — Scale (steps 13–16)**

**Step 13: Outreach system**
Stand up or optimize the outbound DM system: 50–100 DMs per day sourcing from TikTok, Kajabi, YouTube, Twitter, Whop, and School. Response → 5–10 DMs + Loom → Day 1/3/5/7 follow-up sequence. Outreach generates immediate revenue and runs as a bridge while the content ecosystem compounds — but by step 13, the inbound content system (steps 6–8) should be the primary lead source. Outreach supplements, it does not replace. See [[lead-sourcing-outreach]].

**Step 14: Setter hiring and training**
When inbound and outbound volume exceeds what the operator can handle, hire a setter. Train on the qualification sequence, the DM approach, the Loom script, and the handoff trigger. See [[lead-sourcing-outreach]].

**Step 15: Paid ads activation**
Begin paid ads only when the organic funnel converts and the operator has budget. Default: ads point to the Profile Funnel, not directly to the landing page. CTA: "follow me to learn everything I know for free." Goal: build a qualified follower base that converts through content over 30–60 days, not a direct-response conversion attempt on cold traffic. Scale to landing page once the funnel is proven.

**Step 16: Webinar or high-volume scale**
For clients with budget, audience size, and a proven close rate: build the webinar funnel. Live webinar to evergreen, $5–$12 CPL target, 20–40% show rate. The webinar is step 16, not step 2, because it requires the unique mechanism, the offer, the VSL, the email sequences, and a baseline close rate to be known quantities before committing webinar ad budget. See [[webinar-framework]].

### What the sequence prevents

The order of operations is a dependency map. Each step fails without the prior step:

- A VSL without a unique mechanism has no differentiating message
- Paid ads without a converting funnel burn budget with no return
- A setter without an offer and a Profile Funnel has nothing to route leads into
- A webinar without a baseline close rate from organic traffic risks destroying ad budget at scale

### When to deviate

The order of operations is a default, not a rigid rule. Legitimate reasons to adjust sequencing:

- Client already has a working VSL and landing page → skip to step 11 and audit rather than rebuild
- Client has no existing audience but has budget → activate outreach (step 13) earlier, parallel to content build
- Client has a large existing audience → the VSL (step 9) and landing page (step 10) become urgent earlier; Profile Funnel is a short-term stopgap, not the long-term architecture
- Client has call data → jump immediately into hammer them content (steps 11–12) before finishing content pillars, because show rate is the bottleneck

The test: is the downstream step able to produce its intended output without the upstream step being complete? If yes, proceed. If no, do not skip.

## How Pulsify uses this

The order of operations is the onboarding anchor. In the first client call, Pulsify shows this sequence and identifies where the client currently sits. Every action item in the engagement maps to a step number. When a client requests something out of sequence, Pulsify references the step number and explains what must be true before that step is unlocked.

The sequence also manages client expectations: a client who wants to "start running ads" but is currently on step 3 has a visible 12-step gap between where they are and what they want. The map makes the conversation about execution order, not about disagreement.
