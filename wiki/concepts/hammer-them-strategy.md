# Hammer them strategy

The hammer them strategy is the system of contextual content injected at key funnel stages — especially between booking and the call — to move leads up interest levels before they speak with a salesperson. Done fully, it produces 60–70%+ show rates and turns salespeople into cashiers.

## Why this matters for Pulsify

Low show rates and unframed leads are the two biggest problems in any call funnel. Most operators do nothing between the booking and the call. They send one calendar confirmation and pray the lead shows up. Hammer them is the mechanism that bridges that gap. It is the single highest-leverage activity between funnel build and cash collected.

For Pulsify clients operating on a Profile Funnel, a modified version applies — content sequences and story sequences serve the same function in a longer, lower-urgency timeline. For clients with VSL funnels and call schedules, the full hammer them strategy is non-negotiable.

## Core mechanics

### The standard funnel without hammer them

```
Ad → VSL → Application → Booking → Call → Close
```

Result: 40–50% show rates, unframed leads, defensive sales calls.

### The funnel with hammer them

```
Ad → VSL → Application → Booking
  → [15–20 retargeting pieces hitting the lead]
  → [6 emails/day for 48–72 hours]
  → Call (lead arrives informed, framed, further along in buying process)
  → Close
```

Result: 60–70%+ show rates, leads arrive with specific questions rather than objections, closes feel like order-taking.

### The four content quadrants

All hammer them content is built from four categories derived from actual sales call data:

1. **Common questions** — what do leads ask at the start of every sales call?
2. **Common objections** — what pushback do they give when the offer is presented?
3. **Expectations** — what do leads need to understand about how to succeed with the offer?
4. **Follow-up questions** — what do they ask after the first set of questions gets answered?

Every piece of content maps to one of these four quadrants. Content that doesn't come from actual sales call data is guessing — and guessing produces no measurable lift.

### Email sequence specifics

- **Volume:** 6 emails per day
- **Window:** 48–72 hours pre-call (this means intentionally spacing calls 2–3 days out from booking, not same-day)
- **48-hour window:** 12 total emails
- **72-hour window:** 18 total emails
- **Open rates when content is relevant:** 50–80%+ per email
- **Content:** NOT reminders. Framing, education, objection handling, FAQ documents, breakout video links, research SOPs, case studies — the same material on the confirmation page, repurposed into email format

### Confirmation page specifics

The confirmation page is a research hub, not just an "your call is booked" screen. Structure:

1. Confirmation headline
2. Main confirmation video (short — welcome, set framing: "Our goal is for you to be fully in or out before the call")
3. Breakout videos: 4–12 videos, 30 seconds to 5 minutes each — pull from the four quadrants
4. Testimonials (below breakout videos) — must be from original source (video, social screenshot, email), never retyped

### Retargeting content volume

- Full implementation: 30–50 short-form pieces + 20–30 long-form pieces
- Minimum to see meaningful show rate lift: 15–20 pieces minimum
- Half-assing this (only short-form, or too few pieces, or content that doesn't match what buyers actually ask) produces zero measurable result

> Jeremy's exact words: "Half-assing this does not produce results — you need the full volume. Done fully = 60–70%+ show rates. Done halfass = no meaningful result."

### Three funnel stages where hammer them applies

1. **Pre-ad:** Cost-effective content to build warm retargeting audience (fractions of a penny per view). CPMs drop when there's prior page interaction.
2. **Between ad and funnel:** Content for people who saw the ad but didn't go to the funnel — sell them harder on why they need to go.
3. **Post-booking (between booking and call):** The highest-leverage window. The four quadrant content. This is the stage most people call "hammer them."

Jeremy calls doing all three simultaneously "the tornado."

### Interest levels framework

Leads arrive at different interest levels:
- **Curious** — just browsing
- **General interest** — has awareness of the problem
- **Moderate interest** — researching solutions
- **High interest** — ready to buy if the right operator shows up

Deals close at high interest. Most leads arrive at curious or general interest. The combined job of marketing + hammer them content is to move them up incrementally. The salesperson is the last step, not the only step.

### Pre-call selfie video (salespeople)

A salesperson texting the lead from a real iPhone — not automated, not green text, not software — with a selfie video referencing their application and offering to send a relevant resource is the single highest-impact action for show rate. When reps stop doing this, show rates drop within 3 weeks.

## How Pulsify uses this

The hammer them strategy is a content production problem. At scale ($50K+/month clients), Pulsify needs to produce 30–50 short-form pieces + 20–30 long-form pieces built specifically from that client's sales call data. This cannot be templated generically — it requires real objection data from real calls. This is why Pulsify needs closers or the client owner taking calls themselves early in the engagement.

At early stages (Marcus-stage clients), a lighter version applies: story sequences on Instagram serve a similar pre-warming function over a longer timeline (30–60 day trust-building cycle vs. 48–72 hour pre-call window).
